## Part 1. Story

### S1. The central question is answered in the first ten minutes

The act asks "whose fault is the dark?" By 22:45 the player holds the two answers that matter most:

- `quay.claim.read`: the narration states the deduction outright: "Nobody files a claim three hours
  early by mistake. Somebody in Gullet knew the hour". Keel's foreknowledge (bible layer 4) is
  handed over on the gangway.
- `quay.pen.berth`: Penhallow, in her second line, disposes of the claim's own theory: "Wreckers
  show a light to bring you in. That light sent me out."

From then on there is no live false theory. The night becomes a matter of collecting confirmations.
The bible promises a thriller "in who knew, when, and who will pay for saying so". The "who knew"
half is spent at the berth.

Ask: let the claim stay strange without the narrator solving it. Keep the timestamp (it is the hook)
and let the player do the subtraction. Move Penhallow's "wrong way round" line to the Brine, later,
after Keel has had a chance to make wreckers sound true (his witnesses, Teague's "I'd see her blind",
the sack). The wreckers theory should be believable until about 23:30 for a player who has not been
to the floor.

Director: Accepted, 2026-10-17. The narration no longer does the subtraction: the hook stays (the timestamp), the player subtracts. SMALL PRINT's "The Board has insured an appointment" survives as a passive at 7, so only a reader built for it hears it. Penhallow's "That light sent me out" moves to the Brine and plays only after 23:30, and Keel's witnesses, Teague's "I'd see her blind" and the sack are placed so wreckers stays believable until then. Assigned: quay and brine writers. **Status, 2026-10-17: Partly done (editor pass 2): two lines still dispose of wreckers at 22:45; see S1a.**

### S2. Nearly everyone confesses, and nobody misleads

On one night, to a stranger, seven of the eight principals give up their secret in full: Keel
(`mole.sq.motive.ok`, `quay.keel.lens.ok`), Marrow (`weigh.marrow.admit`, `gut.smoke.marrow.look`),
Wenna (`brine.wenna.coat.ok`, `brine.wenna.glass`), Sabine (`brine.oven.admit`), Fennick
(`quay.fennick.dark.true`), Spratt (`gut.spratt.claim.ok`), Merrow (`floor.merrow.where.true`). Crake
admits the yellow ink too (`quay.crake.yellow.ok`). Several of these cost the player nothing:
Fennick's full confession needs only darkness, and Wenna's glass confession is one menu option.

The result is an act with no resistance. Every principal wants to unburden. Act 2 asks "who pays",
and by then the only secret left in Gullet is the Shelf.

Ask: pick two principals who hold out on Night 1 and break on Night 2. My candidates are Fennick
(he says the relics came "by grace" and keeps saying it; the purchase is proven by numbers and the
pie dish, not by his mouth) and Crake (the director already proposes this in REWORK-PLAN 1.3 item 7).
Give one principal a lie that holds up under a casual check and misleads a careless player. Sabine's
"left at nine" is close; it collapses too fast because Candlemas points at the clock in the same node
(`brine.sabine.alibi`: "Look at the clock. Not at her.").

Director: Accepted. Fennick and Crake hold out on Night 1 and break on Night 2: Fennick keeps saying "by grace" and the purchase is proven only by the numbers and the pie dish; Crake tells her lie well and the yellow ink catches her in Act 2 (her line "I struck a sentence. I did not strike a lamp." moves there). Sabine's "left at nine" now survives a casual check: Candlemas no longer points at the clock in the same node; breaking it needs the slate and the clock together. Wenna's glass confession is gated behind a cost. Assigned: quay, brine; dawn already does not need fennick.bought.

thing, then a voice translates it. Articulate agrees from the other side: voice and instrument lines
carry about three times the flagged-pattern density of the narration (see Part 2).

The voices should argue, tempt and notice. They should not annotate. A useful test: if the voice line
were deleted, would the player miss information? If not, it is commentary.

Director: Accepted. Voices argue, tempt and notice; they do not annotate. The test (would the player miss information if the line went?) is in every writer's addendum, with the "He is not X.
