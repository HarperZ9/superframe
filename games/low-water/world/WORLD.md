# Low Water: the island

The world bible for Low Water as one continuous place. The island is generated (`world/README.md`), so every number here is read from seed `20261118` and changes with the seed. The names, the people and what a player finds are written by hand and hold for any seed.

Author, 2026-11-18: "I think I would like for Low Water to become like a, I don't know, like a larger world, large scale, 3D, like a full world."

The existing direction stays: the oil and ink base, the sodium light law, the grotesque cast, real combat with bosses. Gullet, the tidal harbour town of the first story, is the seat of the Storm Coast. The art direction this bible follows is `ARCHIVE-ART-AND-RENDERING.md` in the Commons saves reading, with its amendments. Labels such as `B2.188` or `img-004 0` are that document's labels for saved images.

## The whole island

| Fact | Value |
|---|---|
| Size | 131 km east to west, 98 km north to south |
| Land | about 8,250 km2 in eleven regions |
| Highest ground | 3,260 m, on the range |
| Ring towns | 15, a median 29 km and 5.9 walking hours apart by road |
| Crossing on foot | Gullet to Cottonwood Ford by road is 219 km and 46 walking hours, about six days |
| Rivers | 113 lines, 8 named, all reaching the sea or one of 120 lakes |

**Shape.** A long range runs north to south down the middle, where the west foreland meets the raised east block. West of it the land is wet and falls slowly to a coast that faces the weather. East of it a dry plateau is cut by canyons that run to the east shore. The north is a cold shield of ponds. The south-west has sunk into a delta, and a city stands on an island across the strait from the bay. The south is a still plain.

**Climate.** The wind is from the west. Air loads over the sea, drops its rain on the climb, and reaches the east block dry. Cold deepens to the north and with height. The island holds a continent's span of climate in 98 km. It is built for a walk.

**The day.** Twenty-four hours, weighted. Night takes 9.5 hours, dawn 3.5, dusk 5, and day 6. The sun stands above 20 degrees for 6 % of the day. Noon is the rare state and it is kept for built things: one sunlit colour against a saturated sky.

**The sky.** Aurora on clear northern nights. A 22 degree halo when thin ice cloud crosses the moon or a low sun. A comet for eight nights in every sixty, low in the north-west. Storm cells over the coast and the marsh, lit from inside.

**Light law.** The source sits inside the frame, low and partly hidden. Sodium keeps value only and colour returns near paraffin. A lamp may flood a wall in one hue, and the hue names the place: sodium on the coast and in the city, mercury green in the works, blue-white in the canyon and the drowned quarter.

**Three scales of building.** Vernacular in one local material per region. Ring towns inside walls, a day apart. Monuments and megastructures far larger than people, the tallest seen from other regions.

## 1. Thaw Country

- **Geography.** 719 km2 of the northern shield south of the works town: low hills, kettle ponds, the long lake of the Thawwater, birch and thin spruce. Seat Hoarstead at 648 m; ring town Thawmere on the west shore.
- **Climate.** Burying snow. Cloud most days, snow falling on most of them, a pale fog. Snow lies in three ages: new, wind-packed and old grey.
- **Light.** Lamp-lit snow under a pink or slate sky. Lit windows carry the frame. Hoar frost with long blue shadows on the clear mornings.
- **Architecture.** Dark timber with steep roofs, buried to the sills. A ring wall of logs and stone. One church.
- **People.** Woodcutters, pond fishers, sledge carriers who run the road to the works town. They count winters, not years.
- **Creatures.** Rooks, a wolf seen at distance, deer, a fox on the ice.
- **What the player finds.** Roads that must be dug before they can be walked. A house kept warm by one family for four generations. The first sight of the smelter glow on the northern horizon.
- **Saves.** AA72, AA201 (Aomori buried to the signs), B2.188 (snow nights under sodium), B8.2 (Hunters in the Snow), B1.233, img-064 77 (hoar frost).

## 2. The Folded Uplands

- **Geography.** 1,779 km2: the range and every spur above 650 m. A moor tier of heath and bright pasture, then an alpine tier of rock, snow bowls and ice to 3,260 m. Seat Cloudsill at 664 m. Ten lone houses stand on the moor, the White House first among them.
- **Climate.** A cloud sea below 950 m and clear air above it. High wind. The valleys fill at night and the peaks stand out of the deck at dawn.
- **Light.** Alpenglow doubled in a tarn. Ridge lines sinking into sky, the far tiers taking the sky's hue before they lose contrast. The planet's shadow in the haze after sunset.
- **Architecture.** White harled stone under slate. One house to a valley. Cairns on the high ground.
- **People.** Shepherds, a guide at Cloudsill, the keeper of the pass. They watch weather for the lowlands and are owed for it.
- **Creatures.** Horses on the moor, gulls blown inland, deer on the green slopes. Bright green pasture is allowed here and nowhere else.
- **What the player finds.** The one road over the range. An avalanche cloud across a bowl. The whole island laid out below from the first clear col, the Tower on the southern plain and the stacks in the north.
- **Saves.** 10-22 set 39 to 42, AA153, AA336, likes 27442321 (the white house on the moor), img-081 65-79 and img-082 0-16 (alpine sets), img-073 23-26 (table mountain), img-087 46-63 (green hills), B5.70 and 24:Y14 (cloud sea).

## 3. The Fog Forest

- **Geography.** 1,737 km2 of the wet west: dark conifers with birch in its autumn, mossy stairs, the Birch Water and its long lake. Seat Birchwell at 395 m.
- **Climate.** Standing fog, the densest air on the island. Drizzle. No wind, so the fog does not clear, it only thins.
- **Light.** Teal and grey. One lamp in fog. A red glow between trunks at dusk. A single shaft into a dark volume where the canopy breaks.
- **Architecture.** Moss-black timber. Stone stairs older than the towns, climbing to shrines painted ox-blood red.
- **People.** Charcoal burners, stair keepers, a congregation that walks the stairs each new moon.
- **Creatures.** Deer, a fox, rooks. Ground fog that holds one animal and lets the rest of the herd vanish.
- **What the player finds.** Paths that are easy to lose and stairs that are not. A birch wood with every trunk marked. The sound of the coast long before the sight of it.
- **Saves.** B1.116 and B1.118 (fog forests in teal), B3.236 (Klimt's birch wood), AA56 (mossy stairs), likes 34062848 (dark forest with a red glow), img-080 66-69, img-068 38.

## 4. The Storm Coast

- **Geography.** 295 km2 along the west shore: sea stacks, columnar basalt, a waterfall into a lit pool, three lights on the headlands. Seat Gullet at the mouth of the Gull, the tidal harbour of the first story.
- **Climate.** Sea fog and swell. Rain on most days, the strongest wind on the island, storm cells offshore with rain shafts under them.
- **Light.** Sodium on wet stone, each lamp doubled in a pool. The great lamp of Gullet. Lightning inside a thunderhead at dusk.
- **Architecture.** A tiled port in slate blue and white. A quay, a weigh-house, a lamp room, a chancel on the hill. A ring wall on the landward side only.
- **People.** The harbour board, the Guild of the Gut, the lamplighters, the congregation of the filament. The cast of the first story lives here.
- **Creatures.** Gulls gathered on one post. Something vast in the water off the stacks, partly seen.
- **What the player finds.** The tide as a clock. The lamp and what its dark cost. The ferry road south to the works.
- **Saves.** B12.371 (storm coast, long exposure), likes 16429529, AA279, AA387, B7.22 (ships in heavy seas), img-082 51-54 (basalt), img-003 54 and 56-67, B14.290.

## 5. The Canyon and Badlands

- **Geography.** 1,990 km2, the largest region: the dry east block at 600 to 1,200 m, cut by the Rust and the Dry Fork into canyons with banded walls. Seat Rustwater on the rim at 1,046 m; ring towns Cottonwood Ford and Dry Chapel on the east shore.
- **Climate.** Dry and far-seeing. The clearest air and the least cloud. Dust at the horizon. Cold clear nights with the Milky Way overhead.
- **Light.** Low sun along strata in rust, sepia, pink, blue and ochre. Hard noon on adobe. One blue-white lamp at a crossroads.
- **Architecture.** Adobe with flat roofs. A white chapel alone. The dry absurd: a cash machine standing in the desert with its screen lit, a motel sign with no motel.
- **People.** Herders, well keepers, a preacher with no parish, drivers who stop for nobody.
- **Creatures.** A herd standing in black water between red walls. Bison and a bear in the far country. Cottonwoods gold along the one still river.
- **What the player finds.** Distance. A day's walk with the destination in view the whole time. The water hole, which is the only place everyone has to meet.
- **Saves.** AA426 (Holmes's canyon panorama), B1.47, likes 19790482, AA227, img-020 42-50 (the water hole), img-085 34-37 (banded hills), img-053 7-14 (cottonwoods), img-001 22 and img-066 71-79 (the dry absurd).

## 6. The Marsh

- **Geography.** 342 km2 of delta in the south-west, under 45 m: reed islands, pools, the slow mouths of the Sedge. Seat Reedhall, ten reed halls round a hearth.
- **Climate.** Storm light. Broken cloud with shafts through it, showers, a mist that lies knee deep.
- **Light.** A bright strip under a black sky. Reeds lit gold against it. Hearth light through woven walls at night.
- **Architecture.** Arched halls of bundled reed, mat and mud brick, after the mudhif of the Marsh Arabs, credited and transformed: here the arches are grown and tied over one winter and the hall is rebuilt by the whole settlement every seventh year. Long boats.
- **People.** Reed cutters and boat builders. They hold the ferry rights to the city and the works and have never sold them.
- **Creatures.** Buffalo standing in the water. Herons and cranes. Eels.
- **What the player finds.** A country where the road is water. The waterline camera, half under the surface. The works on one horizon and the city on the other.
- **Saves.** 10-22 set 10 and 21, AA41, AA176, img-007 75-79 and img-008 0-7 (the reed settlement), img-003 68-77 (waterline lens), img-021 22-25.

## 7. The City Across the Water

- **Geography.** 125 km2 of an island 13 km across, a strait away from the bay. Seat Overwater on the shore that faces the mainland. The ferry to Ironmouth takes 2.3 hours.
- **Climate.** Smoke and drizzle. Haze that holds the lamp light over the roofs.
- **Light.** A skyline doubled in the strait. Three lit windows to a block. Deco doors lit from inside. A stained-glass wall that throws colour on wet paving.
- **Architecture.** Ochre stucco tenements, embankments, deco towers, one planted glass hall. Crowds.
- **People.** Clerks, insurers, tram crews, the Mutual Indemnity of the Lower Shelf, which may or may not still exist.
- **Creatures.** Gulls and rooks. A horse in the fog on the embankment.
- **What the player finds.** The place the first story's paperwork came from. A crowd after weeks of empty country. Rooms: a library wall, a red room, a capsule with one round window.
- **Saves.** AA185 (Blue Moscow), AA247 (embankments), B5.130 (deco doors), likes 55247376 (ochre facades), AA264, B5.151, B5.132, B15.162, img-002 33-47 (capsule rooms), img-027 21-64 (one-hue lamps).

## 8. The Works

- **Geography.** 39 km2 on the mainland shore nearest the city. Seat Ironmouth. Five blast furnaces, twelve stacks to 230 m, three cooling towers, tanks, pipe racks, and a church standing beside them.
- **Climate.** Steam and flare. The air is orange below and green above.
- **Light.** Thousands of small lamps on pipes and towers, doubled in still water. Mercury green, sodium and white. A flare that lights the cloud base.
- **Architecture.** Industry built as sacred architecture: rusted plate, pipe, a control room of glowing panels. The machine voice in every sign: flat colour, one typeface, drawn objects.
- **People.** Shift crews, a works council, the church's one priest. Three shifts, so the place has no night.
- **Creatures.** None that stay. Birds cross it high.
- **What the player finds.** The cathedral of the island and the cost of it. A control room where every dial still answers.
- **Saves.** B3.6 to B3.28 (factory night views), B3.311 (Bethlehem's furnaces), B5.138, AA313, img-025 7-22, img-088 65-79, img-012 26-42, img-031 42-79 (the machine voice).

## 9. The Monument Ground

- **Geography.** 703 km2 of still plain in the south. Seat Plinth; ring town Cypress Gate on the south shore. The Processional and Cypress Water cross it.
- **Climate.** Still and clear. Almost no wind. Thin high cloud that makes haloes.
- **Light.** Long shadows of very tall things. Dusk behind a tower. A moon over ruins, a statue, a harbour.
- **Architecture.** The Tower, 1,200 m, seen from other regions. Seven spires of 420 to 900 m. Two stepped tower-mountains. A dome, a star fort, a prison, colonnades, cypress groves, and one black geometric volume set into a ruin. People live in the megastructures.
- **People.** Custodians, pilgrims, families on the 200th floor who have never been down.
- **Creatures.** Rooks round the Tower. Horses in the cypress shade.
- **What the player finds.** Scale as dread. A building that is in view for three days of walking. The inner arena opens here.
- **Saves.** B3.36 (Piranesi's Prisons), B3.276 (star-fort plans), AA311 (Boullée), B2.251 (John Martin), AA58, img-011 34-78 (tapered towers above forest), img-069 52-57 (tower-mountains), img-003 17-26 (the black volume), B14.541.

## 10. The Drowned Quarter

- **Geography.** 29 km2: the low rim of the city island and the shallows round it. Thirty blocks stand in up to seven metres of water.
- **Climate.** Fog on still water. No wind.
- **Light.** Dawn fog with blocks rising out of it. A blue-white lamp still burning on an upper floor. Reflections that are sharper than the things reflected.
- **Architecture.** The city's old quarter, taken by the water and then by plants: ivy, moss, roots, a station with a pond where the tracks were.
- **People.** A few who never left the upper floors. Boatmen from the marsh who know which streets are deep.
- **Creatures.** Herons on the lamp posts. Fish in the stairwells.
- **What the player finds.** The city's history with the lid off. A flat reached by boat through a window. What the first story's insurer wrote off.
- **Saves.** B5.103 (overgrown stations and ponds), AA404, B4.12 (ruins in snow), AA28, B3.312, img-003 68-77.

## 11. The Far-North Works Town

- **Geography.** 489 km2 of the north coast. Seat Kolvik; ring town Pipe End. Eighteen pastel slab blocks, three smelter stacks to 200 m, pipes above ground, a ring block, a saucer hall and two relics standing alone in the white.
- **Climate.** Whiteout. Blowing snow and a dense pale fog, with clear spells that open the whole sky.
- **Light.** Sodium on drift. Three lit windows to a block. Aurora over the stacks on the clear nights, green below and violet above.
- **Architecture.** Painted concrete in pink, mint, yellow and blue. Insulated pipe on stilts. Vehicles buried to the roof.
- **People.** Smelter crews on long contracts, a planetarium keeper with no visitors.
- **Creatures.** A bear at the tip. Dogs.
- **What the player finds.** The relic: one large made thing with nobody near it. A town that is all inside. The comet, on its eight nights.
- **Saves.** img-004 0-21 (Gronsky's Norilsk), img-003 8-16 and 27-44 (three lit windows, Polar Night), img-002 18-32 (Restricted Areas), img-085 59-73, likes 26261203 (aurora), img-048 30 (comet).
