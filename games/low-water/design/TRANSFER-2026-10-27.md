# Transfers into Low Water, 2026-10-27

What the superframe, the reference library and today's experiments give Low Water's renderer, with the state of each and the test it must pass in the game. Written from the superframe session; nothing in Low Water's own files was changed. The author, 2026-10-27: "All of the rendering materials and techniques should also apply to our engine for Low Water, as well as the techniques from the source materials. And improvements to shaders introduced by the recent physics and mathematics advancements"; then "low water should use buildlang, where possible"; then "but a lot of existing work is rust, python, and JS".

## Language policy for these transfers

- **Data first.** Each transfer that can be a table, a parameter set or a law ships as data: a float binary plus a JSON manifest. The JS bake, the C++ runtime and a shader texture read the same file, so nothing is rewritten.
- **BuildLang where a kernel is small and self-contained.** buildc 1.10.0 exports C-ABI functions into a static library with a generated header, which Low Water's C-ABI game layer can link. The first kernel, below, is proven equal to its JavaScript twin.
- **Existing Rust, Python and JS stay** where they are. Nothing is ported for its own sake. The DECISIONS rule holds: the game and engine come first, and BuildLang runs in parallel, off the critical path.
- **Not yet possible in BuildLang 1.10:**
  - run-time table sizes. Update the same day: BuildLang branch `feat/c-abi-slices-20261027` adds checked slices across the C ABI, and the kernel now takes a pointer, a float count and the grid side. The fixed-size build is kept as `native/lightlut17`;
  - shader output from the same source: the HLSL and GLSL emitters exist but are not tried here.

## 1. Spectral light response (built, tested; wiring not done)

**What it fixes.** Surfaces are lit as albedo times light RGB, after the light is pooled in `light/field.mjs`. Under a near-monochrome lamp, physical surfaces converge in colour, and an RGB product cannot do that. The design note for `sodium` in `light/sources.mjs` asks for exactly this: "one colour for everyone, skin turned to the colour of the road".

**What exists**
- **Tables.** Superframe `tools/light-response.mjs` reads `game/engine/light/sources.mjs` and `kelvin.mjs` and writes one 17 x 17 x 17 table per light kind (18 kinds, 1.0 MB).
  - Each light's spectrum is built from the same recipe the game uses: black bodies plus lines, weighted by luminance share, through the game's own colour matching fit (Wyman, Sloan and Shirley).
  - Surfaces are smooth reflectance spectra.
  - The manifest records the source file's sha256.
  - Regenerate: `node tools/light-response.mjs ../2026-10-16-low-water/game/engine/light/sources.mjs out/light-response`.
- **Samplers.**
  - JS: superframe `lib/medium/lightlut.mjs`.
  - BuildLang: superframe `native/lightlut/main.bld`, exporting `lw_light_reflect(const float* table, size_t count, size_t side, r, g, b) -> Rgb`. Every table read is checked against `count`. It builds with `buildc build --emit staticlib native/lightlut` on a buildc that has C ABI slices.
  - On the real tables the two agree to 2.7e-15: 18 kinds by 2003 surfaces, linked through a C host (`native/lightlut/driver.c`).

**Measured** (superframe `tests/lightlut.test.mjs` and `tests/native-lightlut.test.mjs`, all passing)
- **White.** For every kind marked for use, white returns the game's light colour (within 1e-5, and within 0.01 in a channel the game holds under 0.01).
- **Control.** Through a daylight window, spectral and RGB lighting agree within 0.03 per channel on five test surfaces.
- **Sodium.** The spread of r/(r+g) across five surfaces (a red coat, a green field, a blue door, skin, the road) is 0.19 spectrally against 0.45 for the RGB product, a 57 percent collapse. Candle: 11 percent. Window: 5 percent.
- **Metric change.** The first collapse metric (max minus min chroma) was blind under warm light and was replaced after the first run. The test says so.

**A finding about the light recipes.** The game mixes line colours after clipping them to sRGB, which understates the lines. Read as physical luminance shares, two recipes disagree badly with their game colours:
- **`fluorescent`:** physical white (1.70, 0.03, 6.42) against the game's (1.00, 0.80, 0.88). The 436 nm line carries 6 percent of the luminance, which takes a great deal of blue power.
- **`police`:** blue at 1.63 times the game's level.

The manifest marks these `advisory`; every flame, lamp and sky kind is marked `use: yes`. Restating the line weights as power shares, or measuring a real lamp, would let them be used.

**Wiring (for the light model's owner)**
- **The change.** In `light/field.mjs` (around lines 65, 89 and 93, where `L.rgb * e` is summed), keep a scalar energy per light kind beside the RGB sum.
- **Where it applies.** At composition (`characters/render/shade.mjs` `compose`, and the paint passes), the colour of a surface is the sum over kinds of `reflect(kind, albedo) * E_kind`, in place of `albedo * sum(L.rgb * e)`. Scenes hold one to three kinds, so the cost is a few table reads a sample.
- **Acceptance in the game:**
  - under window light the frame matches the current bake within delta E 1;
  - in the sodium street, the hue spread of costumes and skin falls by more than 40 percent;
  - a flame-lit room keeps its warmth with less smear between red and orange cloth.

## 3. Facades and shadow (findings, three experiments)

Pre-registered tests in `mission-control/2026-10-27-newton-lineage/` (DECO-PREREG.md, DECO3D-PREREG.md and DECO3D-RELIEF-PREREG.md):
- **Setbacks** cannot shade their own walls from the street. Their shadow cascade lies on the terraces and shows from above or afar, under low sun or moon.
- **Relief** (piers and spandrel ledges) carries the street-level shadow. In a 2 x 2 at equal volume, relief adds a cast-shadow share of 0.25 by day; setbacks add 0.001. About 0.11 of that share exists only because the light moves. Relief also raises pattern richness from about 0.95 to 5.8 bits.
- **The change rate** between 15-minute frames was not raised by relief (0.034 for all four buildings). The pre-registered prediction failed on that measure.

For Low Water: the street-level drama of its buildings is in their relief (piers, reveals, ledges) under raking light. Setbacks matter in long and high shots.

## 3b. Directional light from 9 numbers (tested in the superframe; not wired)

`field.mjs` pools light without direction, so a surface is lit the same from every side. In a pre-registered test (`mission-control/2026-10-27-newton-lineage/MOMENTS-PREREG.md`, 400 scenes):
- **Errors.** Storing 9 numbers per point and reading them back as a maximum-entropy density gives a median irradiance error of 0.02 against the exact light. Spherical harmonics from the same 9 numbers give 0.05, and today's direction-free pooling gives 0.66.
- **No negative light.** The maximum-entropy read-back is never negative; spherical harmonics go below zero in 43% of scenes, which shows as dark rings.
- **Where it runs.** The fit costs 5 to 10 ms per probe, so it belongs in the light bake, with a small irradiance table per probe at run time. That table is not built.
- **Code.** Superframe `lib/medium/moments.mjs`; test `tests/moments.test.mjs`.

## 4. Research lane: not ready to ship

From the 2026-10-27 deep dive (`mission-control/2026-10-27-newton-lineage/DEEP-DIVE.md`). Every 2026 mathematical claim named there is unreviewed.
- **Gaussian Light Transport** (arXiv 2609.11430, SIGGRAPH Asia 2026). Global illumination is fitted as a mixture of 13-D Gaussians by minimising the rendering equation's residual: view-independent and fast to evaluate. Candidate for the bake's indirect light. Reproduce one scene from the paper first.
- **Local smoothing and Kakeya (claimed).** Bounds on how sharp caustics and focused wave energy can be at a given band limit. Theory for the water and glass passes, not a shader.
- **ETH refutation (claimed).** If it holds, exact placement and packing searches get faster. Measure the gap between our heuristics and the optimum on small cases now.
- **Navier-Stokes blow-up (claimed).** A generator for flows approaching a singularity, once the construction is public and shown to apply to the true equations.
