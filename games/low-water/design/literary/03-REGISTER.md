# Literary bible 3: the motif and symbol register

Part of [../LITERARY.md](../LITERARY.md). Each entry gives the meaning (for writers, never stated in the
game), where it appears now (quoted), how it changes across the five eras, the mechanic that carries it,
and how its rendered look could shift between eras, dreams and perspective chapters. The look notes are
what the writing asks for; the looks themselves belong to `design/SENSORY.md` and the rendering lead.

Rule for every entry: a symbol is an object somebody owns, uses or made. It never speaks, and no one
explains it.

## The five that persist

### The Haldane table
- **Meaning.** The household as it is, and what the town did to it. Made from a door: a way in, laid flat
  to eat from.
- **Now.** "a table Ilo made out of a door" (`q_coda:18`); the prop, with a goat carved on a leg that
  Bet "started sanding ... off ... in the autumn, when the notices went up, and stopped halfway"
  (`q_props:13`).
- **Across eras.** New in a barn (1); fewer places, the goat half sanded (2); ten families in shifts, the
  goat "black with hands" (3); initials cut in the edge, A.H. struck through (4); in the antique shop,
  "farmhouse, rustic, period", goat still on the leg (5) (`q_props:12-17`).
- **Mechanic.** `@prop q.haldane_table` with era and model states; the narrow lens; the world record
  (a player who sat at it in era 1 finds it priced in era 5, PITCHES 3c.5).
- **Look shift.** Each era paints it in that era's medium as MEDIA rule 1 sets for made pieces: warm
  oil (1), woodcut-hard blacks (2), lithograph out of register (3), flat halftone (4), shop-window glare
  (5). In the dream it is set for more people than live in the house, and the end of it goes past the
  wall. In Bet's chapter it is seen from standing height with her hands on it, never from the camera's
  height.

### The bowls
- **Meaning.** Who is still at the table. A count nobody says aloud.
- **Now.** The bowls are the model's readout (`bowls(haldane)`, `q_coda:18-20`); the fifth "on the
  shelf, upside down" (`q_coda:39`); Bet turns it the right way up and back over (`q_coda:49`); the two
  on the shelf "have been wiped. Somebody wipes them every day." (`q_props:26`).
- **Across eras.** Five matched from Osby fair (1); four, or three (2); odd relief bowls, "One is stamped
  with the district's number" (3); plastic (4); one survivor on the windowsill (5) (`q_props:25-31`).
- **Mechanic.** Rules H1 to H3 (`q_community:227-252`); the dream shows the table the model would lay
  (`q_dreams:15-17`); the read-back prints "Four bowls." flat (`q_readback:29`).
- **Look shift.** The turned-over bowl is the one object the self-deception blur (PITCHES, "What they
  tell themselves", rule 3) may use in era 2: blurred for a protagonist who has not owned his part,
  sharp in the read-back.

### The street sign, Exchange Street
- **Meaning.** Who the street belongs to, said in paint.
- **Now.** "with two holes through the X from October, when the militia fired over the bread queue. Rust
  has started running down from the holes like something leaking." (`q_props:38`).
- **Across eras.** Enamel (1); shot (2); renamed in paint (3); CHAFFERS OUT (4); heritage green with the
  old letters showing (5) (`q_props:37-41`).
- **Mechanic.** The model's standing for the Vell chooses the era 4 and 5 states (`MEDIA:86`).
- **Look shift.** In the glyph style the sign is the place where the type layer and the painted layer
  disagree: the glyph mode shows the old letters under the new paint in every era.

### The exchange scale
- **Meaning.** Fair measure, and what is done in its name.
- **Now.** In era 2 it weighs the requisition: "The weighman's stool is still beside it, and nobody sits
  on it." (`q_props:48`).
- **Across eras.** Grain (1); requisition (2); rations (3); scrap, base left (4); a bench with a plaque
  that "gives the wrong year" (5) (`q_props:47-51`).
- **Mechanic.** Wide lens prop; proposal: a weigh verb that lets a player weigh one sack in era 2 and
  find it light (Orme's night carts, `HOUSEHOLDS:64`).
- **Look shift.** In the dream the scale's pans hold bowls.

### The burial ground
- **Meaning.** Who is written down after they die.
- **Now.** The gap in the railings (`q_props:61`); the unmarked corner (`q_props:60`); on a later run the
  stone "cut by someone who couldn't do the whole name: T. HALDANE" (`q_props:58`), or blank with "a heel
  of bread on it, and the birds haven't had it" (`q_props:59`).
- **Across eras.** Iron (1); gap (2); unmarked section (3); overgrown (4); the fence moved ten yards for
  the ring road, "The corner it used to stand on is under the verge" (5) (`q_props:57-64`).
- **Mechanic.** The world record across runs (`q_record:5-7`); the lore chain's hidden fragment
  (`q_lore:55-60`).
- **Look shift.** Seen only in the wide lens until a run finds the corner; then the camera can go down to
  it. The era 5 verge is painted as a clean road edge with one stone showing at a wrong angle.

## Motifs carried by people and things

### Counting
- **Meaning.** Witness. To count aloud is to make the loss exist.
- **Now.** Anni counting sacks at the cart (`q_ned:148`, `q_will:88-91`); "There's forty-one of us"
  (`q_perspective:34`); the sister at the home "said she'd soon stop that" (`q_coda:58`); SUM's
  "You always want to count them properly" (`q_start:33`).
- **Across eras.** Anni counts in 2; in 3 she is the adult who still counts the shifts at the table; in 5
  Mara counts the households left on the clearance list.
- **Mechanic.** SUM, the tallies prop (`MEDIA` "Anni's tallies"), the read-back.

### Pencil and ink
- **Meaning.** The truth kept where it can be denied, against the record that cannot be.
- **Now.** `q_ned:43-52`, `q_ned:61-63`, `q_ned:245-248`; the cell log "in Will's pencil, pressed hard"
  (`q_props:113`); "The other half a sack is only on the page" (`q_props:114`).
- **Across eras.** See [02-DEVICES-B.md](02-DEVICES-B.md) section 12.
- **Mechanic.** The read-back is ink by definition. Proposal: pencil text in documents is drawn in a
  lighter hand and can be "rubbed" by an option, which enters the record as a record event.

### The hand on the hidden thing
- **Meaning.** What a person keeps checking is still there, because it might be taken.
- **Now.** Will's bread: "he keeps putting his hand on it to see if it's still there" (`q_will:12`) and
  GRIP's "Your hand's on the bread again." (`q_will:18`, `q_will:158`). Ilo with the death paper: "He
  keeps putting his hand on the pocket to see if it's still there." (`q_coda:63`). Tomas keeps "his right
  hand over" the thread (`q_ned:83`). The two exact echoes, the bread and the paper, bind the man who held
  Tomas to the father who lost him.
- **Mechanic.** GRIP's repeated line; a portrait cue (the hand) in the talk panel.

### The blue thread
- **Meaning.** Belonging, worn on the body where it can be seen or hidden.
- **Now.** `q_ned:83`; Holt looks at it (`q_ned:174`); Will in the corner: "He had that thread on his
  wrist still. Gedge wanted it off him and I said leave it." (`q_lore:73`).
- **Across eras.** Open (1); taken off to pass (3); worn again as a choice (5) (`MEDIA:70`).
- **Look shift.** It is the one saturated colour on a Hesh figure in eras 2 and 3. In the dream every
  wrist at the table has one, including the Tarn ones.

### "Only"
- **Meaning.** Minimising as a family trade.
- **Now.** `q_ned:156`, `q_coda:141`, `q_dinner:81-83`; inverted in Anni's "I'm only saying it so
  somebody's said it" (`q_ned:150`), where "only" means the least she can do.
- **Mechanic.** Proposal in [01-DEVICES-A.md](01-DEVICES-A.md) section 7: counted in the final read-back.

### The coat
- **Meaning.** Identity that can be swapped. The coat gets the wrong man taken.
- **Now.** The Orme lad's brown coat on Tomas (`q_perspective:33`, `q_perspective:59-61`); the inspector's
  overcoat and the militia greatcoat as items with PRIDE +1 (`q_rules:171-189`).
- **Across eras.** Proposal: the brown coat with the patch is in the era 3 relief bundle, and an
  allocation clerk gives it to a Hesh boy.

### The good hat
- **Meaning.** Office as inheritance; manners as power.
- **Now.** "The good hat is on the hook by the door" (`q_ned:10`); Abel's "That hat's been in more offices
  than you have" (`q_dinner:55`); "That's my hat on your head." (`q_coda:152`); the item, "People who have
  been sent a return know it." (`q_rules:182`); Tomas's father "still talks about your father's hat"
  (`q_ned:85`).
- **Across eras.** On Nan's sideboard in era 5, "the only thing Kit asks about" (`MEDIA` Furrowe hat
  photograph).

### Bread
- **Meaning.** Everything the economy runs on, carried in pockets.
- **Now.** The heel in Will's pocket (`q_will:12`); "the heel on Abel's plate because it's the softest"
  (`q_dinner:16`); Abel halves it for Ned (`q_dinner:113`); the heel on the blank stone (`q_props:59`).
- **Across eras.** The relief loaf (3), Halvard's "Bread for the World" billboard (4), the food bank (5).

### The song
- **Meaning.** The Hesh inner life, heard by the Tarn as noise.
- **Now.** Ilo "sang all day" (`q_dinner:81`); Tesk's prayer for the water, heard as "That warbling they
  do" (`q_will:13-15`).
- **Gap and proposal.** No melody or words exist. Write one short Hesh water song in Heshen (invented),
  heard whole once in era 1 at the harvest, in pieces after. In era 5 it is the sound under the clip,
  somebody humming off camera. Its words are never translated.

### The door
- **Meaning.** Who is let in. The table was a door; the granary door is the climax; Tomas leaves Ned's
  door open "and the cold comes in" (`q_ned:121`); the clearance (era 5) is at the Haldane house door
  (`STORY:109`).

## Symbols proposed for the long game
- **The ledger with barless sevens.** Mott's family habit, "his sevens without a bar, the way his father
  wrote them" (`q_props:82`). It proves whose hand wrote 310. In era 5 Frank Mott writes his grocery list
  the same way, at the Haldane table (`HOUSEHOLDS:24`).
- **The over-painted portrait.** Pask over the Crown Council's man, "the old face shows at the collar in
  raking light" (`MEDIA:46`). The look shift: the player turns it round in era 5 and the two faces are
  in the same painting.
- **The Crown.** In the content it is a pub on Mill Street (`q_discourse:9`) that keeps its name across
  five eras (`MEDIA:114`), while the Crown Council that ruled in era 1 (`WORLD:10`) is gone by era 2. The
  old sovereign survives as a sign over a bar where the rumour is carried. Use it; do not explain it.
