# Literary bible 8: writing, dialogue, internal monologue, worldbuilding

Part of [../LITERARY.md](../LITERARY.md). The author, 2026-10-22, verbatim: "Writing, Character Dialogue,
Internal Monologue, and Worldbuilding are crucial, and we want to touch hard on the topics, and
societal/cultural pain points, and lows of humanity covered in the months we have been working." The
pain points have their own register, [09-PAIN-POINTS.md](09-PAIN-POINTS.md). Sample lines are PROPOSALS.

## 1. Writing (narration)

**What the slice does well.** Short declaratives in the present tense; one physical detail that carries
the feeling; no explanation after the image.
- "Mott takes his hat off and holds it in both hands. It's an old hat, and the brim is coming away at the
  back." (`q_ned:38`)
- "Bet gets up and turns the bowl on the shelf the right way up, and then she stands there with her hand
  on it, and then she turns it back over." (`q_coda:49`)
- "Davey's shoulders come up round his ears and stay there until the hand has gone back." (`q_dinner:190`)

**What to watch.** The narrator's closing irony (see [06-ALLEGORY-AUDIT.md](06-ALLEGORY-AUDIT.md)
section 2): ration it to one a scene. Second person appears in the voices by design ("Your hand's on the
bread", `q_will:18`) and was removed from prop text (design/state/story.md, 2026-10-22); keep that split.

**Scaling to 15 hours.** The slice's pacing tells a writer how: about 3,000 to 3,500 words read in one run
(PROGRESS step 158) for about 30 minutes. The design budget is 5 to 6 hours of main story (`STORY:8-17`),
and the author's 2026-10-22 expansion grows the main path modestly and puts most added hours into
optional and replayable content (game memory note). The bible's position: 15 hours is reached by breadth
(six classes, perspective pairs, households, the world record's second runs), never by longer scenes. The
brevity rule stands: "Of two lines that do the same thing, the shorter wins" (REWORK-PLAN section 8).

**PROPOSALS for narration.**
- One wide beat (W) before every narrow scene (S), each under 80 words, as the slice does at the tower
  (`q_start:16-19`).
- Every era opens with the same view from the bell tower, so the player measures the fall by comparing.
  In era 5 the bell tower is "a phone mast" (`WORLD:25`), and the view is from the car park.

## 2. Character dialogue

**Strengths to keep.**
- Speech that runs away from its speaker: "It's only four, and it's only till the review, and the
  review's only, it's only, Aaron, hold the horse, will you, he's going to go." (`q_ned:156`)
- Children who say the true thing at the wrong time: "Is he in the cells? Can I come and see?"
  (`q_dinner:164`); "I'm twelve." (`q_dinner:111`).
- Contempt spoken with love, the PITCHES 3a rule 3, in one breath: "I'd have trusted him with my life. Not
  with my grain." (`q_dinner:81`).
- The confession that circles and runs itself down, under DIALOGUE-STANDARD 3a: Will on the Crown's steps
  (`q_lore:70-77`), which ends on a repeat, "You'll not hear me say different", never on a summary.

**Gaps.**
- The Hesh never speak to each other without a Tarn listener. Every Hesh line in the slice is said to an
  official or across a table the officials have set.
- The perpetrators are always tired or drunk. Nobody in the slice enjoys power, and some people do.
- Women's talk is mostly about feeding others. Edie, Mary, Bet and Lil each get one line that is about
  themselves or none.

**PROPOSALS.**
- A Hesh-only scene per era. Era 2: the Haldanes walking home from the post, in Heshen and Tarnish mixed,
  Aaron furious, Tomas making a joke of it, Bet stopping both. PROPOSAL, Tomas: "He had a bayonet and he
  was scared of Anni. Did you see him? He was scared of a girl with a sum."
- One perpetrator who enjoys it. Gedge already whistles while he chalks six sacks (`q_will:165`). Give him
  one line of pleasure. PROPOSAL, Gedge: "Six. That's a good day, sergeant. That's a day they'll talk about
  at the Crown."
- One line each for the women about themselves. PROPOSAL, Mary, alone at the stove after the dinner: "I
  had a blue dress for the harvest supper. I sold it in November. I keep forgetting and going to the
  wardrobe."

## 3. Internal monologue

**What exists.** The protagonist's mind is the sixteen voices (`q_rules:48-127`). Five self-lies are tagged
`{self: id}` and paired in the read-back (`q_readback:37-62`):

| Self-lie | Voice and line | Paired with |
|---|---|---|
| `q.sl.signed` | SUM: "You've signed that the sheet says three-ten. Nobody can say you didn't." (`q_ned:71`) | What Ned did with his pencil (`q_readback:40-42`) |
| `q.sl.add_up` | SUM: "Two-ninety is what they sent. You only add up what they send." (`q_office:31`) | The Haldane table's bowls (`q_readback:46`) |
| `q.sl.check` | PRIDE: "Nobody's going to count those forty sacks again." (`q_office:109`) | The same table (`q_readback:50`) |
| `q.sl.forty_carts` | BALLAST: "If one cart goes through without a pass, forty go tomorrow" (`q_will:63`) | What happened to the cart (`q_readback:54-58`) |
| `q.sl.never_hand` | PRIDE: "You've never once lifted a hand to that boy. Not once." (`q_dinner:191`) | "Davey's shoulders came up round his ears ... Edie pulled her sleeve down" (`q_readback:62`) |

Most voice lines are true, as the design requires so the player keeps trusting the voices (PITCHES, "What
they tell themselves", the risk): THE DARK's "You looked away before you saw whether he was breathing"
(`q_granary:106`) is true and unwelcome. The ratio in the slice is about five self-lies among roughly
thirty voice lines (counted by reading the `voice:` records in the scene files; approximate).

**Gaps.**
- Voices never argue with each other in one beat. THEMES.md 10.4 designed "Louder voices that contradict
  each other" for a growing gap, and the Quota has not used it.
- The victims have inner voices in Bet's chapter (`q_perspective:37`, `q_perspective:52`, `q_perspective:61`),
  and Bet's "That's the Orme lad's coat" works as a self-lie of mercy. It is not tagged, so the read-back
  cannot pair it.
- The other four classes have no self-lies written.

**PROPOSALS.**
- Two voices in one beat at the gap. PROPOSAL, at the dinner on the blamed route: SUM: "Two-ninety was the
  villages. The rest is the Commissioner's business." CARBON: "The goat comes and smells his hand. You're
  standing on the rock. There's nothing under it."
- Tag Bet's coat line as a victim's self-lie and print it in a separate column of the read-back, headed
  by her name, never by a verdict. The point: the people harmed also tell themselves things, and the
  game holds them to nothing for it.
- A self-lie for each other class, written so the player agrees on first hearing:
  - PROPOSAL, Lind (CONDOLENCE): "If you write starvation they'll take him for the district count and
    she'll never see him again. Write his stomach."
  - PROPOSAL, Ashby (FORM LETTER): "The paper would never have run the boy. You'd only have lost the
    granary picture as well."
  - PROPOSAL, Orme (THE CHIT): "If you sell it at night, somebody eats it. If they take it at noon, it goes
    on a train."
  - PROPOSAL, Mott (SUM): "Moor Holt wrote three-twenty. You're under Moor Holt. You're the honest one."
- The blur grammar (PITCHES, "What they tell themselves", rule 3) first appears on Edie's sleeve at the
  Brack table, small and plainly true, so the player learns it before it matters.

## 4. Worldbuilding

**What exists.** The plain, its river and eleven villages; three peoples with faith, language, food,
names, dress and what they are picked on for (`WORLD:7-61`); invented slurs (`WORLD:50-52`); the myths
told about each people, in full (`WORLD:54-61`); five eras with a government and an instrument each
(`WORLD:63-71`); the era 5 movements (`WORLD:73-84`). The world's rules are written as a model a designer
can read (`SYSTEMS` sections 1 and 2) and every model rule carries a `because:` line.

**Gaps.**
- **No Heshen.** Not one word of the Hesh language appears in the content, though QUOTA-VOICES 11.2 asks
  for Heshen words for water, rain, the Well and kin.
- **No scripture.** The Furrow Church and the Well have no text, so their sermons and prayers have nothing
  to quote.
- **No Directorate doctrine.** Pask has a deal (`q_office:14`) and no ideology. The voice table gives the
  Captain "comrade" as a full stop (QUOTA-VOICES 11.2), and the word is never used in the content.
- **No calendar.** The slice names Lent (`MEDIA:63`), the lambing (`q_coda:41`) and Osby fair
  (`q_props:25`), and nothing ties them into a year.

**PROPOSALS.**
- **A Heshen word list of about forty words** (water, rain in its nine kinds, kin terms, the Well's
  prayers), built by the story lead with the voice director, used untranslated. The "nine words for rain"
  joke (`WORLD:44`) becomes true and specific, and a player who learns two of them hears the rest.
- **Scripture fragments** for each faith, ten lines each, original: the Furrow Church's Sower readings
  and the Well's water prayers. People quote them wrongly, as people do.
- **The Directorate's poster words**, era 2 and 3: "THE PLAIN FEEDS THE PLAN", "EVERY SACK A TRACTOR".
  Pask never uses them; Krall does.
- **A plain calendar**: Osby fair (late summer), harvest supper, the census (every twelve years, which
  is why era 1 and era 2 are twelve years apart), Lent, the Well's night prayer, the lambing. Era 5 adds
  the commemoration day. Each era's slice is set on a calendar day, so returning players feel the year.
- **One proverb per people**, quoted by a named person with a source ("my mother used to say"), so it
  passes the dialogue standard as something a person repeats, never something the writer coins.
