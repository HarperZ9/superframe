# Literary bible 7: diction and tone, per class and per faction

Part of [../LITERARY.md](../LITERARY.md). This guide sits beside the voice director's files and does not
replace them: `design/voice/QUOTA-VOICES.md` (speech of the plain, the slice cast, the read-back, the
slur rule) and `design/voice/quota-cast.json`. Where they differ, the voice director's files govern
performance and this file governs word choice.

Every sample line is a **PROPOSAL** for the author to approve. Each was written to the read-aloud test
of DIALOGUE-STANDARD section 1: this person, this place, this listener, now. None is in the content.

## 1. Rules that hold for every speaker

- Concrete nouns from the speaker's work and house: sacks, nibs, passes, ration cards, slack and
  cardboard, a heel of bread. No abstract nouns for the theme (hunger, justice, truth) in anyone's mouth
  except as a quoted official phrase.
- Numbers are said the way the speaker's trade says them. Ned: "two hundred and five". Mott and Will:
  "three-ten", "forty". Pask: "Three-forty" (`q_office:13`).
- No epigram, mirrored pair, corrective negation or closing zinger in speech (DIALOGUE-STANDARD section 4).
- One habit word per person, used often enough to be a tic and never explained.

## 2. The six family-trade lines (the classes)

### Mott, the Clerks
- **Diction.** Apologies, the clerk's "Mr" and "Mrs", sheet names in capitals, sums half said aloud. The
  family lie is in his first scene: "Everyone's writing three hundred." (`q_ned:39`).
- **Tone.** Anxious, decent, quick to agree. Says sorry and means it, and does it anyway.
- **Era drift.** Era 1 Edwin, proud of a clean register; era 5 Frank, retired, pedantic and frightened.
- PROPOSAL, Edwin Mott, era 1, at the census table: "Haldane. After the farm. It's only for the
  register, it doesn't have to be your name at home."
- PROPOSAL, Frank Mott, era 5: "My dad's sevens. Look. No bar. He did them like that on my birthday
  cards."

### Furrowe, the Inspectors
- **Diction.** "Only" (`q_ned:156`, `q_coda:141`); the formula "Received and found correct"
  (`q_ned:70`); full numbers; questions about health that are really about the sheet ("It's the dry
  cough, is it, or the wet?", `q_ned:16`).
- **Tone.** Tired, precise, says less than he knows (QUOTA-VOICES 11.3, Ned's row).
- **Era drift.** Abel's pride in the hat (1); Ned's pencil (2); Pell's surveyor's caution (3); Ruth
  the auditor, who uses "adjustment" without hearing it (4); Nan, who says "I'm only saying" like her
  great-grandfather (5).
- PROPOSAL, Ruth Furrowe, era 4, at the mill: "I'm not closing anything. I'm counting what's here so
  somebody else can close it."
- PROPOSAL, Ned, era 2, at the road post, to himself (SUM): "Four sacks, two seed. Six. You'll want to
  write six."

### Ashby, the Correspondents
- **Diction.** Captions, deadlines, the editor's name, what the picture "shows". Says "nice" about
  pictures of terrible things, because that is the trade's word.
- **Tone.** Quick, charming, a little ashamed, funnier than anyone else at the table.
- PROPOSAL, Lena Ashby, era 2: "Can you stand on the left, Mr Krall, the light's better, and look at the
  sacks, not me. Lovely. Again."
- PROPOSAL, Ellie Ashby, era 5, to her camera: "So I'm on Mill Street, where it happened, and honestly I
  just want to say I'm not here to make anyone the bad guy."

### Lind, the Doctors
- **Diction.** Body words said kindly to families and clinically to clerks; the cause-of-death phrase as
  a formula ("intestinal illness", `HOUSEHOLDS:47-48`). Asks what the patient ate and already knows.
- **Tone.** Gentle, exhausted, practical. Lies to let families bury their dead, which is the line's lie:
  "I wrote what would let the families bury them" (`HOUSEHOLDS:51`).
- PROPOSAL, Dr Clara Lind, era 2, to Bet: "I'll put it down as his stomach, Mrs Haldane. Then they'll let
  you have him."
- PROPOSAL, Dr Sam Lind, era 5, at the vaccine centre: "We had forty booked from the Upper Town. Six came.
  I rang the others. Most of them thought I was the council."

### Brack, the Militia and the police
- **Diction.** Orders said as weather ("Order's the order", `q_will:168`); "lad" for juniors and for the
  people he holds; the drill yard's short sentences; "missus" (`q_will:85`).
- **Tone.** Parade voice at work; at home, drunk, soft and generous, "which is the dangerous voice"
  (QUOTA-VOICES 11.3).
- **Era drift.** Josh at the road post (1), Will (2, 3), Ann on the picket line (4), Leon in the van (5).
  Each generation's slur changes and the cadence does not.
- PROPOSAL, Will, era 3, at the allocation riot: "Back on the step, all of you. On the step. I'm not
  asking twice. I am asking twice. Back."
- PROPOSAL, Leon Brack, era 5, to his own son: "You don't film the police, Callum. You film it if
  somebody hits me. That's different."

### Orme, the Millers and bankers
- **Diction.** Prices, margins, "the market", first names for workers and surnames for rivals; gentle
  phrasing of hard things. The line's lie: "The price is the price." (`HOUSEHOLDS:68`).
- **Tone.** Reasonable, hospitable, never in a hurry.
- PROPOSAL, George Orme, era 2: "I'm not holding anything back. I'm holding it till Thursday."
- PROPOSAL, Charlotte Orme, era 5, at the syndicate: "We've done the consultation. We had a very good
  turnout from the people who'll be living there."

## 3. Factions and carriers

### The Directorate (Pask, Holt)
- **Diction.** Pask speaks plainly on purpose: "I'll be plain, because I'm tired" (`q_office:14`). Holt
  reads aloud; written Arlen said aloud (QUOTA-VOICES 11.2). Figures, titles, "the review".
- PROPOSAL, Holt, era 2, reading an order to the cells: "District six. Requisition at the post. Grain
  found is hoarding. That's the lot. Questions. No. Good."

### The brigade chairmen
- **Diction.** Meeting voice, big and glad; "the plain", "my barns", "ask anybody" (`q_office:95`).
- PROPOSAL, Krall, era 2, after the meeting: "They'll put my figure in the Gazette, Ned. Iris has bought
  three copies already."

### Mill Street
- **Diction.** Work and money, sewing and slack; "I'm not daft" (`q_dinner:124`); questions that are
  orders ("Did you ask him?", `q_dinner:148`).
- PROPOSAL, Edie, era 2, to a neighbour at the relief queue: "No, he's well. He's on nights. It's the
  cold, it gets in his temper."

### The Hesh families
- **Diction.** Full forms in Tarnish, no contractions under stress (Old Tesk: "I was not singing",
  `q_will:15`); Heshen words for water, rain, the Well and kin when moved (QUOTA-VOICES 11.2). The
  half-Hesh children speak Tarnish first and apologise.
- **Tone.** Patient in front of the Tarn, sharp and funny among themselves. The slice shows only the
  first; the second is needed.
- PROPOSAL, Aaron Haldane, era 2, to Anni, out of earshot of the post: "You're counting for him now, are
  you? Count his teeth. He's got eleven."
- PROPOSAL, Dorrah Tesk, era 2, see [04-LORE-SPINE.md](04-LORE-SPINE.md) section 2.

### The Furrow Church
- **Diction.** Scripture of fields and thrift; the minister's plain care at a deathbed and his pulpit's
  slow blame (`q_discourse:55`).
- PROPOSAL, the minister, era 2, at a sickbed in Haldane Yard: "I'll sit. No, I'll sit, you go and lie
  down. I've sat with worse than me."
- PROPOSAL, the same man, the Sunday after, from the pulpit: "Some in this town have bread they did not
  sow. I name nobody. You know your neighbours."

### The Well (Hesh faith)
- **Diction.** Water, washing, the shared cup; the dead "buried with water" (`WORLD:43`).
- PROPOSAL, Old Tesk, in the cells: "If I die in here, sergeant, somebody must wash me. Not you. Ask for
  Dorrah."

### Halvard Grain Company
- **Diction.** Contract terms, tonnage, an outsider's polite Tarnish.
- PROPOSAL, a Halvard tally clerk on the export train, era 2, to a soldier: "Four hundred short again. They
  dock me for it, you know. Not them, me."

### The Crown (the pub on Mill Street)
- **Diction.** The queue's talk: "everybody says" (`q_ned:92`), what the radio said, who was seen with whom.
- PROPOSAL, the landlord, era 2: "I'm not saying it's the Hesh. I'm saying the radio said it and I've not
  heard anybody say different."

### Era 5 movements
Each speaks from what it pays and loses (PITCHES 3b, "Every side at full strength"). One line each:
- PROPOSAL, Furrow League: "My dad worked forty years in that mill and they gave his job to a company."
- PROPOSAL, Plain Union: "Rent's up a third since the syndicate bought the row. Who's that for?"
- PROPOSAL, New Edrin Alliance: "I'm not calling you racist, Gran. I'm saying that word's got a history."
- PROPOSAL, Open Tarn: "The bread in that food bank is from Halvard. You're welcome."
- PROPOSAL, Well Assembly: "She can come in when she's covered. That's not me, that's the Well."
- PROPOSAL, none: "Can we just eat?"

## 4. The inner voices

The sixteen voices keep their records (`q_rules:48-127`). Diction by compartment:
- **THE PEN** (SUM, PRECEDENT, SMALL PRINT, CARBON): numbers, documents, the past in the present tense.
  SUM's comfort is the false one most often ("You've signed that the sheet says three-ten. Nobody can say
  you didn't.", `q_ned:71`).
- **THE COAT** (KNEES, BALLAST, PRIDE, GRIP): the body and the uniform. GRIP talks about what the hands
  feel ("Your hand's on the bread again.", `q_will:18`).
- **THE HAT** (CONDOLENCE, FORM LETTER, THE CHIT, HEARSAY): people and what they want. CONDOLENCE is
  "A little too sure it understands" (`q_rules:92`).
- **THE NOSE** (SMELL, THE DARK, UNDERTOW, SALT): what is hidden and what fear knows.

Self-lies are said in the same voice as true lines (QUOTA-VOICES 11). See [08-CRAFT.md](08-CRAFT.md)
section 3.

## 5. The read-back

One flat voice, READBACK, never imitating a character (QUOTA-VOICES 11.4). Diction: time, place, a
name, a number, the player's own words in quotation marks. No adjectives of judgement. The existing rows
meet this ("Four bowls.", `q_readback:29`).
