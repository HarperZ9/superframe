# Literary bible 2: devices, foreshadowing to tone

Part of [../LITERARY.md](../LITERARY.md). Same conventions as [01-DEVICES-A.md](01-DEVICES-A.md).

## 8. Foreshadowing

**What exists.**
- The dawn view ends on the burial ground: "Since the frost they have been leaving the coffins in the
  church porch" (`q_start:19`). Tomas's unmarked burial (`q_lore:73`) is the same fact, closer.
- Mott's son "has the cough" (`q_ned:11`), and on the stairs Mott "coughs, and it isn't the cold"
  (`q_ned:73`). Sickness follows hunger in the model (R6, `SYSTEMS:56`) and in the Lind line's
  "intestinal illness" (`HOUSEHOLDS:47`).
- Davey: "Sefa Tesk's not allowed to sit by the stove at school now. Mr Tamm moved him" (`q_dinner:186`).
  This points at era 3, when Heshen is banned in school (`STORY:72`).
- The prop for the burial ground names "the gap in the railings ... where people climb in at night to
  bury their own" (`q_props:61`) before the corner scene tells the player who used it (`q_lore:73`).
- The dream. It runs the community model a week ahead through the dreamer's fear and shows the table
  that future lays (`q_dreams:3-6`). Measured after the settle fix: the inspector's dream came true on 9
  of 14 runs and the sergeant's on 6 of 8 (`q_dreams:11-12`; PROGRESS step 169). The earlier figure of
  9 of 22 (step 167) was taken with a bug that hid every sergeant's match. Both readings are held:
  "THE DARK: it is what is coming" and "SUM: it is the day's count, come back at night"
  (`q_dreams:18-19`).

**Gaps.** Foreshadowing inside one day is strong. Across eras there is almost none written yet, and a
15-hour game needs plants that pay off a generation later. The Sefa line also has a continuity fault
(see [04-LORE-SPINE.md](04-LORE-SPINE.md) section 5).

**Proposals.**
- **Plant in era 1, pay in era 5.** Every era 1 scene carries one object or phrase that returns in era 5
  changed. Ilo's carved spoon with a name in the handle (`MEDIA:71`) is placed in era 1 as a gift and
  found in era 5's antique shop with a price ticket.
- **Dreams that point across eras.** The dream system already copies the model and runs it forward
  without touching the game (`q_dreams:3`). Run one dream per era a whole era ahead instead of a week,
  and show a prop state from the next era (the Haldane table's era 3 "shifts" state, `q_props:15`). The
  dream comes true only in what the model decides, so it holds both readings by construction.
- **The world record as foreshadowing.** A second run opens on the post board with the previous
  character's line chalked under the notice (`q_props:122`). The player reads their old words before
  reaching the scene where they said them. That is foreshadowing whose source is the player.

## 9. Hyperbole

**What exists.** The plot runs on official hyperbole: Krall's 340 for a village that grew 190
(`q_granary:54`), Moor Holt's 320 (`q_ned:39`), Sallow's 320 written "from the car" (`q_coda:138`). The
characters' own exaggerations are literal under famine: Mary's "There's people up that hill would kill
for that plate" (`q_dinner:96`). Holt's "Eleven mile in that" (`q_granary:19`) is admiration that sounds
like a complaint. Abel's "Nine words for rain and none for thank you" (`q_dinner:79`) is the contempt
joke built as an exaggeration.

**Gaps.** Hyperbole has one tone, the lie. The Hesh have no voice of exaggeration of their own (humour,
boasting, a tall tale), which leaves them flat.

**Proposals.**
- Name it as a system: the quota is hyperbole made into law. SUM, which "Distrusts a round figure"
  (`q_rules:52`), gets a line at each inflated number that measures the exaggeration in the body: what
  130 sacks the barns never had would weigh, how many carts.
- Give Ilo a tall tale. PROPOSAL, Ilo at the era 1 harvest supper: "On the hill we had a ram so big the
  sheep paid him rent." It is the joke the Tarn laugh at, and it is the last time the town laughs with a
  Hesh man.

## 10. Imagery, all five senses

The art and sensory pass is a separate document, `design/SENSORY.md` (written by the art and sensory
agent: looks, transitions, audio, haptics). This section covers only what the writing calls for.

**What exists, by sense.**
- **Sight.** The strongest. "A woman near the back is holding a child who is too big to be held"
  (`q_start:17`); "a shawl frozen to the cobbles" (`q_granary:93`); the grain "running out round him like
  sand in a glass" (`q_granary:103`).
- **Sound.** "Through the door you can hear him kicking something until it breaks" (`q_will:36`); "the
  sound starts as feet on the frozen square and turns into voices. Then a fist on the door. Then a lot of
  fists" (`q_granary:18`); Tesk's prayer heard as "That warbling" (`q_will:13`).
- **Smell.** "The smell of the bucket in the end cell and of Gedge's pipe" (`q_will:11`); Will's slur
  route, "the whole hill stinks of fat" (`q_office:119`). Smell is where the slander lives.
- **Touch.** "the wood's frozen and takes skin off his lip" (`q_granary:53`); Bet's sack "is heavier than
  she thought" (`q_perspective:51`); Tomas's arm: "There's hardly anything inside the sleeve, the coat's that much too big"
  (`q_will:123`).
- **Taste.** The weakest, in a game about hunger. Food is counted and seen: "hot water with salt in it,
  which Bet calls soup" (`q_coda:21`); "a stew that is mostly turnip" (`q_dinner:16`). Nobody tastes
  anything.

**Gaps.** Taste is missing. Hunger also has bodily images that the slice avoids (the swelling, the
skin, the hair), and cold has no sound of its own.

**Proposals: the five senses per scene type** (writing beats; looks and audio in SENSORY.md).

| Scene type | Sight | Sound | Smell | Touch | Taste |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| The table (narrow lens) | What is in the bowl, and what is not | Spoons on the rim; the counting | The stove's fuel (cardboard at the Bracks', `q_dinner:122`) | The bowl's heat, or its lack | One taste per meal: salt, turnip, the bread heel's crust |
| The office | Ink against pencil | A chair creaks (`q_office:42`); coughing | Wet wool, the biscuits nobody is offered (`q_office:9`) | Cold pen, gloves off (`q_ned:10`) | None: nobody eats here, by design |
| The post and road | The white road, the brazier | Wind with nothing to stop it (`q_will:71`) | Brazier smoke; horse | Bayonet into sacks | The heel of bread in Will's pocket |
| The cells | Two lamps, one smoking | The prayer, the bucket, keys | Bucket, pipe | Stone bench | The water ration |
| The crowd (wide lens) | The queue's shape | Its silence: "Nobody talks" (`q_start:17`) | Many bodies in the cold | Standing on gratings for the warmth (`q_start:41`) | None |
| The dream | The table as it may be | Steam that makes no sound | Gruel that smells like a real meal | Bowl held against a coat | The first warm taste in the game, which never comes |

- PROPOSAL, taste beat, Bet's chapter: "She puts her finger in the gruel to see if it's hot enough for
  Anni. It's hot. It tastes of the pot." One line; the only time the slice says what food tastes like.
- PROPOSAL, the hungry body, aftermath only: Edie turning in a waistband for a woman whose skirt now
  goes round her twice. She says nothing about it; the pins are in her mouth.

## 11. Irony

**Verbal, existing.** Holt: "Take your cart. Take your Hesh. I'll see you at the office." (`q_ned:188`).
Pask: "It's a known thing. Good. That's the first sensible sentence anybody's said to me this morning."
(`q_office:71`). Lil on Sallow: "Lovely manners." (`q_coda:138`). Tomas, after four sacks are taken:
"Thank you for the seed." (`q_will:146`).

**Situational, existing.** The talk-out's best outcome sends Anni to count "the sacks they took off us"
into the brigade's store (`q_coda:92`): the girl who counted to protect her family is hired to count
against it. The granary is "the one the paper photographs" (`q_granary:14`). Pask's tractors are bought
to "stop this happening next winter" with the grain that makes it happen this winter (`q_office:14`).
Holt calls the crowd "Mud-hands" and says "My mother was one" (`q_granary:21`).

**Dramatic, existing.** Bet's chapter: the man the guards take wears "the brown coat with the patch"
(`q_perspective:59`), and she tells herself "That's the Orme lad's coat" (`q_perspective:61`). The player
saw her put that coat on Tomas (`q_perspective:33`). The engineers' note says the game never says which
is true (`q_perspective:5-6`). This is the best dramatic irony in the slice.

**Gaps.** Dramatic irony across runs is built (the world record) and barely used in scenes.

**Proposals.**
- **Dramatic irony through the world record.** On a second run, the Haldane table's era 2 state is
  unchanged, but the player knows the stone in the corner (`q_props:58`). Bet's line "He'll be down for
  the lambing" (`q_coda:41`) now plays to someone who has read the cell log. Write two or three lines per
  era whose meaning only exists for a player with a prior fact (`prior(...)` conditions exist,
  `q_props:58`).
- **Verbal irony in the read-back is forbidden.** The flat voice never uses it (QUOTA-VOICES 11.4).
  Irony belongs to people; the document only sets lines side by side.
- **Situational irony per era**, one each: the census clerk who names Ilo (era 1) has a grandson who
  lives in the Mott house with ten families (era 3); the auditor who closes the mill (era 4) is a
  Furrowe, whose great-grandfather was struck off for an honest figure.

## 12. Metaphor

**What exists.** Pencil and ink as private truth and public record: "Nobody reads the pencil. Nobody has
ever asked him about the pencil." (`q_ned:52`); India rubber that "comes off" (`q_ned:63`); Mrs Tesk
looking "at the pencil and then at him" and THE DARK: "Pencil again." (`q_ned:247-248`). The household is
a set of bowls. The district is a sheet of figures. The district "is reviewed" means the bayonets come
back (`q_office:50`).

**Gaps.** The pencil metaphor needs an era-by-era life or it stays one man's habit.

**Proposals.** Carry pencil and ink through the five eras as the motif of what can be rubbed out: era 1
the census register in ink, the seasonal hands in pencil; era 3 the allocation list, with the Hesh
families pencilled "pending"; era 4 the bank's map shaded in pencil, then inked at the board meeting;
era 5 the clip's comments, which can be deleted, against the archive, which cannot. See the register,
[03-REGISTER.md](03-REGISTER.md).

## 14. Personification

**What exists.** The sixteen voices are faculties given speech (`q_rules:48-127`): SUM "Distrusts a
round figure", PRIDE is "the uniform". The model's rules personify talk in their reasons: "the pub
forgets" and "the pulpit keeps it" (`q_discourse:58`, `q_discourse:66`); "talk travels faster than paper"
(`q_discourse:47`). Rust runs from bullet holes "like something leaking" (`q_props:38`).

**Gaps.** Personification is safe where the dialogue standard allows it: voices are "Lamb's own habits of
mind" (DIALOGUE-STANDARD section 5), and now the protagonist's. It must never reach a person speaking as
a symbol.

**Proposals.** Let the scale, the bell and the train be given human verbs only by characters who would
say so: Abel, "That scale never told a lie in thirty years"; a child in era 4, "the mill's dead." The
narration stays literal.

## 15. Simile

**What exists.** The slice's similes come from the speaker's own world and are mostly domestic:
"the way you'd say it in church" (`q_office:12`); "as if the table might not be there" (`q_dinner:161`);
"the way you'd take something off a dog" (`q_dinner:201`); "the way you'd say it to someone who'd stepped
on your hand and then stepped off" (`q_will:147`); "holds it against her like a child" (`q_granary:81`);
"the cat, which is so thin it looks like a different kind of animal" (`q_dinner:129`).

**Gaps.** None in quality. The rule should be written down so new writers keep it.

**Proposals.** The simile rule: a simile's second term comes from the point-of-view person's trade or
house. Ned's from paper and offices, Will's from the post and the drill yard, Bet's from the kitchen and
the barn. Era 5's similes come from screens, which is its own comment.

## 16. Symbolism

The register is [03-REGISTER.md](03-REGISTER.md). One finding belongs here: the number 41. It is the
day (`slice.lwd:15`), Ned's age (`q_world:134`, "Forty-one."), and the crowd Anni counts: "There's
forty-one of us" (`q_perspective:34`). The archived Gullet acts used "forty-one chalk ticks" as their
dream's unexplained number (THEMES.md 9a). Nothing in the files says the repeat is chosen. It should be
chosen on purpose or removed; this bible proposes keeping it as the one number the game never explains.

## 17. Theme

**What exists.** THEMES.md's six questions were written for Gullet and carry over (06-KEEP-CUT, line 16).
The Quota's own theme is stated in the author's words: how "incentive-based issues deny groups of people
access to life and liberty" (PITCHES.md, revision 4 preface). The slice holds its themes in what people
pay: Mott's wife "giving hers to the little one since November" (`q_ned:40`).

**Proposals.** The Quota's six questions, restated for the plain:
1. Who writes the number, and who eats it?
2. Is the lie that keeps your post better than the truth that costs your neighbour's bread?
3. Do you blame the clerk or the quota?
4. When a crowd breaks a door to eat, whose door is it?
5. Who counts as from here?
6. What does a town owe the dead it did not write down?

## 18. Tone

**What exists.** Flat, cold and exact, with dry humour from habit: Edie's "That's off somebody's cart.
I'm not daft." (`q_dinner:124`); Will's "Holt'll love that" (`q_will:73`); Mary's "He can't
reach. There." (`q_dinner:21`). The narration never comments.

**Gaps.** The slice has no joy, so its grief has nothing to fall from. Tomas has "a laugh that makes other
people laugh" (`q_will:93`), and the player only hears about it.

**Proposals.** One scene of ordinary pleasure per era, before its hinge, written as carefully as the
grief: era 2, the Sheaf on day 40 with Tomas, sent to fetch Ilo home, making the back table laugh from the bench by the door; era 5, the Saro kitchen with
Danil burning the bread. The Decay rules already ask for "somebody laughing outside the shuttered shop"
(PITCHES, Decay, failure modes).
