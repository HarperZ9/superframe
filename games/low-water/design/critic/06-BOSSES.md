# Critic 6: the arena as the self's monsters, attacked

Part of [../CRITIC.md](../CRITIC.md). The author's idea, 2026-10-22, verbatim as relayed by the
coordinator: "What if the bossfights and arena can be the mettle in which the primary character can
improve their skills or dispositions and character flaws; and the enemies/bosses are monstrous
iterations and metamorphosis' of self in these terrible amalgamations of the internal flaws."

Everything here is **PROPOSAL**.

## 1. What the idea gets right

The arena is the weakest-tied system in the design: eight archetypes and two bosses built
content-neutral and reskinned (`design/quota/05-ARENA.md:4-9`). Making the fights about the character
gives them a reason to exist in a story about people. Silent Hill 2 proves the form can carry guilt:
the executioner exists because James wants to be punished (file 02, entry 5).

## 2. Where it slides

1. **Into self-help.** "Improve their skills or dispositions and character flaws" is a growth arc. Beat
   your flaw, level up, become better. In a persecution story, the protagonist's growth is paid for by
   the persecuted. The Hesh become the gym where an inspector works on himself.
2. **Into a therapy arc.** Flaws as monsters invite the language of facing your demons. Defeating the
   embodiment of a flaw says the flaw can be defeated. The game's own rules say otherwise: "Honesty is
   not the good route" (`design/PITCHES.md:1221`, as cited at `design/STORY-ASSESSMENT.md:191`), and
   pillar 4 forbids a redemption discount (`design/literary/06-ALLEGORY-AUDIT.md:86-87`).
3. **Into the psychologising of atrocity.** If the evil is inner monsters, the genocide becomes a story
   about the perpetrator's psyche. The requisition, the list and the notices are social machines; the
   game's best scenes show ordinary people with small reasons inside them. Inner monsters move the cause
   from the machine to the soul. The author's environment-attribution rule rejects that move for AI
   (harm explained by the incentive structure, never by inner intent); the same test applies here.
4. **Into a reward economy for virtue.** If fighting improves dispositions, the player farms the arena
   to become kinder. The design already has the better ethic: "Every loaf won is one a named family
   didn't get; the board by the gate chalks whose" (`05-ARENA.md:14`). The arena's improvement is paid
   by named people. That line is stronger than the new idea, and the new idea should be bent to it.
5. **Into monsters that look like the victims.** Amalgamations and metamorphoses drift toward bodies.
   The surreal rule "Never a people" (`design/sensory/03-SURREAL.md:38`) must bind every boss: no boss
   is drawn as Hesh, as a child, or as a starving body.

## 3. What would make it land like Silent Hill 2 or Spec Ops

Five rules for any boss of the self:

1. **The boss is the self-lie, and winning proves it.** The fight's victory condition is the act the lie
   excused. The player wins by doing it again.
2. **The boss's moves are the character's own verbs.** The pen, the bar, the hand, the arm. The player
   attacks with them.
3. **Nothing improves that should not.** Skills may improve: the player gets better at the harmful verb.
   No disposition improves. Flaws are not fixed by fighting.
4. **The fight ends at a table.** No level-up screen. The next scene is a meal, and the cost is in a bowl.
5. **Two or three fights, not a system.** A self-boss in every bout becomes a genre. Keep the arena's
   bread fights as the material economy, and put the self-bosses only where the story's hinges are.

## 4. Boss concepts from the Quota's self-lies and failing parents

### B1. Received and Found Correct (Ned; SUM's self-lie)
- **Source.** SUM: "The sheet says three-ten. You've signed that the sheet says three-ten. Nobody can say
  you didn't." (`content/quota/q_ned.lwd:71`) and "You only add up what they send." (`q_office.lwd:31`).
- **Form.** A clerk's body built of stacked return sheets, with many hands each holding the good pen.
  It throws figures that land as weights on the floor.
- **Mechanism.** The only attack button is the signature stroke. Each hit signs a sheet. When it falls,
  its last sheet reads the district total the player reported, and the room applauds, one man first and
  two more after him (`q_office.lwd:12`). Putting the pen down is possible. Then the fight does not end,
  the clock runs in the room, and at two o'clock the fight breaks off on its own: Tomas has waited at the
  post road (`q_ned.lwd:130`) for nobody.
- **What winning costs.** The win is the signature. The fight proves SUM right: nobody can say he did not
  sign.

### B2. Friday (Will; PRIDE's self-lie, and the failing father)
- **Source.** PRIDE: "You've never once lifted a hand to that boy. Not once. Your father did it every
  Friday and you swore." (`content/quota/q_dinner.lwd:191`). "Davey's shoulders come up round his ears
  and stay there until the hand has gone back." (`q_dinner.lwd:190`).
- **Form.** Will's father as Will remembers him: a big man with a belt, in the Brack kitchen, the room
  stretched tall. The boss is never shown with a face; it has Will's coat on.
- **Mechanism.** Will fights his father with his hands. Every landed blow plays one sound from the room
  off screen: a boy breathing in sharply. The boss's tells are Davey's shoulders, drawn on the wall as a
  shadow; the player learns to read the shadow to time the hits. When the father falls, the arena
  dissolves to the kitchen table, and Davey's shoulders are up round his ears.
- **What winning costs.** The player has lifted a hand, many times, and learned to read a child's fear
  as timing. The lie is proved false by winning.

### B3. Forty Carts (Will; BALLAST's self-lie)
- **Source.** BALLAST: "If one cart goes through without a pass, forty go tomorrow, and then there's no
  post. There's just you on a road." (`content/quota/q_will.lwd:63`).
- **Form.** The order on the nail made large: a typed sheet in the Captain's green ink, armoured, at the
  post. It summons carts, more and more, a swarm from the dip in the road, drawn as carts only, never as
  people (the surreal break "The crowd that repeats", `03-SURREAL.md:51`, applied to objects).
- **Mechanism.** The player holds the pole against forty carts. Holding wins.
- **What winning costs.** When the last cart turns, the swarm resolves into one cart on the road: a thin
  horse, a small boy at its head, a woman on the bench, a girl standing in the back counting. It was
  always one. The player has fought forty and turned back the Haldanes.

### B4. The Good Hat (Ned; Abel, the failing father)
- **Source.** Abel: "You let him decide what he heard." (`q_dinner.lwd:55`). The hat "been in more
  offices than you have" (`q_dinner.lwd:55`).
- **Form.** The exchange steps, and a figure made of courtesy: a bowing coat under Abel's hat.
- **Mechanism.** It cannot be damaged by hitting. Every strike the player lands is turned into a bow, and
  bowing heals it. The only damage is done by standing still and taking its blows without bowing, which
  costs health. The player can win only by losing health on purpose, or quit.
- **What winning costs.** A win leaves the player character hurt for the next fight and changes nothing
  at the Furrowe table: Abel says "Eat that." (`q_dinner.lwd:114`). A parent's teaching is not beaten in
  a fight. A quit, by bowing out, returns the post, the good pen and full bowls.

### B5. One to reject: Bet's coat
- **Source.** Bet's mercy-lie, "Say it's the Orme lad." (`content/quota/q_perspective.lwd:62`).
- **Why not.** A victim's self-protection made into a monster for the player to beat would turn her grief
  into an obstacle and the persecuted mother into the game's enemy. The victims' lies stay in scenes.

## 5. Recommendation

Adopt the idea at the hinges only, under the five rules: B1 for the inspector at the district office,
B2 and B3 for the militia line around the post and the kitchen, and B4 as the optional fight that cannot
be won cleanly. Keep the bread fights' material economy as the arena's spine, so that any gain the
character makes in the ring is paid by a named family. Reject the growth arc: nobody improves their
disposition by fighting, and the game should say so through what winning does.
