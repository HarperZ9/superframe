# The author's references, 22 October 2026

The author sent 77 images without comment. They are the author's own eye, and design comes from them rather than from a model's defaults. The image files sit next to this note. They are gitignored, because they are other people's work kept for private study and never shipped. Everything taken from them is method: light, palette, composition, technique. No image, element or likeness goes into the game.

First read by the coordinator on 2026-10-22 over the first 40 images. Rewritten the same day by the art director's archivist after opening all 77 files: every number below matches `CATALOGUE.md`, which describes each file and its attribution. The first pass cited numbers assigned before the files were copied, and about a third of its citations pointed at the wrong file. The corrections are listed at the end.

## What the set says as a whole

1. **One hard light against a dark or saturated field.**
   - Sodium light on one end of a mobile home under a storm cloud (18).
   - A shotgun house lit frontally under a magenta sky (13).
   - Boarded and memorial architecture in raking light against red, slate or charcoal skies (11, 12, 14, 19).
   - Van der Neer's moon on a river (31 to 33).
   - Bierstadt's campfire under the moon (38).
   - A lone lit cabin in a black forest (69).
   - Sodium streetlights in fog and snow (63, 64).

   The figure is small and the world is large. The light picks out one thing, and the dark holds everything else.
2. **Faith set against industry and capital.**
   - A crucifix against a smokestack plume (19).
   - Cemetery crosses against office towers (11).
   - The Calvary of three crosses over a society tomb (14).
   - A refinery under star-filter glare (17).
   - A billboard reading "Stop masking pain. Find the root cause!" seen through a car window (20).

   This is the Furrow Church and the Crown against Halvard's export train, said in images. The billboard is the theme stated by the world itself: a public notice, as in the discourse mechanic.
3. **Vernacular houses as witnesses.**
   - A shotgun house under a magenta sky (13).
   - A boarded-up Italianate house under a red sky (12).
   - A mobile home (18).
   - White cottages under mountain and moor (39, 42).
   - A white gabled house with one tree and one window of flowers (04).

   Dispossession and the empty house: the Haldane barns, a Hesh house after the removal, Anni's kitchen.
4. **The sublime and the infernal.**
   - John Martin's mezzotints (22 to 24): Pandemonium, the Creation of Light, the angel on the stair.
   - Schinkel's temple in a cave under a starfield (62): stage architecture framed by raw rock.
   - A great egret flaring its plumes over a heron under a storm sky (47, © Bence Máté): the sublime found in a marsh, lit by flash.

   The Host and the inner arena belong here: vast architecture, a single shaft of light, tiny figures, black velvet tone.
5. **Battle as chaos and paint.**
   - Trafalgar four ways (26 to 28, 30): Wyllie's holed sails, Turner's drowning foreground, Dawson's clean line, and an unattributed wreck plate with rigging torn across the frame.
   - West's Death of Nelson (29).

   Smoke, torn sails, drowning men in the foreground, and the dying man made into a pietà. This is the intensity and the staging the arena and the granary fight should aim at.
6. **Print, halftone, glitch, scan line.**
   - The printer test page scribbled with marker (25).
   - Four glitch photographs: dot-screen sun over a torii (34), Fuji in interlace ripples (35), a walker in a torn crossing (36), a house dissolving into red and blue streaks (37).
   - Pixel art: a winter park under a patterned dome at sunset (74), one limited palette carrying warm sky and cold snow.

   This matches the design canon's scanline and halftone technique and raw-native's dither engine. It is the look for propaganda, records, memory and the surreal breaks.
7. **Land and weather.**
   - Glen Coe and Glenfinnan (39, 40, 41, 42): ochre bracken, wet green, low cloud, one white house, a steam train's plume.
   - The swamp under the moon (10) and the flash-lit swamp with a pale figure (21).
   - Haze over mountains under a white disc (15), rays through cloud (43), layered blue ranges (45), a storm wall over a switchback road (46), a fogged mountain road at dusk (44).
   - A long exposure with violet-lit desert stems (16).
   - The Dolomites on colour film (56 to 59): warm rock, flat blue sky, people small against spires.

   This is the palette of the plain and the hills, and the uncanny of the night outdoors.
8. **The coast at the edge of the day.** A thread the first pass did not have.
   - Japanese harbour towns reached down stone stairs (65, 68), seen from above (67) and from a pavilion (66).
   - Blue-hour seawalls, bridges, hillsides and car parks (48 to 51, 53, 54).
   - A lone walker on an orange sea edge with a city behind (55).

   Wires cross most of these frames. The town is read from a height, down a stair, toward water. That is Lamp Quay's geography and the mole steps.
9. **Ruin, growth and grace.**
   - Flint ruin arches taken back by ivy and trees, with a lens flare left in (75, 77).
   - A white dove in a green canopy (76).
   - Schinkel's beech grove at morning (61): backlit trees, children on the path, a town and sea in haze.

   The green and lit counterweight to the night set. Light coming through leaves, not falling on stone.
10. **Interiors drawn as plans of power.**
    - An engraved octagonal hall with a trophy column over a fireplace (60).
    - A mountain hut with a red tablecloth and the spires outside the window (58).
11. **Making the picture.**
    - Blockout to finished render of one city scene (05, 06).
    - Flat value painting to finished painting (07, 08).
    - Wind-animated trees, a three-frame post (01 to 03).
    - The white house with dappled shadow (04).
    - A tower against a single black cloud (09).
    - A tech-demo pause menu with torn-paper buttons over a lit grassland (52).

    Shapes and values first, then render. Leaves that move. Shadow as a graphic shape. A menu that belongs to the world's material.
12. **The pre-rendered map.**
    - Four late-1990s CRPG area maps, pre-rendered and seen from a high isometric angle (70 to 73): a walled harbour city, a domed port, a river village, a treetop city.

    Every building is legible as a shape from above. Streets, walls and water organise the whole place into one readable picture. See "Camera and map" below.

## Where it lands in Low Water (proposals for the art lead and the sensory looks)

- **Night is van der Neer, not black.** Moonlit cloud carries the light. Water reflects a broken band. Land sits in warm brown-black. Era 2's winter nights at the post, the granary and the river use this (31 to 33). The current plates read flat navy; see the browser stills of 2026-10-22.
- **One source per scene, hard and warm, against cold dark.** The lantern, the brazier, the sodium lamp at the office door. A figure lit by one source reads, as in 13, 18, 21 and 38. This answers the black-silhouette defect in the returns room: give the figures the room's key light, not ambient.
- **Sodium in weather.** 63 and 64 show what sodium does in fog and snow: the light becomes a volume and the snow takes its colour. The quay's fog nights and the frost on the mole are the first cases.
- **Places as witnesses.** Each house plate gets the treatment of 13, 18, 39 and 42: frontal or distant, still, one light, the sky doing the emotion. Anni's kitchen at year 38 and the empty Haldane house in a later era are the first candidates.
- **Faith against industry in the skyline.** The Furrow Church's crosses and Halvard's chimney in one frame, as in 19 and 11, at the exchange square and the granary.
- **The arena and the Host are John Martin.** Black-velvet mezzotint tone, colossal architecture, one shaft of light, tiny figures. The inner arena's look in SENSORY 01-LOOKS should cite 22 to 24. Schinkel's cave temple (62) is the model for a built thing seen through raw rock.
- **The granary fight and the inner arena are staged like Trafalgar.**
  - Smoke as a compositional mass that hides and reveals (26 to 28).
  - Debris and rigging (30).
  - The dying arranged into an unbearable tableau after West (29). Use it once, with care.
- **Print-process looks for propaganda, records and memory.** Halftone, CMYK misregistration and marker over print (25, 34 to 37). The notices, the census page, the read-back's witness tin and surreal breaks. Pixel art (74) is a candidate look for a memory or a child's view.
- **The hills and the plain are Glencoe.** Ochre bracken, mist that eats the top of the frame, a single white house (39 to 42): the Tesks' fold, the road to the hills Tomas did not come home from. The steam plume of 41 is the export train's look in open country.
- **The flash in the swamp (21)** is the look for the cells' night, or the dream's premonition: a hard frontal light on a pale body in a dark, wet place. The flash-lit egret (47) shows the same light making a creature monumental.
- **Down the stairs to the water.** The harbour stairs (65, 68) frame the walk from the town down to the quay: steep descent, wires overhead, water as the goal of the frame.
- **The green counter-look.** The ruin and canopy images (61, 75 to 77) give the daylight and memory scenes a look that is not night: backlit leaves, stone taken back by growth, one white bird.
- **Menus belong to the world.** The torn-paper buttons of 52 point at a pause and title screen cut from the game's own paper: a notice, a ledger page.

## Where it lands in raw-native (engine tasks, added to the build queue)

1. **Vegetation with wind (01 to 03).** A foliage system with hierarchical wind (trunk sway, branch, leaf flutter), driven by the game's wind state. Bounds: stable at 60 fps on the Quota scenes. The Oil and Ink pass must not shimmer under leaf motion; reuse the temporal-instability metric.
2. **Moonlit night model (31 to 33, 38).** Cloud-lit sky radiance with a moon disc and silver linings on the Preetham and cloud stack, plus water reflection of a broken moon band. CPU reference first.
3. **Halftone, misregistration and scan-line looks (25, 34 to 37).** Built on the dither engine as a style-state look, which needs P22 from SENSORY. Includes CMYK channel offset, a dot screen at an angle, and interlace tearing. A limited-palette pixel mode (74) is a variant of the same quantiser.
4. **Star-filter and spectral glare for hard sources (17).** A diffraction-spike kernel on bright points, in the bloom pass. The lens-flare ghost of 75 is a second, optional kernel.
5. **Volumetric shaft of light (23, 24, 43).** A single directional god-ray through cloud for the Host's movement, from the clouds stage 1 volume.
6. **Smoke as a staging mass (26 to 30).** Gunpowder and brazier smoke from the clouds and smoke stage on the GPU, lit by flashes. Steam from the export train (41) uses the same stage.
7. **Light in participating media (63, 64).** Sodium point lights scattering in fog and lighting falling snow, from the same volume as task 5.
8. **Leaf transmission (75 to 77).** Backlit foliage shows its light through the leaf. A thin translucency term on the foliage material of task 1.
9. **Plate bake at a fixed high angle (70 to 73).** See the camera proposal below. The bake tools already take a camera, so the engine side is a camera preset and a test.

## Camera and map (proposal for the author)

Status: proposal. Not decided. The camera decision of 2026-10-17 (`design/CAMERA.md`) stands until the author rules.

What the isometric references show (70 to 73). A whole place is pre-rendered from one high, fixed angle. Every roof, wall, street and shoreline reads as a shape. The player learns the town as one picture, and the same picture can serve as the map.

What Low Water already has. Painted plates, an orthographic camera snapped to plate pixels, plates re-baked by tools that take a camera, and three pull-back moments where the view eases out to the whole plate.

The proposal, in three parts:

1. **Bake the exterior plates from a fixed three-quarter angle.** Choose one elevation (somewhere between the plates' present view and the steep isometric of 70 to 73) and use it for every exterior plate. Interiors keep their own framing. One angle across the town makes adjacent plates join and makes the town learnable.
2. **Bake a whole-town map plate from the same angle.** Lamp Quay and its neighbours as one pre-rendered picture in the Oil and Ink look, in the manner of 70 to 72. The pull-back moments ease out toward it, and the pause or map screen shows it. Visited places come up out of a darker, unvisited ground.
3. **Keep the close camera for people.** The 2.25 times closer framing for faces and fights is unchanged. The map angle governs the bake and the pull-back, not the play distance.

Tests before deciding. Bake Lamp Quay at two candidate elevations and at the present angle. Compare at the 1080-line target: does a person still read at 130 to 160 px; do the mole steps and the water front stay legible for a Tide Run; does the painted brushwork survive the steeper view. Then show the author the three stills.

Costs. Every exterior plate is re-baked. A steeper angle shows more roof and less wall, which changes what the plate painter paints. A map plate is a large new painting.

## Does not prove

- The reading of the author's intent is the coordinator's and the archivist's. The author did not caption the images.
- Image numbers follow the files in this folder, 01 to 77. They were checked against each file on 2026-10-22.
- Attributions marked moderate in `CATALOGUE.md` rest on resemblance to known works, not on a signature. Check them before citing outside this folder.
- The camera proposal is untested. No plate has been baked at a new angle.
