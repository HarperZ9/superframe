# Voice calibration, 2026-10-21 (lead, for the author's reaction)

Author's verdict on VOICE-SAMPLES: not visceral, not gritty, not heavy enough. Wanted: True Detective
seasons 1 and 3 (the recorded interview, memory across years, confession, the guilt a man carries
into old age); the Fielder and Oppenheim film You Can See Everything (a person living inside a lie
they built and half-believe, reality performed for a camera); writing that makes the player feel
they need to wash the guilt off.

Diagnosis: the samples moved from slogans to civic detail (wages, coke bills). Both stay outside the
person. Grit lives inside: the one thing they did, the body, the specific memory they can't put
down, the lie they tell themselves every morning.

## Sample: the tape (dawn, the back room of the Brine)

GORM: Tape's running. Say your name for it.

LAMB: Lamb.

GORM: First name.

LAMB: Just Lamb.

GORM: Three nights you've been in Gullet. Tell me about the first one.

LAMB: I've told you about the first one.

GORM: Tell me the bit you left out.

(The tide is at the door. You can hear it under the floor.)

LAMB: There's a ferry used to run out of Hesk. The Margaret. Eleven years ago she went down off the
point with nine aboard. I did the assessment. I was drinking then. A lot. The owner bought me
dinner on the Tuesday and my report came out on the Friday and it said weather. It said nobody
could've known.

GORM: Could somebody have known?

LAMB: The pumps were rotten. I put my hand straight through the housing on one of them. I remember
the rust on my cuff. I remember thinking, I'll have to get that cleaned.

GORM: Did you?

LAMB: Couple of days later. Yeah.

(Gorm writes something. She doesn't look up.)

LAMB: One of them was fourteen. The cook's lad. His mother wrote to the company and they sent her
my report, because that's what you do, you send them the report. Then she wrote to me. I've got the
letter. It's in my case, the inside pocket. I've carried it to every job for eleven years and I've
never opened it.

GORM: Why not?

LAMB: You know why not.

GORM: Say it for the tape.

LAMB: Because I quit drinking the week it came, and everyone I've ever met has shaken my hand for
that. And if I open it, I'm not the man who got sober. I'm the man who put his hand through a pump
and wrote "weather".

(Long pause. The tape hisses.)

GORM: There's a woman up on the hill. Wenna. Sat in that lighthouse six years without wages. You
wrote her up last night as "absent from post".

LAMB: She was absent from post.

GORM: She was at the Brine, by the stove. Nobody's paid for her coal since the spring. She's sat by Sabine's fire every night since March, because it's the only warm room she can afford.

LAMB: I didn't know that.

GORM: You didn't ask.

## What this sample is testing
- Can the player feel Lamb's guilt in the body (rust on the cuff, the unopened letter) instead of
  being told about it?
- The interview frame (True Detective): a recorder, a patient questioner, the past arriving in
  pieces, and the present crime echoing the old one.
- The lie he lives (You Can See Everything): the sober-man story everyone praises is the thing
  that lets him not read the letter.
- The game's choice point: does Lamb open the letter? The player decides, and it costs.


Corrections (lead, 2026-10-21): first draft broke canon in three places (Gorm is Sergeant Ida Gorm; Lamb's first name and gender stay open; on Low Night Wenna was by the Brine's stove). Fixed above.

## Sample v2: pushed harder (author: "Closer, push harder")

GORM: The clinic. Hesk. The one you went to.

LAMB: Saint Agnes.

GORM: Six weeks, it says here. Private room. Who paid?

LAMB: I did.

GORM: On an assessor's wage.

(Lamb doesn't answer. Somewhere under the floor the water finds a new way in.)

LAMB: He gave me an envelope at the dinner. The owner. I didn't open that one either. I'm good at
not opening things. I put it in the drawer with my socks and I drank for another month, and then
the letter came, and I took the envelope out and I counted it on the bed. Four hundred and twenty
pounds. Saint Agnes was four hundred.

GORM: And the twenty?

LAMB: A bottle for the train. Last one. I drank it looking out the window and I cried most of the
way, and a woman across from me gave me her handkerchief. I've still got that too.

(Gorm stops writing.)

LAMB: Eleven years. People clap at the meetings. They give you a coin. Every one of those coins, I
bought with that boy. Every morning I don't drink, I'm spending him. You want to know why I'm good
at this job, sergeant? Because I know exactly what a man looks like when he's sitting on the thing
that paid for his life. I see it across a table before they've sat down. I see it in the mirror
when I shave.

GORM: Then why come here?

LAMB: They sent me.

GORM: That's not what I asked.

(The voices, low, all at once:)
THE PEN: Tell her the truth.
HEARSAY: Tell her a better one.
THE NOSE: She already knows. Look at her hands.

(Choice: open the letter now, on the tape / say "I came to find someone worse than me" / say nothing)

## v3 attempt at the beat the author called weak ("hand through a pump... wrote weather")
Author: the summary line is weak, there needs to be much more self-deprecation, it should feel
personal and human.

GORM: Say why, for the tape.

LAMB: Because people shake my hand. That's why. I quit the week it came and people have been
shaking my hand ever since. And I say thanks. I say one day at a time. I've got the whole bit, I'm
very good at the bit. There's a lad at my meeting, nineteen, calls me his sponsor. Rings me at two
in the morning when he's got a can in his hand and I talk him down. Me. I talk him down.
(He laughs, and it's not a laugh. Gorm waits. The tape hisses.)

GORM: And the letter?

LAMB: Inside pocket.

Revision (lead): cut the last two v3 lines ("coward with a nice coat", "dining out"); they were summaries. Ends on the laugh and the pocket.
