# Low Water: the rework plan

From the director, 2026-10-17. Private. Written before any line is rewritten.

The author, 2026-10-17 (verbatim): "Second water is approved, but I would like to go back to Act 1
and Act 2 and ensure that everything is more fleshed out and that there's different ways for the
main playable character to interact with the environment and to get through with different
playthroughs, like one combat based, one charisma based, like an RPG, stealth based, utility
based, etc., etc. And then between the acts, the daylight scene should be playable, and less focus
on word count and more impact on the impact and the brevity of what's being told in the story and
the writing. Really deliver something impactful in the writing, and make sure that you use
articulate as well, and that you plan it out well, and that the story is good, and that the combat
is good, and, you know, come on, let's really dig into it."

What this settles: Act 2 is approved. The daylight interlude is playable. Word count is no longer
a target anywhere; the measure is what a line does, and when two versions do the same thing the
shorter one wins. Still open: whether strangers from Hesk are Act 2's opponents. Act 2 is designed
so either answer works (section 6.4).

## 1. The story

### 1.1 The spine of the three nights

Gullet put its own light out by a chain of small decisions, each defensible on its own. Lamb is
sent to name the cause, and finds that naming is itself an act: whatever Lamb writes becomes a
fact the town has to live under. The three nights are three questions about that act.

| Night | Question | What Lamb learns | How far into the water |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Low Night | Whose fault is the dark? | The truth is shared, and a verdict is a choice about who carries it | Lamb walks the floor of the sea and comes back wet |
| 2. Second Water | Who pays? | The word Lamb wrote is enforced by people who believe the Shelf exists, and the Shelf may be one person on the Ropewalk | Lamb goes out by boat, past where anyone can walk |
| 3. The Gale | What will the town live with? | There is no verdict that leaves Lamb dry | Lamb goes inside the Old Light, under the sea |

The personal line under all three: Lamb has been dry for eleven years "as a matter of professional
pride". Dryness is distance. Each night the town takes a little more of it. Lamb never gets an arc
of redemption; Lamb gets wet, and chooses what to do about it. The four fates of Lamb at the end
(the morning boat, keeper of Mother, the Shelf's typewriter, the Brine's tab) are four answers to
"will you go back to being dry?"

### 1.2 Each act's turn

- **Act 1.** Turn one, midnight: Lamb's provisional word goes up the tube and comes back as rumour.
  From then on Lamb is a party, not a witness. Turn two, 04:50: the claimant climbs to destroy the
  evidence with a hammer, and the cover-up becomes something physical in a frozen room. The act
  ends when Lamb sends the cause and sees the town in colour for one minute.
- **Act 2.** Turn one, the evening boat: the engineer who wrote the restrike clause arrives and
  says he wrote it so that nobody would ever be able to say no one was at the panel. Crake struck
  it. Turn two, the second low water: the ships stranded on Night 1 are being stripped, and the
  people stripping them are the wreckers the claim invented, made real by the claim (section 6.4).
  Turn three, the Ropewalk: the Shelf's machine with the sticking e is in a back room in Gullet.
  The act ends with the choice of which lamps the town lights for the gale.
- **Act 3.** Turn one, the eye of the storm: forty minutes when a boat can reach the inside of
  the Old Light. Turn two, inside: the place where the low light burned, still never explained.
  The final verdict, the fates of Mother and of Lamb, and the next night shown as the town's lights.

### 1.3 Where Act 1 sags or explains itself

Read across the edited act with Articulate's findings beside it (section 8):

1. **Movement II has no engine.** From 00:40 to 03:30 the act is a list of scheduled scenes
   (the Gut opens, the Wet Hour, Crake rings) with no question pulling the player through them.
   Fix: make the 02:50 blackout **the town's appointment**. Everyone who has a reason to want
   thirty minutes of dark has planned for it, the way Keel planned for the vote: the Low Company
   to take Merrow out of the cells (if Lamb wrote wreckers), the Guild to hold a second vote by
   smell on the provisional cause (if Lamb wrote the Guild), Crake's Lamplighters to search the
   Brine (if Lamb wrote the Trust), Sabine to fire the oven for the last time (if Lamb wrote the
   keeper), Keel to go up the mole early (if the sea, or nothing). The Glass and HEARSAY tell Lamb
   from 01:00 that something is planned for when the lights go. Movement II becomes a countdown
   to a dark Lamb can use, prevent or join.
2. **Instruments and voices answer too much.** Several scenes have an instrument state the
   inference (the pink lamps, the restrike timing) before the player has made it. The rework
   lets the instrument read and lets Lamb conclude: the Kit gives numbers and temperament; the
   conclusion is a player choice or a pencil check.
3. **The thesis is spoken.** "The town is honest in the dark" is said by four people and two
   voices. Keep it once, as the Margin note's title, and let the scenes show it.
4. **Narration explains after the image.** The edit pass cut most of these; the rewrite removes
   the rest. Articulate's corrective-negation and contrast-pair findings (32 of 79) sit mostly
   here: "not X, but Y" lines that tell the reader what the image already showed.
5. **The endings report.** Each dawn ending narrates consequences in a paragraph. Rework: one
   person, one image, one line of the Shelf. The epilogue lists stay (they are the town's lights)
   but shorter.
6. **Merrow and Wenna carry the act's heart and are reached late.** Most players meet Wenna in
   the Brine early and then leave her by the stove. Give her one scene that moves (the climb, or
   the refusal at the bottom of the stairs) in every play.
7. **Crake tells her own lie too easily on the telephone.** Make her harder to catch: she tells
   it, and only the yellow ink, the engineer (Act 2) or a recorded slip in the Record catches her.

### 1.4 The moments the game hangs on

These are protected in every rewrite. Each must land in two lines or fewer at its peak.

1. **The low light from the deck.** Lamb's own memory is the first evidence, and it is four
   minutes wrong.
2. **Keel quoting Lamb back**, with the ending changed ("it turned us" filed as "it lured us").
   The Record bites for the first time.
3. **Wenna at the bottom of the hundred and twelve stairs**, climbing or not.
4. **Merrow in the drowned lamp**: the five minutes nothing of his was burning.
5. **The midnight tube**: Lamb's first act on the town.
6. **The second blackout**: the town's appointment, and what Lamb does in the dark.
7. **Keel and the hammer on the frost**, and the choice to let him break only the green rings.
8. **Dawn**: the one minute of colour; the cause goes up.
9. **The daylight interlude**: Gullet in colour, and one conversation that cannot happen in the dark.
10. **Act 2: the sticking e** heard through a wall on the Ropewalk.
11. **Act 3: inside the Old Light** in the storm's eye.

## 2. Play styles

Five styles, each built from the existing compartments, voices, the Kit and the Margin. A Lamb can
mix them; a pure build of each can finish both acts.

| Style | What it is | Compartment and voices | Kit and tools | Margin notes that feed it | Pays in |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Fist** (combat) | Through people, physically: holds ground, carries, forces, fights | COAT high: GRIP, BALLAST, KNEES, PRIDE | Boathook, pole, hammer, the case as a shield; moves taught by people | m.body, m.poles, m.level | Standing, Cold, tools broken, favour, `book.violence` |
| **Tongue** (talk) | Through people, socially: condolence, bluff, bargains, rumour | HAT high: CONDOLENCE, FORM LETTER, THE CHIT, HEARSAY | Chits, the Tab, guises | m.owed, m.flatfish, m.grace, m.watched | Favour, chits, the Record (what Lamb said is quoted back) |
| **Shadow** (stealth) | Around people: dark, fog, crowds, smell, waiting | NOSE high: THE DARK, UNDERTOW, SMELL; BALLAST | Lantern discipline (doused, shuttered), bell jar and blindfold, the Lamplighter coat | m.dark, m.small, m.downwind | Time, Dread, the risk of being seen (a blown guise, Gorm's book) |
| **Hands** (tools and craft) | Through the world: the tide, the switch, ropes, lamps, salvage, rigging | COAT and PEN: GRIP, SUM, CARBON | The Kit's fittings, salvage, rope, the boathook, the quay switch, lamps | m.copper, m.stairs, m.colour, m.level | Salvage, time at the bench, the town's lamps (visible consequences) |

Investigation earns its place as a fifth style because the game is an inquiry: an Eye Lamb reaches
the same facts as the others by reading instead of entering, and the town barely notices.

**Rules that make styles real.**
- Every main obstacle has at least three routes that differ in play (section 3). A route is not a
  different check on the same option: it is a different thing to do in the world.
- Every verdict-critical fact has at least three ways in, and the dawn verdict accepts equivalent
  facts (section 3.3): the same case can be made from what Lamb saw, what Lamb was told, or what
  Lamb worked out.
- A route chosen leaves a mark: Fist routes leave bruises and `struck.*`; Tongue routes leave
  `said.*` that people quote; Shadow routes leave nothing unless Lamb is seen; Hands routes change
  the town (a lamp lit, a door barred, a sack sunk with a cork on it); Eye routes cost time and
  Wear.
- Failure stays interesting in every style: a failed sneak is a conversation in bad light; a failed
  talk becomes a fight or a chase or a debt; a failed fight is a Down scene; a failed rig floods,
  breaks or lights the wrong thing; a failed inference is a wrong fact Lamb believes (marked in
  the Daybook in pencil) that someone can later correct.

### 2.1 A pure-talk run and a pure-fight run to the dawn

**Tongue only.** Keel's key by favour (the Board reads Lamb as a cheque); the works order read in
the office. Marrow's admission in the dark, asked as a confession, not a charge. Sabine's slate by
paying Wenna's rounds; the oven by buying a hot loaf. Fennick lends relic 113 for Lamb's word.
Merrow tells the five minutes because Lamb kept his copper quiet. Wenna climbs because Lamb asked
the right way. At 04:50 Lamb talks Keel into breaking only the green rings. Verdict possible:
any, including a divided cause. Consequence at dawn: the town trusts Lamb and has told Lamb too
much; every faction expects something back on Night 2; the Record is full of promises.

**Fist only.** The office door forced (the Lamplighters come; a pole fight; Lamb wins or goes
down in the cells and is let out at 01:00 with the works order in hand because Hobb pities the
assessor). The after-vote turns and Lamb fights blindfolded and wins; the Guild reads Lamb as a
nose and Marrow tells the switch to someone who fought fair in the dark. The salvors fight for the
copper; Lamb takes the sack and Merrow, beaten, tells the five minutes "so it's written by
somebody who earned it". The oven prism lifted out hot (GRIP, Cold, a burn). Wenna carried up the
stairs. Keel fought on the ice for the hammer. Verdict possible: Guild, Trust and Board, keeper,
divided. Consequence: the Lamplighters' book is full of violence; the Trust reads Lamb as an
enemy; on Night 2 the Lamplighters come in fours and the Low Company respects Lamb; nobody tells
Lamb anything unasked.

## 5. The daylight interlude: "Two in the Afternoon"

Playable, short and sharp: about twenty to thirty minutes of play, in daylight, with no checks and
no meters, and its own small choices that set Act 2.

**Situation.** Lamb wakes at two in the afternoon above the Brine. The town is in colour: oxblood,
mustard, teal, bone. The tide is out again, ordinary and grey. The evening boat comes at six. Lamb
has until four, when the light starts to go, and time for **two** of the four afternoon calls,
chosen on the quay. Sleep is a fifth choice: Lamb can go back to bed and start Night 2 rested
(Wear 0) but knowing nothing new.

| Call | Where | What happens | Sets in Act 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wenna | The mole, in daylight | She shows Lamb Mother's lens in sunlight: the green rings plain for anyone to see, and nobody looking. She says nothing about the night; she points | `day.wenna`: Wenna will climb on Night 2 without being asked; CONDOLENCE +1 with her |
| Pip | The tube terminus | The afternoon canister: a typed message that is not for Lamb, addressed to "the Gullet clerk". The sticking e | `day.pip`: the Ropewalk thread opens early (A9) |
| Merrow, or the cells | The mole steps, or the cells | Mending a net, or behind the grating, depending on the cause. He says what the Low Company will do tonight | `day.merrow`: the Low Company's Night 2 plan known in advance |
| Marrow | The smokehouse | Smoking the strike's fish in daylight, for the first time in thirty years, because the vote is banned or because it carried | `day.marrow`: Guild favour +1, or Spratt's plan known |

**Small choices inside the calls.** What Lamb pins on the wall before going out (one fact, which
the first Act 2 scene quotes); whether Lamb buys a second lantern (Hands and Shadow will want it);
whether Lamb writes to the Shelf by the afternoon tube (Tongue: a line the Shelf answers on Night
2) or to nobody.

**The one image.** At four the sun goes behind the hill and the colours go out of the houses one
by one, from the bottom of the terraces up, like a tide coming in. The quay switch clicks. Night 2.

## 7. Fights that matter

For the combat engineer and the clean-room specs, which carry the feel. This is the encounter
design: when to fight, when not to, what it costs.

1. **A fight is a choice with a price, never the default.** Every encounter has at least one talk,
   sneak or tool route beside it, and the fight's price is visible before it starts: the time it
   takes, the Cold, the tools that can break, the favour it costs, and whether a Lamplighter can
   see it.
2. **Fights pay in access and respect, never in experience.** Winning gets Lamb somewhere, keeps
   something whole, or changes how a faction reads Lamb. Pages never come from fighting.
3. **Set fights are few and earned by the story.** Three in Act 1 (the salvors over the copper, the
   after-vote in the dark, Keel and the hammer), four in Act 2 (the strippers, the Lamplighters'
   station or its talk, the zealots round the Old Bulb if a relic is taken, the frozen gutter).
   Talk changes their shape, not their existence.
4. **The night fights on both sides.** Water, frost, light, wind and smoke shape every fight (the
   Fray, 18.8). The best fight in Act 1 is Keel on his own ice: the floor is the opponent.
5. **Violence escalates through the Record.** Each `book.violence` makes the Lamplighters come in
   larger numbers on Night 2, and makes the Trust's read of Lamb harder to change. A Lamb who has
   fought nobody is met at the station with tea.
6. **Going down is a scene, and a scene is a cost.** The cells, the floor, the gutter, the Brine's
   settle: each takes time, an item and some dignity, and each is remembered.
7. **Principals never die, and nobody dies by default.** A beaten opponent yields, flees or goes
   down, and appears again with a bruise and a grievance.

## 8. The brevity rule and Articulate

**The brevity rule**, in every brief: a line earns its place by changing what the player knows,
what someone thinks of Lamb, what the town looks like, or what Lamb can do next. Of two lines that
do the same thing, the shorter wins. At a moment the game hangs on (1.4), the peak is two lines or
fewer. No scene explains its own image. The thesis is never said aloud.

**Articulate**, local only (`check` and `score`; `judge` only with `--backend none` or host). The
spoken and narrated lines are extracted from each `.lwd` file without markup
(`content/tools/extract_prose.py`) and checked per district (`content/tools/articulate_table.py`,
mode literary-fiction/narrate). Before the rework, Act 1 has 79 high and medium findings across
eight districts; the top rules are contrast pairs (19), substitutions (18), corrective negation
(13) and clichés (11). The aim is fewer of those, as a by-product of better lines; Articulate names
patterns and does not decide whether a line is good. The numbers live in design/STATE.md before
and after.
