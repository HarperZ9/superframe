# The Quota, 1: world bible

Part of [QUOTA-DESIGN.md](../QUOTA-DESIGN.md). Everything here is invented. The research cases behind it
are in PITCHES.md revision 4, section 5, and are never named in the game.

## The country and the plain

- **The Commonwealth of Arl**, capital Arlen, three days east of the plain by rail in era 1 and four hours
  by road in era 5. Its governments across the five eras: the Crown Council (era 1), the People's
  Directorate (eras 2 and 3), the Second Republic (eras 4 and 5).
- **The Tarn**, a wide grain plain under low hills, with one river, the Edd, and eleven villages
  (Krall's End, Osby, Moor Holt, Tilney, Wether, Calder Green, Pike, Upton Tarn, Saltings, Brome, Ness).
- **The Hesh hills** to the north, sheep country, where the Hesh come from.
- **The Vellmark**, two countries east, across the mountains, where a war in era 4 sends the Vell west.
- **The Sund**, a trading federation to the west, home of the Halvard Grain Company.

## Edrin and the Upper Town

Edrin is the plain's market town, on a rise over the Edd. **The Upper Town** is its old heart, the
district the game follows for a hundred and twenty years.

| Place | Era 1 | Era 5 |
|---|---|---|
| Exchange Square | The grain exchange, the scale, the bell tower | A car park, the scale's base under a bench, the bell tower a phone mast |
| Exchange Street | Merchants' houses, five storeys | Flats over shuttered shops |
| Mill Street | Down to the mill race | The stabbing on Tuesday; the clip |
| The Furrow Church | The town's church, full | Half-full; the food bank in the hall |
| The Well House | A Hesh prayer room in a barn yard | A purpose-built Well House, its windows meshed |
| The burial ground | On the hill's shoulder | Its edge moved back for the ring road |
| The mill | The Orme mill, the town's biggest employer | Shut since era 4; the boxing club in its yard |
| The district office | The registrar's, then the Directorate's | The council archive |
| Haldane Yard | Barns for the seasonal hands | The Haldane great-granddaughter's flat |

The Lower Town by the river and the new estates on the far bank grow in eras 3 to 5. The game stays in
the Upper Town except for the villages in era 2 and the hill in era 2's lore.

## The peoples

| | The Tarn | The Hesh | The Vell |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who | The plain's majority: farmers, merchants, clerks; their own poor, the cottars and day hands | Hill herders who come down as seasonal hands and stay | Refugees from the war in the Vellmark, from era 4 |
| Faith | The Furrow Church: the field as the gift, the harvest blessed, sin as waste | The Well: a water faith; daily washing, a shared cup, the dead buried with water | The Deep Well, a stricter branch: headscarves for women, a fast month |
| Language | Tarnish | Heshen (nine words for rain, the Tarn joke goes) | Vellish, with its own script |
| Food | Wheat bread, dripping, beer | Mutton, sheep's-fat bread, sour milk | Millet, peppers, sweet tea |
| Names | Furrowe, Haldane, Krall, Orme, Mott, Brack | Ilo, Tesk, Anni, Dorrah, Sefa | Saro, Danil, Mira, Kasem |
| Dress | Dark wool, flat caps, aprons | Braided hair, felt, a blue thread at the wrist | Bright scarves, embroidered collars |
| Picked on for | (by the town: the cottars' mud, their drinking) | The smell of the fat, braids, the shared cup, "nine words for rain" | Skin a shade darker, the script, the scarves, the fast |

**The slurs, invented** (spoken in full under the slur rule): "mud-hands" for the Tarn poor;
"tallowmen" for the Hesh; "chaffers" for the Vell. Among the Hesh themselves, the town Hesh call the hill
Hesh "goat-Hesh", and both call the Vell "chaffers" by era 4.

**The myths the majority tells,** written in full in play:
- About the Hesh: they hoard grain in holes under the goats; they have no word for "mine"; they breed in
  the winter; they wash their dead in the river and bring the fever; a Hesh will smile and steal.
- About the Vell: they are replacing us; they get the flats first; they won't learn Tarnish; their men
  carry knives; their women are kept; they send the money home.
- About the Tarn poor: they drink the dole; they voted for it; they're too thick to leave.
- From the town's progressives in era 5 about the Furrow faithful and the village Tarn: they're backward,
  inbred, the reason the country is like this.
