## 4. The witness: Mira Saro, year 103

**Where.** The Saro flat on Mill Street, year 103. Mira is 60. **Listener:** Danil (5), her grandson, Teo's
boy, who will be face down in the clip twelve years later (`design/spine-plan/DECISIONS-2026-10-22.md:70`).
**Opening object:** a tea glass from the Vellmark, the one that came in the birdcage's straw.

**The guest, by class.** Inspectors: Ruth (71), for the council archive's oral history. Militia: Ann (63),
retired, come with a young constable about a complaint from the shop below; the constable is Leon. Clerks:
Irene Mott (78), with a housing form. Correspondents: Paul Ashby (68), writing his memoir. Doctors: Hal
Lind (78), visiting the old woman upstairs. Millers: Victor Orme (83), the Orme trust's Christmas visit.

**Reach.** She saw: the station, the birdcage, the sign, the Row on fire, her father on the stairs, Anni's
kitchen. She heard: the Sheaf from Kasem, who came home and sat down without his coat off; the board from
the Gazette. She never knew: who threw the bottle; how the map was drawn. **Omission:** she skips the brother
left at the border (`design/quota/02-HOUSEHOLDS.md:117-118`); the checker records it. **Echo:** CHAFFERS OUT's
line, where Ann heard it, plays as Mira says "and the policewoman wrote it down."

**Last beat, an effect.** Danil asks the Vell word for the river at home. Mira can't find it. She says it
in Tarnish. He says the Tarnish word back to her twice, pleased he can say it.
