# Spine plan 9: Anni, and who her father is

Part of [../SPINE-PLAN.md](../SPINE-PLAN.md). Story lead, 2026-10-22, rewritten the same day after the
father was decided. Sections 1 to 3 are records and canon. Sections 4 to 11 are **PROPOSAL** for the
author; no line here is in content. Samples were read aloud before they were kept.

## 1. The decision, and why

The author, verbatim, 2026-10-22, first: "Out of wedlock." Then, asked who the father is: "You decide,
and expand upon it deeply." The coordinator decided. Canon from that decision:

- **Anni's father is Ned Furrowe.** He was about 27 and already promised to Mary. Bet was a Tarn
  cottar's daughter of about 20, working at the Furrowe farm. Both were adults.
- **Consent under class power stays contested.** Bet's account and Ned's never agree, and the narration
  never rules.
- **Abel Furrowe covered it.** He married Bet to Ilo, the Hesh hand who had worked two summers for him,
  and gave Ilo the Haldane barns tenancy. Edwin Mott entered Ilo as "Haldane, after the farm". Anni's
  father column is blank.
- **Ilo knew, took the bargain and loved Anni anyway.** "Put Haldane" is both love and a deal.
- **Anni is fully Tarn by blood, raised Hesh, and wears the thread.** Her long careful face is Ned's.
- **Pell and Davey are her half-brothers** (as relayed; see the fault below). Ned's falsified barns
  figure starves his own daughter;
  his careful kindness to the Haldanes is guilt. The Tarn list offered to Anni in era 3 is her
  birthright, and taking it means denying Ilo.

**The soft options, and why they were rejected.**
- **Unnamed for good.** A mystery that never closes keeps every man in the story clean. Nobody pays for
  the blank column, and it becomes decoration: a question for the player to enjoy, not a debt anyone owes.
- **Ilo, before the wedding.** That makes the cover-up a love story. The ruled column becomes a clerk's
  oddity, Abel's tenancy becomes generosity, and class power drops out of the Haldane house altogether.
  It also confirms the town's slur ("they breed in the winter", `design/quota/01-WORLD.md:55`).

**Why Ned, against the other hard options.** A nameless Tarn hand spreads the guilt over a class and
lands it on nobody. Will or Holt would be coincidence. Abel would be gothic. Ned is structure: the
Furrowes own the barns, sign the figures and employ the girls who work on the farm, so the same house
holds the land, the pen and the power over a cottar's daughter. The inspector line is the family that
made her paper, and the player plays that family in eras 1, 2 and 3.

**A fault in the canon as relayed.** The canon names "Pell and Davey" as her half-brothers. Davey is
Will and Edie Brack's son, nine, in the slice (`content/quota/q_world.lwd:184-188`,
`content/quota/q_dinner.lwd:126`), and the Furrowe table has one child, Pell
(`content/quota/q_dinner.lwd:16`). Making Davey Ned's would mean Ned fathered Edie's child, which no
one decided. This file treats Pell as her only half-brother on Ned's side and asks the coordinator
(section 12).

## 3. Six accounts, each partial

The past is held by six living people and none holds all of it. Each account leaves something out, and
the leaving out is the character.

**Bet's account.** At 20, to Edie Brack, who let her dresses out that spring, she said almost nothing:
he was kind to her, and she was thirsty, and it was harvest. By era 3 she has a harder sentence for her
daughter: "You didn't say no to a Furrowe. Not on that farm." Both are hers. The second can be heard as
force or as a girl's sense of where she stood; she never says which. What she leaves out: that Ilo was
in the barns that summer. She believes he doesn't know.

**Ned's account.** He has never said it aloud. When he comes near it he remembers the milk: Bet brought
it up to the house every morning that summer and waited while you drank it. In that version it was a
summer, and he was young. What he leaves out: whose farm it was, and what a cottar's daughter could
refuse a Furrowe. His kindness to the Haldanes since is the account he gives with his hands.

**Abel's arrangement.** Abel's account is the town's: he found a girl in trouble a husband and a good
hand a barn, and kept the lad off the hill the winter the rule would have sent him up. Ask anybody who
did better out of that summer. He is pleased with it. "Lovely lad, sang all day"
(`content/quota/q_dinner.lwd:81`) is a man admiring work he did well. What he leaves out: that he paid
for his son's name with a Hesh man's residency.

**Ilo's choice.** Ilo knew at the harvest supper, from the way Abel's lad wouldn't look at the cottar's
girl. When Abel offered the tenancy and the wife together, Ilo understood the price and took it, because
the other choice was the hill in winter with no papers. Then he loved the child, which nobody paid him
for. "Put Haldane" at the census table is the deal and the love in two words. What he leaves out: he
has never told Bet he knows, and he will not tell Anni anything.

**Mary's suspicion.** Mary married a man who wanted it done by spring, the season a farm girl was sent
home. She never asked. She has watched the Haldane girl's face lengthen for fourteen years at the
relief queue and the church gate, and she knows the girl's birthday. What she leaves out: everything,
to everyone, including herself.

**Edwin Mott's ruled line.** Mott asked for the father, twice. Ilo said "Put Haldane." Mott looked past
him at Abel in the doorway, hat in hand, and ruled a line down the father column, the way he rules every
empty column. In the margin, in pencil, he wrote "per A.F." His account, if anyone asked: he writes what
he is told, and what he isn't told he rules off. Walter inherits the register and the pencil.

## 7. The persecution argument: paper makes the people (PROPOSAL)

The register files a child under the father's people. Where the column is ruled, the child follows the
household. So Anni, with no Hesh blood, is entered HESH, while her brothers, Hesh by blood on one side,
are entered HALF-HESH (`content/quota/q_props.lwd:113`). On paper she is the most Hesh child in the
house. One signature in one column would make her Tarn overnight, with no change in her body at all.
Ethnicity in Edrin is a column, a thread and a cup, and the office owns the column.

**The reading the writing must prevent:** that she is worth saving because her blood is Tarn, or that
the story escapes the Hesh by revealing someone was "really" Tarn. The guards:
1. **Blood saves nobody by itself.** Only a man's signature and the office's list move her, and the same
   office starves her brothers. Tomas, with Hesh blood, dies on every route that holds him.
2. **Every escape takes something Hesh from her on screen**: the thread at the door, Heshen at the
   table, Ilo off her children's names, a Hesh family's room.
3. **Nobody the story trusts treats her better for it.** Those who change toward her because of blood
   are the office, Mary's ration of manners, and Aaron's resentment.
4. **No instrument confirms blood.** No test, doctor or record ever states it. Faces are the evidence,
   and reading faces is the persecutors' method; the camera says so by its framing.
5. **The Hesh refuse the category.** The Well counts her by the cup she drank from at the rite. The
   Tesks always knew, and it changed nothing for them. Only the Tarn and their paper treat the fact as
   news.
6. **No "really".** No narration or interface string may contain "really Tarn", "Tarn blood", "by
   blood", "real father" or "true father". Characters may say them, with a hearer; those lines are the
   office's grammar in a person's mouth. A lint for this joins dirlint's word list.
7. **Era 5 gets no heritage test.** Ryan Haldane, who posts "they should all go back", never learns
   what he is. The racist-discovers-his-blood twist is banned: it makes blood the truth.
8. **Neither choice is a verdict.** If she keeps the thread, the game does not praise her; it charges
   her a goat-Hesh family's name. If she takes the list, the game does not call her a traitor; it shows
   her the house and whose it was.

## 8. The Hesh as the place Tarn shame is put (PROPOSAL)

**The town** tells it wrong and likes the wrong version: Bet got a child off a Hesh hand before the
wedding, which proves what everyone says of them (`01-WORLD.md:55`). Abel's cover works because the slur
was ready to wear it. Tarn shame went up the hill dressed as Hesh appetite.

**The Furrow Church** churched Bet after the wedding and entered Anni "base-born, Haldane household". The
minister praised the match from the pulpit as thrift: a girl kept from ruin, a hand set to work, nothing
wasted. Twelve years later the same Church's home on Tilney Road cuts the thread off the child it
blessed into a Hesh house (03-FATES.md section 1).

**The Well** gave her the thread at the cup when Ilo carried her up the hill. Old Tesk was against it:
a Tarn child is Tarn trouble, and the town will say the Hesh steal children. Ilo won. The Hesh on the hill
say of her, in Tarnish, "She came with the barns", and some of them think less of Ilo for the barn.

**What the Hesh know that the Tarn do not:** whose she is, from the hands' talk and from Ilo at the cup;
that the tenancy was a price; that "came with the barns" is an old phrase because it has happened
before; and that the Tarn's stories about Hesh appetite are built from Tarn acts. They have no way to
say any of it that a Tarn would hear.

## 12. Hostile self-critique, and questions

- **Soap.** The secret father is the oldest device in serial drama, and the risk is that "whose child is
  she" pushes the genocide into the background. What keeps it out: the reveal changes no fate already
  decided, opens no rescue by itself, and pays off only through the office's list.
- **Melodrama.** Confrontation is the soap's payoff. There is none: no scene says it, nobody weeps over
  it, and the strongest lines are evasions.
- **Twist for its own sake.** The inspector being the father looks contrived. The test: cut it, and
