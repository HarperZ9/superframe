# Spine plan 10: the inferno, a mythic arena of the self-lies

Part of [../SPINE-PLAN.md](../SPINE-PLAN.md). **PROPOSAL for the author.** It extends the inner arena
([08-ARENA.md](08-ARENA.md)) into a bestiary of twelve bosses and a final Adversary, and answers the
critic's two attacks ([../critic/06-BOSSES.md](../critic/06-BOSSES.md),
[../critic/07-INFERNO.md](../critic/07-INFERNO.md)). No content or code is changed.

## The author's direction, verbatim (2026-10-22)

- "Fates are decided by named people in written scenes is a fantastic way to explore this, and a
  combined monstrosity. Bringing the great sins, and a battle with different representations and models
  of demons and the devil/shaytan himself would be incredibly intense."
- Endorsed principles: the bosses are the character's own self-lies made flesh, each a fusion of the lie
  and the people it cost; winning isn't healing (beating a lie by force can prove it, some fights end only
  by refusing, enduring or giving way, and some are meant to be lost); the character changes rather than
  simply getting stronger, and every boon is paid for with something the story takes away; the outer
  world stays human, so the metaphor never becomes an alibi.
- Added later the same day: "Ah yes, and the battle with the Golem, and many more other mythical creatures
  and entities; like an arena of intense battle. As well as Frankenstein, and other previously referenced
  media and story material we discussed over the months."

## The Adversary, three variants

- **A, the Accuser.** A clerk at a desk with one lamp, true on every fact, unbeatable, reading the run's
  record on ice; the run's kept flaws stand behind his chair as evidence. He ends when the player stops
  answering, then turns to a fresh page headed with the next people.
- **B, the Host.** The author's combined monstrosity: the run's kept lies in one body with the faces of
  the player's own line, speaking the player's words. Force freezes the ice under the player; each limb
  falls only when its lie is named.
- **C, the One Who Withdraws.** No body and no fight: the last kept lie as a whisper in an empty yard,
  ended once by recognition, and the note card "It will be back."

## Answers to the critic

**Critic 6 (bosses).**
1. *Bosses only at turning points, where winning means doing the thing the lie excused.* Agreed and
   adopted as rule 3 and rule 4 ([inferno/02-BESTIARY-A.md](inferno/02-BESTIARY-A.md) section 1). The
   author's "arena of intense battle" is met once, in the Descent, built from the run.
2. *Reject the growth arc.* Agreed. Boons sharpen the harmful verb and cost a named person; no outcome
   heals; the external arena menu never offers inner bosses. One disagreement: the non-force outcomes
   still move a `dc`, which is a gain, and the self-critique puts that to the author
   ([inferno/08-CRITIQUE.md](inferno/08-CRITIQUE.md) section 3.4).
3. *"Every loaf won is one a named family didn't get" governs.* Agreed: rule 7. Every boon's price is a
   written beat on a named person.
4. *Its four bosses.* B1 is Received and Found Correct's base; B2 is Friday's first phase; B3 is Forty
   Carts' third phase; B4 is Fond of Them's mechanic, moved to the root. B5, Bet's mercy-lie as a
   monster, is rejected here too. **Disagreement:** B4's quit returns full bowls, which writes a fate; here
   it returns nothing.

**Critic 7 (inferno).**
1. *A devil can excuse the humans.* It does not here because: the outer world stays human and a check
   enforces it (`humanworldcheck`); every boss wears only its liar's face; perpetrators never enter the
   arena; every Adversary variant disclaims power over anyone, as Iblis does in Qur'an 14:22; and in Job
   the adversary is an officer of the court, not a cause. The self-critique names where this can still
   fail (the Golem and Frankenstein are alibi myths) and the reader test that would catch it.
2. *The classical sins are the wrong list.* Agreed in substance. Every boss carries the town's virtue as
   its name and the sin only as a hidden label, after the yetzer hara reading: the drive that builds the
   house kills the boy. Diligence, fatherhood, thrift, duty, courtesy, protection and obedience are the
   killers. I keep the classical list as the designer's index, because Gregory's queen of pride is the
   right root for contempt.
3. *Demonising imagery is a tool of persecution; use it on purpose.* Adopted with a change. The Furrow
   Church's folk name for its devil is the Tallowman, and "tallowmen" is already the Tarn slur for the
   Hesh (`design/quota/01-WORLD.md:51`). I decline the critic's "tares", which follows a Gospel parable
   too closely. The optional Doom repaint shows a named minister paying to put threads on devils.
4. *"Shaytan" maps invented faiths onto real ones.* The Well's adversary is the Dry One, the one who drinks
   alone; the Furrow Church's is the Accuser and the Tallowman. The real-name question is put to the author
   with three options and not settled ([inferno/05-FAITH.md](inferno/05-FAITH.md) section 4).
   **Disagreement:** the critic gives the Well no devil; that makes the victims saintly, the critic's own
   mode 9.
5. *The Accuser, and the author's mission on trial.* Developed as variant A. **Disagreements:** his offer
   of the grave for names would buy an exclusive story with a complicit act, which `complicitycheck`
   forbids, so he offers to strike the player's own lines instead; and he belongs to the state's office
   as much as to the church.
