# Inferno 5: the faiths, who calls whom devils, and the sensitivity read

Part of [../10-INFERNO.md](../10-INFERNO.md). **PROPOSAL throughout.** Built on the faith table
(`design/quota/01-WORLD.md:43`), the voice guide's faith sections (`design/literary/07-VOICE-GUIDE.md:115-128`)
and the critic's proposal that the Furrow Church's devil-name become a slur (`design/critic/07-INFERNO.md`,
section 4).

## 1. The Furrow Church: the Accuser, and the Tallowman

The Furrow Church is the Tarn faith of the field: the harvest blessed, sin as waste, the Sower over the
altar (`design/quota/08-WORLD-MEDIA.md:63`). Its scripture is to be written new, ten lines of Sower
readings, misquoted by the people who use them (`design/literary/08-CRAFT.md:132-133`).

**Its adversary has two names, and the second is the slur.**

- **In the pulpit, the Accuser.** A learned name, used by the minister: the one who stands in the court
  of the harvest and accuses the field. This is the figure of [04-ADVERSARY.md](04-ADVERSARY.md) variant
  A, and the church's own theology says he is an officer of the court, not a rival god.
- **In the kitchen, the Tallowman.** The folk name: the one who comes into the field at night with a
  burning lump of fat and sets the stooks alight, so the harvest is wasted. Waste is the church's sin,
  so the waster is its devil.
- **The slur already exists.** The Tarn call the Hesh "tallowmen" (`design/quota/01-WORLD.md:51`), for
  the fat and tallow of their goat trade. **PROPOSAL:** the slur is older than the scripture's folk
  devil, or the other way round, and nobody in the town remembers which. The minister never says the
  word about the Hesh. He says "Some in this town have bread they did not sow. I name nobody. You know
  your neighbours." (voice guide PROPOSAL, `07-VOICE-GUIDE.md:119-120`), and the congregation goes home
  and says tallowmen.
- **Why the Tallowman and not the critic's "tares".** The critic proposes that "tares", the weeds an
  enemy sows among the wheat, be the slur. That reading is one of the Gospel parables almost word for
  word, which maps the Furrow Church onto Christianity more closely than the handling rules allow (states,
  parties and peoples invented, `design/QUOTA-DESIGN.md:31-32`). The Tallowman grows from a slur already
  in the world and from the church's own sin of waste. I disagree with the critic here and keep his
  method: the devil's name is the slur.
- **Persecution in religious language.** The rhetoric from the pulpit is already a carrier with a rule
  (`content/quota/q_discourse.lwd:49-56`, R17c: "it lands slowly and it stays"). The Tallowman adds the
  word the pulpit's slow blame lands as.

### The Doom over the arch

**PROPOSAL, optional.** The Furrow Church has a Doom painted over the chancel arch two centuries before
era 1: the Accuser at the field's edge and his small devils driving sinners into a stove's mouth. In era 1
the minister of the day, named, pays a painter, named, to touch it up for the harvest festival. The
painter gives three of the devils a thread at the wrist. It is a written scene with a decider, after the
fates plan (`design/spine-plan/03-FATES.md`). Era 2: the Doom is covered with the Sower for Lent and
stays covered through the famine (`08-WORLD-MEDIA.md:63`). Era 5: the heritage trust uncovers and
restores it, threads and all, and the plaque calls it "a fine example of rural devotional painting".

This shows what medieval Europe did to real minorities, drawing them as devils in churches, as an act a
person with a brush did for a fee. **Risk:** it is the image the critic warns about
(`07-INFERNO.md`, section 2.4), so it is shown only in the world, as a painting, in the base look, never
in the inner arena, and never lit for drama. The Christian and Jewish readers decide whether it stays.

## 2. The Well: the one who drinks alone

The Well is the Hesh water faith: daily washing, the shared cup, the dead buried with water, the prayer
for the water said at night (`01-WORLD.md:43`; `content/quota/q_will.lwd:15`).

**Its adversary is a person, not a creature: the one who drinks alone.** The shared cup is the faith's
centre, so its evil is the cup kept back: the closed well, the hoarded water, the one who drinks and does
not pass it on. The Heshen word is left for the word list (`design/literary/08-CRAFT.md:129`); PROPOSAL
as a gloss, "the Dry One".

- **Why the Well needs one.** The critic would give the Well no devil (`07-INFERNO.md`, section 4).
  That makes the persecuted people pure, the critic's own failure mode 9, and leaves them no way to hate
  anyone. The Hesh have contempt of their own (`design/spine-plan/04-PERSECUTION.md`, section 4).
- **Who the Hesh call the Dry Ones.** The town Hesh say it of the goat-Hesh who will not share with the
  children from over the ridge, and Dorrah says it back at them (`04-PERSECUTION.md`, section 4, the
  Dorrah and Ilo sample). In era 5 Sefa Tesk at the Well House door says it of a Vell girl who drinks
  with her head uncovered (`design/quota/02-HOUSEHOLDS.md:100-101`). Within the faith the word is a
  charge of selfishness; at the Well House door it becomes a charge of not belonging.
- **What the Hesh call the Tarn.** In era 2 the Hesh have a word for the requisition brigades that means
  "those who take the cup off the table". It is said quietly, at night, and nobody writes it down. The
  asymmetry stays plain: the Hesh word has no list, no notice and no rifle behind it.

## 4. The real-name question, for the author

The author's words were "the devil/shaytan himself". The critic warns that a real faith's name for the
devil maps the invented faiths onto real ones (`07-INFERNO.md`, section 2.5). This file does not settle
it. The options:

| Option | What it means | For | Against |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Invented names only** (Accuser, Tallowman, the Dry One) | No real sacred name is spoken anywhere in the game | Keeps the handling rule; no real faith becomes the nearest target | The author's word "shaytan" is lost in play |
| **2. Real names outside the world** | The "About the history" page (`04-PERSECUTION.md`, section 8) names the traditions the design drew on, Satan, Iblis, Mara and the rest, with sources | Credits the sources honestly; the world stays invented | A player may map anyway |
| **3. Real names inside the world** | A character says "Shaytan" or "Satan" | The author's word, kept | The Hesh, the persecuted faith, become the nearest real faith in a player's mind; this breaks the handling rule as written |

The designer's lean is option 1 with option 2 (stated as a lean, not a pick). If the author wants option
3, the Muslim reader decides how and whether, before any line is written.

## 5. Respect, prophets and the sensitivity read

- **No prophet is depicted.** No figure in the game is Adam, Abraham, Moses, Jesus, Muhammad or the
  Buddha, or stands for one. Iblis's refusal is used as structure only. The Sower on the altar is an
  invented painting of an invented scripture, covered in era 2, and no boss wears it.
- **No sacred text is quoted in the world.** The traditions are quoted in design files and on the
  history page only.
- **No real sacred object is desecrated** for effect: no inverted cross, no torn page, no defiled
  vessel. The critic's blasphemy-as-decoration marker (`07-INFERNO.md`, section 3) is a check
  ([06-SYSTEMS.md](06-SYSTEMS.md), `inferno-imagecheck`).

**The readers.** The production plan already names Muslim and Jewish readers (`design/quota/07-PRODUCTION.md:64-68`).
PROPOSAL scope for this plan, before M2 and before any boss reaches content:
