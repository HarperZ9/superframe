# Inferno 2: the bestiary, part one (the rules, the root, and Ned's line)

Part of [../10-INFERNO.md](../10-INFERNO.md). **PROPOSAL throughout.** Every spoken line here is a
PROPOSAL for the author to approve or cut before it becomes content, and each was written to be read
aloud. Part two is [03-BESTIARY-B.md](03-BESTIARY-B.md).

## 1. Rules every boss obeys

1. **A boss is one self-lie made flesh.** It exists in a run only if the character heard that lie and
   the player did not turn the voice away (`design/spine-plan/08-ARENA.md`, section 3). Five lies are
   tagged today (`content/quota/q_readback.lwd:38-66`). Lines that exist in content but are not tagged,
   and new lines this file needs, are marked **TAG** or **NEW LINE**.
2. **Two names.** The boss's name is the town's: a virtue a neighbour would praise. The sin is the
   designer's label, after the yetzer hara reading ([01-SOURCES.md](01-SOURCES.md) section 3), kept in
   the `@flaw` record and never shown. This answers the critic's point that the town kills with its
   virtues (`design/critic/07-INFERNO.md`, section 2.3).
3. **Only at hinges.** A boss is fought on the night after the scene where its lie excused an act. The
   critic's rule (`design/critic/06-BOSSES.md`, section 3, rule 5) is adopted for story play. The late
   Descent ([06-SYSTEMS.md](06-SYSTEMS.md) section 3) brings back the run's kept flaws once, as a
   gauntlet. Nowhere else.
4. **Force proves the lie.** Each boss can be beaten or ended by force, or has a force path that ends
   in a loss. In every case the force outcome makes the lie true inside the dream, and its voice gets
   easier to obey: its `dc` falls.
5. **Refuse, endure, yield, lose.** Each boss has at least one way to end that is not force. **Refuse**:
   leave by a door that this fight alone has. **Endure**: survive its pattern without landing a blow.
   **Yield**: lay the tool down and take an action the lie forbids. **Lose**: go down, and the loss plays
   what happened. None of these heals. Each changes the disposition, and none changes a fate.
6. **Faces.** A boss wears only the face of its liar or of his own line (Abel, Ned, Pell; Josh, Will,
   Leon). The people a lie cost appear as objects, shadows and sounds: a coat, a tally, a thread, a pair
   of shoulders, a breath. No Hesh person, no child and no world perpetrator (Holt, Gedge, Pask, Krall,
   Mott) is ever drawn as a monster or fought. A perpetrator may be heard as a human voice from outside
   the dream. This narrows the brief's "the faces of the people it cost" on purpose: a victim's face on
   a monster draws a people as a monster (`design/sensory/03-SURREAL.md`, rule 8).
7. **Every boon is paid, by a named person.** The bread fights' rule governs: "every loaf won is one a
   named family didn't get" (`design/quota/05-ARENA.md:14`). A boon is offered only after a force
   outcome, and its cost is a written beat in a later scene, on a named person.
8. **The outer world stays human.** No boss, demon or devil acts in a world scene. Demons exist in
   sermons, posters, nightmares and the inner arena only (the critic's addition, adopted).
9. **No lesson aloud, no tally.** As 08-ARENA rules 7 and 8.

**Numbering.** Boss numbers are ids shared by every inferno file, not a play order; Boss 10 sits with Ned's line here because it is his son's.

**Format per boss.** Whose lie and when; the two names; the myth used as method; form and morphs;
sound and voice; arena; the fight's rules; boon and cost; disposition; faces and objects. Senses and
care per boss are in [07-SENSES.md](07-SENSES.md).

## 2. The root

### Boss 1. Fond of Them

- **Whose lie, when.** Abel Furrowe, era 1, the night after the census board decides seasonal hands
  are not residents, with Abel in the room (`design/quota/03-STORY.md:28-31`). Era 2's Abel says the
  lie at the table: "I'm fond of them ... Not with my grain." (`content/quota/q_dinner.lwd:81`).
  **TAG** an era 1 PRIDE line at the census scene, PROPOSAL: "You've had a Hesh lad in your loft all
  winter. Nobody in this room can say you're against them."
- **Town's name:** Courtesy. **Sin:** pride, Gregory's queen of the vices; Iblis's "I am better than
  him", contempt by what each is made of.
- **Method.** Gregory's queen hands the heart to her generals: this boss is the root, fought first in
  the Furrowe line, and its hat returns in the Adversary. Iblis: the Tarn are wheat and the Hesh are
  "tallowmen" (`design/quota/01-WORLD.md:51`), fat that burns. Pinocchio (critic B4 extended): a son
  carved to bow.
- **Form and morphs.**
  1. *The host.* A bowing coat under Abel's good hat, at the head of the harvest supper's long table.
     Abel's face at sixty-three, smiling, the hat's line across the forehead. It passes plates; each plate
     lands as a blow.
  2. *The strings.* Below half, fine threads run from the character's wrists and shoulders up into the
     hat's brim. Each bow the character makes is pulled. The camera stays at the character's height,
     so the player sees the strings go up and never sees where they end.
  3. *The offer.* The coat sits. The hat lifts off and floats to the character, brim first.
- **Sound and voice.** A warm man of sixty-three, fond, never menacing. The good hat's rising fourth
  (`design/sensory/04-SENSES.md`, section 2), proud and whole. Under it, off in the yard, a young man
  singing: Ilo, who "sang all day" (`q_dinner.lwd:81`).
- **Lines (PROPOSAL).**
  > Sit down, sit down. There's plenty. There's always plenty if people are sensible.
  > I'm not against them. I had one in my loft all winter. Ask him. He'll tell you.
  > It isn't wickedness. It's what they're made of. You can't blame tallow for burning.
  > There. You see? You bow lovely. Your father bowed lovely.
- **Arena.** The harvest supper in Orme's barn, the census ledger open at the head of the table. The
  anchor: the barn door, unpainted, propped against the wall. It is the door Ilo will make into the
  Haldane table (`design/literary/03-REGISTER.md`, the Haldane table).
- **Rules.** Blows turn into bows and heal it (critic B4). **Force:** the only "win" is to take the hat
  in phase 3. The fight ends with the hat on the character's head: courtesy won, and the hat goes down
  the line. **Endure:** stand without bowing and take its blows; it costs standing heavily, and when
  the pattern has run three times the hat is left on the table, nobody's. **Refuse:** during the toast
  to "the Hesh temperament" (`03-STORY.md:40`) a door opens at the barn's end. Walking out ends the
  fight; the character wakes cold and the census is unchanged.
- **Boon (force only).** *The Good Hat*: the first line the character speaks in an office scene is
  heard. In the arena, poise up. **Cost:** a written beat at the second sitting of the supper: Ilo is not
  asked in and eats in the yard, and the character sees it through the window. In era 2 the hat comes to
  Ned, with "You let him decide what he heard" (`q_dinner.lwd:55`).
- **Disposition.** Force: PRIDE `dc` -1 for the Furrowe line's next generation; CONDOLENCE -1.
  Endure: PRIDE `dc` +1. Refuse: PRECEDENT -1; standing at the exchange lower in the next scene.
- **Faces and objects.** Abel's own face. Ilo as a voice singing outside, never seen. The barn door.

**Disagreement with the critic (B4).** The critic's quit "returns the post, the good pen and full
bowls" (`design/critic/06-BOSSES.md`, B4). Bowls are a fate field and no inner outcome may write one
(`08-ARENA.md`, rule 5). Here a quit keeps the hat on the table and returns nothing.

## 3. Ned's line, era 2 and era 3

### Boss 2. Received and Found Correct

- **Whose lie, when.** Ned, era 2, the night after the returns room. SUM: "The sheet says three-ten.
  You've signed that the sheet says three-ten. Nobody can say you didn't." (`content/quota/q_ned.lwd:71`,
  tagged `q.sl.signed`).
- **Town's name:** Diligence: "I sign everything. I'm very good at it." (`q_dinner.lwd:108`).
  **Sin:** false witness, *shahadat al-zur*, among the gravest of al-kaba'ir.
- **Method.** Critic B1 as the base. Bosch's tabletop eye with the pupil left empty: "nobody can say"
  means nobody sees. The repeated warning, turned round: the lie repeats.
- **Form and morphs.**
  1. *The clerk.* A body of stacked return sheets with many hands, each holding the good pen. Where the
     face should be, the shine India rubber leaves (`q_ned.lwd:61`). If Ned kept Anni's tally, it is
     pinned to the chest like a ticket (`said.ned_keep_tally`).
  2. *The flock.* The sheets lift off it and wheel round the room like starlings, each with a figure,
     and land as weights.
  3. *The eye.* The round desk tilts into a bowl, its rim the eleven villages in Ned's hand, its empty
     pupil a signature line. The clerk is the ink pooled in the pupil.
- **Sound and voice.** SUM's own voice, close and dry. The ledger motif in D dorian with the written
  figure on top and the true one in the bass (`04-SENSES.md`, section 2). Applause from an empty room:
  one man, then two (`content/quota/q_office.lwd:12`).
- **Lines (PROPOSAL).**
  > Three-ten. Received and found correct. Lovely clear hand.
  > Nobody can say you didn't. Nobody can say you didn't. Nobody can say.
  > I never wrote a figure in my life. I only sign them.
  > Mott's gone home. Sign, and you can go home too.
- **Arena.** The returns room enlarged; the district map with pencil under the ink; the clock.
- **Rules.** The only attack is the signature stroke (critic B1). Each hit signs a sheet. **Force:**
  when it falls the last sheet reads the district total and the room applauds; nobody can say he did
  not sign. **Yield:** put the pen down (the lay-down action, [06-SYSTEMS.md](06-SYSTEMS.md)). The
  fight does not end. The clock runs, the room empties, and at two it gives way to the post road, where
  "Tomas is there before him, stamping his feet" (`q_ned.lwd:130`). That is a memory, and it changes no
  fate. Ned wakes in the chair.
- **Boon (force).** *A Clear Hand*: the signature stroke starts two frames sooner in later inner fights.
  **Cost:** in the next office scene Walter Mott brings his next sheet to Ned before anyone else, and
  says why: "You're the one that signs." Named person: Walter Mott.
- **Disposition.** Force: SUM `dc` -1, SMALL PRINT -1. Yield: SUM `dc` +1; CONDOLENCE +1 at the next
  Mott beat.
- **Faces and objects.** Ned's hands. Anni's tally, an object. Mott's sevens without a bar on the sheets
  (`content/quota/q_props.lwd:82`), his hand and never his face.

### Boss 3. Only

- **Whose lie, when.** Ned, era 2, the night after the district office. SUM: "Two-ninety is what they
  sent. You only add up what they send." (`q_office.lwd:31`, tagged `q.sl.add_up`). It returns at the
  coda: "It's only a number." (`content/quota/q_coda.lwd:141`; **TAG**).
- **Town's name:** Humility, the clerk who is only a clerk. **Sin:** acedia, Evagrius's noonday demon.
- **Method.** The noonday demon: the sun does not move and the day is fifty hours long. 08-ARENA's
  eleven chairmen's coats with nobody in them.
- **Form and morphs.**
  1. *The meeting.* Eleven coats on eleven chairs round the district table. The twelfth chair is the
     character's. When he strikes one, they clap.
  2. *Noon.* The sun stops on the table. Dust stands in the light. The coats rise and close in, so slowly
     the player cannot see them move, only find them nearer.
  3. *The chair.* The coats sit back down. The camera finds the twelfth chair with a coat on it and
     nobody inside.
- **Sound and voice.** SUM again, mild and reasonable. The bell tower's single bell (`04-SENSES.md`)
  struck once at the start and never again. The room tone is the only bed; it does not change.
- **Lines (PROPOSAL).**
  > You only add up what they send. That's the whole of the job.
  > It's only noon. It's been noon a while. Nobody's in a hurry.
  > Only a desk. Only till the spring. Only a number.
  > Sit down. Your chair's still warm.
- **Arena.** The district office at noon, light across the table, the window's sun fixed.
- **Rules.** **Force:** every coat struck claps, and noon gets longer; when all are down the clock moves
  to one and the vote passes. Nothing the character did mattered, which is the lie proved. **Endure:**
  the trap. Noon has no end; enduring only lengthens it, and the game lets the player find that out.
  **Refuse:** this fight has a door (08-ARENA). Walking out ends it. The cost is waking with nothing
  changed, and that is the honest outcome.
- **Boon (force).** *Patience*: Breath recovers faster. **Cost:** at the coda Ned's speech gains one
  more "only", and Mary does not answer it; the scene holds the silence. Named person: Mary.
- **Disposition.** Force: SUM +1, CARBON +1. Refuse: UNDERTOW +1, SUM `dc` +1.
- **Faces and objects.** None human. The coats are the chairmen's, empty. The character's own chair.

### Boss 10. The Figure (era 3, Pell)

- **Whose lie, when.** Pell Furrowe, reconstruction surveyor, era 3 (`design/quota/02-HOUSEHOLDS.md:32`),
  the night after the allocation office reads Ned's old figure as the district's record. **NEW LINE**,
  PRECEDENT: "Your father was a careful man. He never put his name to a number he hadn't counted."
  Ned's own words were "Reported as found" (`02-HOUSEHOLDS.md:31`).
- **Town's name:** Loyalty to one's father. **Sin:** the maker's abandonment, and false witness
  inherited.
- **Method.** Frankenstein. The maker made a thing in an afternoon and would not look at it again; the
  thing tells its own story, which is the true record; del Toro's creature cannot die. In the author's
  readings the three Haldane-side makers map differently: Ned is Victor, who walks away from what he
  made; Ilo made a Tarn family by giving up his name, a maker who disguised what he made; Bet made "up
  at Mrs Tesk's" (`content/quota/q_coda.lwd:41`), a story that outlives its use. Only Ned's line is a
  boss. Ilo's and Bet's stay written scenes, because no Hesh character has an arena (08-ARENA rule 6).
- **Form and morphs.**
  1. *The visitor.* A tall, stooped, careful figure stitched from eleven village sheets, each in its own
     clerk's hand, sewn with a pen nib. Ned's handwriting across its skin. It does not attack. It follows
     Pell and asks.
  2. *The testimony.* Struck, it answers back, and with each phase it tells one village: the barn, the
     figure, the clerk, the family. Eleven short tellings.
  3. *The ice.* The surveyor's office breaks up into the frozen Edd. The chase that Victor ran north
     runs here on the river. It cannot die.
- **Sound and voice.** Slow, grammatical, taught by the record it was made from. Pell's own room tone,
  then wind on ice. The ledger motif played on one string.
- **Lines (PROPOSAL).**
  > Your father made me in an afternoon. He wouldn't look at me after.
  > I was eleven villages. He said me out loud, and they clapped.
  > Show me where it says I wasn't made. Go on. You've got the book.
  > I don't want anything. I wanted him to have looked at me.
- **Arena.** Pell's office over the allocation map; then the river ice under the bridge.
- **Rules.** **Force:** it cannot die. Pell defends his father's name by attacking his father's work and
  becomes the maker's heir in the chase; the fight ends when his standing runs out on the ice. Force
  ends in a loss, and the loss proves the lie, since he kept the careful father. **Endure:** stand and
  take its eleven tellings without a blow. At the end it sits on the ice, and its last sheet shows the
  Haldane barns: a hundred and ninety in pencil under three-ten.
- **Boon.** None: force ends in a loss.
- **Disposition.** Force: PRECEDENT +1, CARBON +1. Endure: SUM `dc` +2 for any lie about the figure in
  Pell's line; PRIDE -1.
- **Faces and objects.** Ned's handwriting; the village sheets; Mott's barless sevens.

## 4. Where the Furrowe line meets the Haldane door

Received and Found Correct, Only and The Figure are Ned's line from the signature through the coda to
the son who inherits the number. Fond of Them is the queen they serve. Ned has no boss for the moment at
the door, "You knew my dad. Ilo." (`q_ned.lwd:85`), on purpose: that betrayal is Judecca's, and it is
kept for the Adversary's ice ([04-ADVERSARY.md](04-ADVERSARY.md)).
