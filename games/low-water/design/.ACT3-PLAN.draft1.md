# Act 3, The Gale: the plan

From the director, 2026-10-17. Private. Planned before any content, to the standard of
`design/REWORK-PLAN.md` (story, five play styles, path matrix, verbs, fights that matter, brevity)
and with the lessons of the story editor's three reads (`design/EDITOR-NOTES.md`). Builds on
`design/PRODUCTION.md` sections 3, 7, 8 and 10, the bible's threads (section 12), and the state Acts
1 and 2 leave (`content/act2/dawn2.lwd`: the three lamps chosen for the gale).

## 1. The question, the spine, the turns

**What will the town live with?** Night 1 named a cause; Night 2 made someone pay for it. Night 3
is the night the claim comes true: in a south-west gale, with the town keeping three lamps alight
on the oil it has, the wreckers the Board invented on Night 1 and the strippers made real on Night 2
try to put Mother out for a ship. Lamb ends the night writing the final apportionment in the Brine
with the surge at the step, and the verdict goes down a tube to a woman on the Ropewalk.

**The personal line resolves.** Lamb has been dry for eleven years as a matter of professional
pride. By the end of Night 3 there is no dry verdict and no dry Lamb: the last scene is Lamb choosing
whether to go back to being dry (the morning boat) or to stay wet in some form (keeper, typist,
regular at the Brine). The coat carries three tide marks at the hem, one per night.

## 2. The night

19:08 to 06:44 (PRODUCTION 8, night 3). South-west gale building 21:00 to 23:50 (18 m/s, gusts
26), rain in bands. The eye passes 00:15 to 01:00: calm, rain stops, stars. Low water 00:34 at
-0.30 m, held up by the surge: the drowned lamp's base is reachable only by boat, 00:14 to 00:54.
The wind backs north-west and rises from 01:10. Clears at dawn (06:04). Storm surge 0.8 m on the
morning high water, 06:44 at 6.2 m: the quay floods to the Brine's step at 06:30.

The gale is the act's real-time danger: gusts stagger anyone on the mole (the Fray, 18.8), rain
bands douse carried lights, spray soaks, and Cold rises fast outdoors. Shelter matters again.

## 4. The Shelf: who types it, and why

The Mutual Indemnity of the Lower Shelf failed eleven years ago, the winter Mother's arc was first
lit and the winter Lamb joined it. Its Hesk office is one room and one clerk who forwards Gullet
business down the old agency line. The far end of the pneumatic tube is not Hesk: it is the back
room of the Gannet house, which was the Shelf's Gullet agency when Hester's husband was its agent.
He went out on the floor on Low Night nineteen years ago with Jory Brine and did not come back.

Since the Shelf failed, Hester has typed its Gullet replies and paid its small claims "at the usual
rate" out of what was left in the agency's float and, for six years, out of the Alderman's letters
(each crossword in the Hesk paper, filled in, with a banker's draft folded inside). Crake pays because
the Trust is insured with the Shelf, and an uninsured Trust could not have borrowed for the sodium,
the automation or the white lamps. Hester types because a town that believes someone will pay keeps
its lamps lit and its boats out, and because her husband's last act was to sign the town's policies.

Her reason is not a scheme. It is a woman keeping a promise to a town on behalf of a company that
does not exist, with the help of a woman who needs the company to exist. The verdict Lamb sends at
dawn goes to her, and she will type the reply.

Equivalent facts that can establish it (any two): `typist.heard` (Act 2), `ropewalk.notice_matched`
(the R with the broken foot), `hester.seen_typing`, `hester.told`, `crake.drafts` (the banker's
drafts in the crossword letters), `pip.told` (Pip, if she knows).

The next night, seen from the hill: which lamps burn and in which colour, who is under each, and

explained things stay so: the low light, and Keel's water.
