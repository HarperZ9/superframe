# Low Water: production plan

Draft 1, 2026-10-16. Private. Director and lead writer's plan for taking Low Water from the one-hour
Lamp Quay slice to a full-length game. Companions: `bible/WORLD-BIBLE.md` (story),
`design/SYSTEMS-FULL.md` (systems and the crossover map), `design/LWD1-FORMAT.md` (data format),
`design/ACT1-PLAN.md` (Act 1 beat sheet), `design/STATE.md` (what exists today).

The author's brief (2026-10-16, verbatim): "Continue working on Low Water until it is a complete
full length story and game with a complete dialogue system, a traits and attributes system, and
about as much value as a game like Zero Parades: For Dead Spies, Disco Elysium, or No Rest For The
Wicked". Later the same day: "Ideally some sort of crossover between the three would be awesome,
feature wise". The game is judged by play, writing and look.

What this tells the plan. Disco Elysium's million words is an outlier; matching it would take years
and would be padding at our scale. The useful comparison is Zero Parades: a dialogue-led RPG of about
21 to 35 hours, 15 skills, a belief system and a pressure system. Low Water aims at that length and
density, adds the physical tension and town-rebuilding of the Moon Studios game in a form that suits
an assessor, and keeps a smaller cast more deeply written than either ZA/UM game can afford.

## 3. Act structure

The game is three nights of the spring tide. Each night the low water comes about fifty minutes later
and a little higher, so the harbour floor gives a shorter window each night and on the third night
none on foot. The weather runs from fog and rain, to a clear and freezing night when everyone can be
seen, to a gale. The days between are single pages: Lamb sleeps above the Brine, wakes once in
daylight, and sees Gullet's colours for the only time in the game.

### Act 1: Low Night (22:41 to 06:10)

The question: whose fault is the dark? Lamb arrives by the ferry that nearly struck the Spit and has
one night to name a cause.

- **Movement I, The Floor Is Open (22:41 to 00:00).** The slice, rewritten with the full systems:
  the floor and the drowned lamp open, the six principals, the fog, the rain, the tide coming back.
  Midnight: the Shelf asks for a provisional cause.
- **Movement II, Weather (00:00 to 03:30).** The provisional cause travels as rumour. The Gut opens
  to Lamb once the Guild leaves the weigh-house. Heavy rain at 01:30. The Lamplighters patrol. The
  stranded fish. Lamb's room above the Brine; salvage and the Kit. Fog thickest at 03:00. Crake
  rings again and the town takes sides on what Lamb said at midnight.
- **Movement III, Frost (03:30 to 06:10).** The rain clears at 04:10 and frost takes the mole.
  Someone climbs to Mother to break her lens, and the frost keeps the prints. High water at 05:10.
  Dawn, and the cause goes up the tube at 06:10.
- **Act 1 ending.** The dawn cause (six shapes, section 7) sets who is fallen and who is lit on
  Night 2. The Shelf answers: the assessor will remain while the spring tides run. The morning ferry's
  master will not sail until Mother burns true.

### Act 2: Second Water (19:10 to 06:20)

The question: who pays? The cause Lamb wrote is now a fact in town, and people act on it. Chancel
Hill opens. Crake in person. The engineer who wrote the restrike clause arrives on the evening boat.
The night is clear, still and freezing: every lamp works and every face is visible, so the
Lamplighters are at their strongest and nobody tells Lamb anything under the sodium. The Congregation
processes the Old Bulb down the hill. The Spit can be reached by boat at the second low water. The
first evidence that the Shelf may be one person in a back room. Ending: a second dawn, and the
choice of which lamps the town lights for the gale.

### Act 3: The Gale (19:08 to 06:44)

The question: what will the town live with? A south-westerly gale. The lowest water of the night
falls in the storm's calm eye, and for forty minutes a boat can reach the inside of the Old Light,
where the low light is. The Shelf's typist. The final verdict is apportioned among the parties Lamb
has a case against. The surge at dawn floods Lamp Quay to the Brine's door. The epilogue is the next
night, shown as the town's lights.

### Day pages (between acts)

Two short daylight scenes of 10 to 15 minutes each, in one place, with no checks: the town in
colour (oxblood, mustard, teal and bone, as the bible promises), the stranded things of the night
in plain light, and one conversation that cannot happen in the dark.

## 8a. Pacing (added 2026-10-16 after the Phase 3 prototype)

Finding from the polish pass (Phase 3 handoff in PROGRESS.md): in the one-hour slice a player
cannot reach every room before midnight; showing both the weigh-house blackout and Mother's arc
took two takes. Act 1 is paced knowing that.

- **Not seeing everything is intended; losing the spine is not.** The tide, the rain and the
  fog close windows on a schedule, so one play reaches about two thirds of Movement I's optional
  beats. Every clue the dawn verdict needs has at least two routes, one of them after midnight:
  C2 (scales) has Marrow's admission in the Gut or the smokehouse; C1 and C12 have the frost prints
  and Merrow at the stove; C8 has the oven again at 03:40; the blackout comes back at 02:50; Mother
  can be restruck at any hour the power is on.
- **Movement I is longer than the slice.** It now runs from 22:41 (the ferry berths), not 23:00:
  79 game minutes against the slice's 60.
- **Time budget.** Talk costs about a minute per two lines, walking 1 to 3 minutes. A first play of
  Movement I is planned at 35 to 45 nodes (about 90 to 120 minutes real time). Movements II and III
  have more clock (210 and 160 minutes) and fewer closing windows, so they are where a player
  catches up and goes deep.
- **The two set pieces that cannot share a take** (a blackout on the quay and Mother's arc relit)
  now each happen twice in Act 1: the 21:38 blackout told in testimony and the 02:50 blackout
  played; the restrike available in Movement I and needed again after 03:20.
- **Check after Act 1 is playable:** time three first plays of Movement I; if fewer than half the
  beats are reached, cut node costs by a third before cutting content.

Each stage is complete and playable before the next starts.

## 10. Combat across the game (added 2026-10-16)

Author direction, 2026-10-16, relayed by the coordinator (a paraphrase of a spoken message):
real-time combat like No Rest for the Wicked, the way its dodges and attacks work, is wanted, as a
full system beside the dialogue spine. Clean-room re-implementation is endorsed; behaviour comes
from `cleanroom/spec/` and public sources only, never from the games' files; expression is
original throughout. The system is `design/SYSTEMS-FULL.md` section 18 (the Fray); the combat
engineer builds it in `game/engine/combat/`.

What fits the fiction: Lamb is an assessor with a claims case, not a soldier. Fights are close and
improvised, with harbour tools, against people who want something and can usually be talked to,
and against the town's animals. Hesk wreckers drawn by the dark arrive in Act 2 as the only
opponents with no stake in the town. No monsters, no gore, no death for Lamb (going down is a
scene). Principals never die in a fight.
