# Era 1, The Exchange: the device audit

Weighted-devices pass, era 1, 2026-10-23, branch `feat/wd-e1`. Part of [DEVICE-AUDIT.md](../DEVICE-AUDIT.md).
Files: `content/quota/e1_*.lwd`. Line numbers are the lines before this pass (commit `dfa8e63`).

The lint found sixteen devices in era 1: eight inscriptions, one document prop, five props with no `carries:` and two
fates no echo showed. Reading every scene found the rest. The era's plot ran on paper at its hinge: the board passed
the rule as a minute, the hands were sent up the hill off a written list with a doctor's signed slips behind it, and
Ilo became Haldane in a census register, with the father column ruled and "per A.F." pencilled in the margin. Each
of those is now a body, a voice, a weight or a door.

## Plot changes

### 1. The census becomes census night at the end barn door

- **Was.** The registrar's front counter, a queue in the rain, Edwin Mott with the big book, Walter with the copy
  slips. Hands were written down with their employers' names; Ruan left with a slip pinned to his coat; Ilo said "Put
  Haldane", Edwin struck ILO and wrote HALDANE, I., after the farm; Bet would not name the father, Edwin ruled the
  column and pencilled "per A.F." or "per letter, A.F."
- **Now.** The Friday after the frost, at dusk, with a harder frost coming down. Edwin Mott walks the census round
  himself with Walter holding the lantern, and at every door asks who sleeps there and their name; whoever has no roof
  and no master to stand for them comes out, and the door is locked. At the barns at Haldane's, let to Ilo from
  Sunday, the end barn is cleared: Teodor is called Orme because Orme employs him, the widow Mill, and Ruan, who will
  not take a surname, carries everything he owns out into the dark. The Corleys, fetched back by the collar, carry
  their bedding out without being asked. Abel (the Inspector) or Josh (the Militia) can take the Corley woman's bundle
  off her back and carry it to the gate: the walk slows and bends (`shoulder(e1.load.bundle)`), and Corley thanks him
  without looking at him. Walter chalks a goat on the shut door, head down. The key is turned (the player turns it,
  `e1.key`). Then Edwin, with the mill row still to do, asks Ilo's name.
- **Why.** The lead's direction: the rule enacted at a door, the families carrying their things out into the first
  frost, the ancestor holding the key or the lantern. The barn door is the table's door, so the table that era 2 eats
  at is the door the hands were locked out of. The player's complicity is in his hands (the key, the bundle), and the
  kindness and the complicity are the same act.
- **Alternatives tested.** (a) The renaming at the exchange scale, by Abel weighing Ilo's sack: rejected, because it
  put the name in a trade scene the Militia line never reaches, and it made Abel the sole namer, which drops Mott and
  the board out of the hinge. (b) The bailiff as the board's man: rejected, because Edwin Mott is already the man who
  worded the rule, and his impatience ("I've the Marren cottages and the whole of the mill row to do tonight") is the
  man who cannot be bothered with a name. (c) The census kept at the counter with the book replaced by speech:
  rejected, because a counter is still a desk.
- **Kept.** `e1.named`, `e1.per_af`, `e1.per_letter`, the node ids `e1.census`, `e1.census.door`, `e1.census.letter`,
  `e1.census.name`, `e1.census.bet`; new nodes `e1.census.carry`, `e1.census.turn`, `e1.census.locked`,
  `e1.census.ilo`. Witness beat `e1.w.ilo.name` now reads `seen q1_barns`.

### 2. Ilo is named to his face, and the name sticks because the man in the hat says it

- **Was.** Ilo said "Put Haldane" to a pen over a column, and kept the slip.
- **Now.** Mott asks for a surname and will not hear "Ilo who's Ilo's". In person (`e1.per_af`), Abel in the doorway
  says "He's in Haldane's barns. Call him Haldane. That'll do him." At a distance (`e1.per_letter`), Abel's stable lad,
  sent with the key round his neck, says it the way he learned it on the walk down: "Mr Furrowe says, Ilo Haldane, after
  the farm." Ilo says the eleven names under his breath, then "Haldane, then." Walter goes off saying it to himself;
  by Monday the Upper Town says it, because it came from Mr Furrowe.
- **Why.** The lead's direction. Ilo still says it himself (the sum voice "He said it himself" keeps its sting), and
  the name is now a social fact made by a man's standing, which is how a name sticks in a town.
- **Flag meaning.** `e1.per_letter` keeps its id; its meaning is now "Abel stayed away and sent the key and his word by
  his stable lad". No other era reads it (grep of `content/`, the tests and the wiring, 2026-10-23).

### 3. Whose child Anni is, said aloud at the barn door

- **Was.** The father column ruled with a ruler and "per A.F." in pencil.
- **Now.** Mott asks "Whose." Bet looks at the frost on the cart wheel. In person, Abel turns his hat once by the brim
  and says "His. She's his. Isn't that right, Ilo," and Ilo says "She's mine." At a distance, the lad says the last of
  his lesson: "the little one's Ilo's, and that's all anybody need know." (2026-11-09: "his" there could be heard as
  Mr Furrowe's and give the secret away; 09-ANNI section 5 wants the first reading to be Ilo.) Abel (or the lad) gives Ilo the key, cold
  enough to hurt (`cold.iron`). Ilo holds the key to the door his friends were just put out of.
- **Why.** The erasure happens in front of Bet and Ilo, from Abel's own mouth or by his proxy, which is worse than a
  pencil and needs no reading.

### 4. The witness opens on the key, not the slip

- **Was.** Ilo unfolds the census slip and has Tomas read it to him.
- **Now.** Ilo puts the end barn key on the Sheaf's table. "What's it open?" "Nothing. The door's your mum's table."
  His name beat names the barn door, the lantern and the Corleys turned out.

### 5. The rule passes by a show of hands

- **Was.** A minute written and read back; "carried"; Lind's answer written down shorter.
- **Now.** Mott says the wording with his eyes shut, the registrar says all those for, and the hands go up: George's
  first, Lind's slow in its glove, Ashby's, Abel's with his hat in it. Josh, at the door, is not asked. Lind's answer
  is shortened aloud, in front of him. In the loft Abel says "I put my hand up for it."
- **Why.** A ruling on paper becomes men's hands in a warm room. Abel's hat going up is the image the player keeps.
- **Kept.** `e1.rule_passed`, `pressure(f.e1_sallis)`, `pressure(f.e1_kel)`, the `inner(fond, ...)` call untouched.

### 6. The first frost has no list

- **Was.** The list on the nail in Mott's hand; Josh reading it out; Lind's signed slips behind it; Josh writing a
  margin note for the kept.
- **Now.** Josh walks the line with his hands behind his back and says the households face by face: he looked in every
  cart in August and went round the barns with the dog. Lind passes them from his gig with an inch of his whip, the
  coughing ones the same. Kept by Abel: Josh swings Arn's bundle into Abel's trap. Kept by Orme: Haig takes the bundle
  into Orme's trap. Sent: the pole goes up on its weight (`e1.pole`), or Josh lifts it himself.
- **Why.** The fate is decided by a man at a pole who knows their faces, which is the coordinator's "a man at the
  granary door deciding who goes in".
- **Kept.** `f.e1_sallis`, its decider JOSH and its three scene nodes; `e1.sallis_sent`, `e1.sallis_kept` (era 3 reads
  `e1.sallis_kept`, `e3_fever.lwd:314, 379`; meaning unchanged), `said.e1_abel_loft`, `said.e1_josh_carter`.

### 7. Kel's charge is his boots on the nail

- **Was.** A paper bag marked EVIDENCE, an entry in the post book, two signatures; the paper pinned to his coat.
- **Now.** Josh hangs the boots by their laces from the nail on the post, barley and all, for every cart to pass, and
  the boy goes up barefoot. His father gives him his own boots at the gate. At the frost the boots are still on the
  nail (`e1.e.kel_boots`); in the barns Davin blows Kel's barley stalk and nobody tells him how (`e1.e.kel_stalk`).
- **Kept.** `f.e1_kel`, decider GEORGE, `e1.kel_kept`, `e1.kel_walked`, `said.e1_abel_sweepings`, `said.e1_josh_tip`.

### 8. Bet's rent is a promise said out loud

- **Was.** Abel writes the rent and gets Mott to witness it; the paper lies under the ham at the wedding.
- **Now.** Bet asks Abel to say it at the wedding in front of her father, Edie and every hand that's left. On
  `e1.rent_fixed` he stands on the cart step in the barns and says it, and spits and shakes on it with Old Carrow; on
  `e1.rent_open` she has his word in the parlour, where nobody heard it.
- **Kept.** `e1.rent_fixed`, `e1.rent_open` (read only in era 1).

### 9. Josh hits Will with the post lantern

- **Was.** The corner of the post book.
- **Now.** The corner of the lantern's iron cage, with blood and a hair on it after; the self-lie e1.sl.lantern names
  the cage. This puts Josh's era 1 boss, The Lantern, in his hand at the moment it is armed.

### 10. Ilo's spoon, the table and the lock

The end barn door comes off its pins on the Monday with Walter's chalk goat still on it; Ilo knocks the lock out with
the crowbar first, and Bet scrubs the goat off the door before it is a table. The table keeps a hole where the lock
was. The lock hangs on a nail by the empty hinge pins in era 2 (`q.barn_lock`); the ghost of the goat is under the
table (`q.copy_slips` state 2).
