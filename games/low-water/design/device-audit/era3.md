## 3. Plot changes

1. **The allocation is a man with the keys.** Was: slips, stamps and a census register at the counter, the far-bank
   flat as a pink slip with a name ruled through. Now: Walter Mott on a stool in front of a board of keys, iron rings
   for the merchants' houses, brass tags for the far bank, sorting families by their wrists, hands and boots. Krall
   slides a bright key to Pell across the green cloth. The Sallises had been walked into the flat and left their
   bundles on its floor, so the theft has a body: their bundles come back over the bridge on the board's cart and are
   tipped on the Mott house step. Why: the coordinator's model ("a man at the granary door deciding who goes in"), and
   a key is a thing the hand closes on or puts down. Rejected: Walter at the actual far-bank stair with the keys. The
   only far-bank place is Aaron's kitchen in year 46 and new places need plates; the office's counter, board and steps
   carry the same act, and the cart at its steps is the threshold the bundles cross. Rejected: Krall himself holding
   the keys. Walter's flaw (he does the job and never asks why) needs his hands on them.
2. **The flat that stays with the Sallises has wet plaster.** Was: on the refuse route the Sallises got the flat, yet
   every later scene put them in the Mott house. Now: the plaster is wet till the new year, so they stay in the Mott
   house wearing the key; Arn dies in the fire with it on its bootlace. Why: the old text contradicted itself, and
   the key worn at the table is a stronger consequence than a missing family.
3. **Anni's choice is made at the rail and the cart.** Was: a form, a Tarn man swearing to a column, Anni signing
   under him, Walter ruling Ilo out as grandfather. Now: a Tarn man stands beside her at the rail and says she is
   his, in front of the queue. Walter lays a key on the brass. The thread comes off. Ilo has carried her bundle down
   and he lifts it onto the far-bank cart himself and steps back with nothing to carry home. On refusal the cart goes
   at five without it and she carries her bundle home herself. Kept: Anni's flaw (pride that will not ask), Ned's
   (he claims her only when his son fetches him), Ilo's (he took the bargain and loves her anyway, and so he carries
   the bundle). Tested against two alternatives. A thread cut at the door alone loses Ilo; the bundle loses nothing
   and gives Ilo the scene's heaviest act. Ned writing nothing but walking down and saying it keeps his shame public,
   which the signature hid.
4. **Per A.F. is said, not read.** Was: a pencil note in a margin read aloud by accident. Now: Walter remembers being
   eight at his father's elbow when Abel came down to the counter and said, Leave it, Edwin. Both classes hear it.
   Why: a clerk's memory is the same complicity in a body. It also seeds the next change.
5. **Walter's guilt over Tomas is a memory said on the stairs.** Was: a caller book with a blue tick sent up in a
   Thursday bundle. Now: his father taught him to remember every caller to an official and say who when asked. Holt
   asked on the Thursday stairs and he said. His pride is his memory: "I could tell you every caller that winter. I
   still can." Why: the guilt is in the man, and the confession now has no object to hide behind.
6. **The well rumour has a voice and a hand.** Was: an unsigned handbill whose barless seven matched Walter's slips
   and a blotter that carried its words backwards. Now: a quiet counter voice behind the player in the queue at the low
   pump, and a clean hand pointing up Mill Street with its forefinger black with ink to the second knuckle. The queue
   takes the words up like a cough round a church. The same voice speaks once in the crowd at the fire. The props
   carry the hand: black prints on the rim of a rat bucket left at the Sholls' door, and Walter's ink rag with a
   bucket smell on its edge. The finger is established at the counter for both classes. Why: blame must come from
   somebody's mouth and somebody's hand. It stays ambiguous: the player never sees the face.
7. **The fence notice is a nailed gate and a dead rat.** Was: CLOSED FOR HEALTH on a notice, and a slur chalked under
   it. Now: Gedge nails boards across the yard gate and says it aloud; a rat hung on the wire at face height. Why: the
   sound of the nails and the smell do the contempt the chalked word did. The front door of the Well House stays a
   door, so Gedge still needs the cart shaft through its handles on the night of the fire.
8. **The fever's names are said at the bed and the thread is cut.** Was: Clara writes a Tarn name in the ward book
   that Hollis reads. Now: Hollis walks the beds every night with a lamp, asking each its name and parish and holding
   the lamp to its wrists. Clara teaches Davin the name for Mim to say, and answers for Bet; she cuts the thread off
   with the scissors round her neck. The false record becomes a lie told aloud and a mark removed from a body. Why:
   it costs the child and the mother something visible, and it ties the fever to the thread motif.
9. **The schoolbook is kept.** The Plain Rebuilt stays in the schoolroom: Mim reads from it aloud and Miss Tamm goes
   over the river's name in red pencil after the blood drips on it. Why: nothing in the plot depends on reading it.
   The scene runs on the ruler, the split knuckle and the pencil pressed through to the next page. The design names
   the schoolbook as where denial begins (stage 6), and the read-aloud rule allows a character reading in a scene.
10. **Over allocation is counted with a finger.** Was: a notice on the door that everybody had read and nobody read
   aloud. Now: Fenn comes through the house at night with a lamp and counts heads with his finger, sleepers and
   babies, then says it at the front door. Pell's choice becomes the curtain: pull it across and Fenn's finger counts
   two Haldane rooms; leave it open and it counts one. Will's choice is unchanged (timing against the chalk). Anni's
   naming becomes a key moved from the back room hook to the hill camp nail, and on the Militia line Will carries the
   Sholls' bed of sacks out to the cart himself (a load). Hanne's "a woman, they said, at the counter" still holds,
   because Anni said it at the rail and nobody saw her.
11. **Dorrah's book is a knot cord.** Was: the household book of births and burials, read out at prayer and saved by
   Ilo. Now: the knot cord from the Well House wall, a blue knot for every Hesh child born in the town in forty
   years and a black knot tied over the blue at each burial. Dorrah runs it through her fingers and says the year's
   dead. Ilo throws it out burning, or carries it out and leaves his palms on it. Why: the thing Ilo dies for should
   be a thing the hands know, and the blue thread is the Hesh mark in every era. Rejected: keeping the book and only
   cutting the reading. It would still be a record the plot turns on. This changes what era 5 inherits (section 4).
12. **Tomas goes over the ridge in the player's man's coat.** Was: a pass with the board's stamp. Now: the coat off
   the player's man's back, which the post waves through at night. The echo is the coat missing from the hall stand.
13. **Aaron's coat is the last one on the hook.** Was: sign there. Now: Will holds out a dead corporal's boiled coat or
   doesn't; the gatekeeper's and the witness's Halden is said, not spelled.
14. **Ned's praise is the scale pan.** Was: framed returns Ned signed. Now: the pan off the exchange scale hung beside
   the Directorate's crest. Why: it follows era 2's change of instrument. On the true route the praise is sincere and
   still ironic.
15. **Flags kept, meanings kept.** No flag id changed. e3.list_signed now means Anni took the far-bank key on a Tarn
   man's word at the rail; e3.list_refused, the cart went without her bundle; e3.list_offered, the offer made at the
   rail and the bundle packed; e3.book_thrown, the cord thrown from the window; e3.margin_told, Walter's confession
   of what he said to Holt. Every other flag keeps its old meaning.
