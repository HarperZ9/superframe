# Act 3, The Gale: the plan

Draft 2, 2026-10-17, revised on the story editor's structural read (`design/EDITOR-NOTES.md`, Part 5,
A1 to A15; draft 1 is kept at `design/.ACT3-PLAN.draft1.md`). Private. Planned before any content,
to the standard of `design/REWORK-PLAN.md` and with the lessons of the editor's three reads of Act 1.

## 1. The question, the spine, the turns

**What will the town live with?** Night 1 named a cause. Night 2 made someone pay for it. Night 3 is
a **second loss on the same policy**: the Shelf's own replies have said so since the first dawn
("FURTHER LOSS ANTICIPATED", "GALE ANTICIPATED. CLAIM 4471/G REMAINS OPEN"). In a south-west gale,
with three lamps lit on the town's oil, men the claim has taught that the Board pays "salvage for
the insurer" go up the mole to make some. The claim made the crime. At dawn Lamb writes a final
apportionment in two lines, the cause of the dark of Low Night and the cause of the loss in the gale,
in the Brine, with the surge at the step and the town in the room voting on what it will live with.

**The personal line resolves at turn one, not at dawn.** Lamb has been dry for eleven years as a
matter of professional pride. On the Ropewalk Lamb learns that those eleven years were spent working
for a company that failed the winter Lamb joined it, kept alive by a clerk and, for the last six
years, by the Alderman's money: Lamb's own wages among it. Dryness was distance, and the distance
was never real. What Lamb does with that is the act. The last choice is whether to go back to being
dry. The coat carries three tide marks at the hem, one per night.

## 4. The Shelf: who types it, why, and who paid Lamb

**The company.** The Mutual Indemnity of the Lower Shelf failed in Hesk eleven years ago, the winter
Mother's arc was first lit. Its Hesk office became one room and one clerk, Abel Strode, who kept the
regional night assessor's post because the claims kept coming down the old agency lines, and hired
Lamb that winter to answer them. Lamb's eleven years of claims (Pell, Rendle, Mudlow, Brack) were
real losses assessed for a company whose payments were made by its surviving local agencies out of
their floats, at "the usual rate".

**The Gullet agency.** The far end of the pneumatic tube is the back room of the Gannet house, the
Shelf's Gullet agency. Will Gannet was its agent. He died of a winter fever eleven years ago, in the
week the Shelf's last letter came, and signed the town's renewals from his bed (a different night and
a different grief from Jory Brine's; nothing in Act 1 changes). Hester has typed the Shelf's Gullet
replies since and paid its small claims from the agency's float.

**The Alderman's money.** Six years ago the float ran low, the year the keeper's wages stopped. The
Alderman began sending the Hesk crossword each week, filled in, with a banker's draft folded inside,
because an uninsured Trust could not have borrowed for the sodium, the automation or the white lamps.
Hester forwards a share to Hesk for the night assessor's wages. **Lamb has been paid, in part, by
Ottilie Crake for six years.** The Trust verdict and the Shelf verdict both reach Lamb's own pocket.

**The bent e.** Seven months ago, the week the weigh-house was shut for Trust electricians and Mother
went onto the quay board, the Alderman's drafts doubled (the rewiring needed the Trust insured on the
altered installation), and with them came the Trust's old office typewriter, retired when the
automation office bought new: the machine the engineer's specification and the struck clause were
typed on. Its e was already bent. From that week the Shelf's replies are typed on the Trust's machine.
This is the payoff of Act 1's midpoint (Pip's tin): an Eye route in Act 3 matches the bent e of the
Shelf's strips to the bent e of the specification (`clause.struck`) or the works order.

**Pip knows.** She has known since she was fifteen; her shilling a week is her grandmother's, and her
Act 1 line ("Mostly I don't read it") and her Act 2 line ("There isn't a Gullet clerk. There's me")
are a girl keeping her grandmother's secret. In Act 3 she tells it, or Lamb lets her not.

**The letter that asked for the claim kept open.** After the Low Night verdict, the Alderman's letter
asked Hester to keep 4471/G open: a gale was forecast, the Trust expected further loss, and one open
claim is cheaper than two. Hester typed FURTHER LOSS ANTICIPATED. Pip read it aloud on the quay.
Sallis, who on Night 2 had stripped the barges as "salvage for the insurer", heard what the Board
would pay for.

**Her reason.** Not a scheme: a woman keeping her husband's promise to a town on behalf of a company
that does not exist, with the help of a woman who needs the company to exist. The verdict Lamb sends
at dawn goes to her, and she will type the reply.

**Equivalent facts for the Shelf verdict** (any two): `typist.heard` (Act 2),
`ropewalk.notice_matched` (Act 2), `hester.seen_typing`, `hester.told`, `crake.drafts`, `pip.told`,
`e.matched` (the bent e matched to the Trust's machine).

## 7. Endings: resolving the spine

### 7.1 The final apportionment: two losses, two lines (dawn3, the Brine, 06:20)

**Line one, the dark of Low Night (Night 1).** The six Act 1 shapes with their equivalents, now
also able to name **the Shelf itself** (section 4's equivalents, any two): a claim made on an insurer
that does not exist; the policy is void because the insurer is. "Malice by wreckers" for Night 1
remains the Board's lie, whatever Lamb saw on Night 3.

**Line two, the loss in the gale (Night 3).** What was lost depends on the night: Mother put out, a
gale lamp put out, the collier on the Spit, or nothing. Its causes: **wreckers** (`wreckers.real`,
with names: true this time, and the game says so), **the Trust** (the letter that asked for the claim
kept open, `crake.drafts` and the anticipated loss), **the Shelf itself**, **the sea** (if nothing was
done by anyone and the gale did it), or **no loss** (if the town held every light).

The final apportionment can name the same party on both lines or different ones. The town's last
vote (B7) decides what the town accepts of it.

## 9. The wreckers (switchable)

`act2_strippers` decides who they are, as it decided who stripped the barges:
- `town`: **Sallis** and the Board's wardens (Pruett, Boddy, Hask, the Penroses): men who learned on
  Night 2 that the Board pays "salvage for the insurer", and heard on Night 3's morning that further
  loss was anticipated. Not Lugg (A5).
- `hesk`: **Captain Pardew's crew**, with **Sallis as their local hand**, who opened the door and told
  them what the claim would pay. The town still has something to live with.

## 11. Size and order
