# World layers: the places, what each era leaves, and the clues in them

World-building lead, 2026-10-23. Everything here is **PROPOSAL** under the author's blanket approval
("All actions and blockers approved", 2026-10-22), reversible, and every prop text it names is DRAFT until the
author approves the lines (ledger row S14).

The author, 2026-10-23, verbatim: "The environments are decent, but more variety, and much more static items,
symbolism, assets, and worldbuilding materials that tell the story of the world. Deep, and layered lore and
storytelling". And the same day, relayed verbatim: "Fantastic, and context clues as well. Worldbuilding through
materials, media, and assets providing context clues, and storytelling."

The method references are Disco Elysium, Zero Parades and No Rest for the Wicked, for how a room carries history
in its objects and how some of it is reachable only on some playthroughs. Method only: nothing is taken from them
(the clean-room rule).

## 1. How a place is read

Every dressed place answers four questions in objects, and never in a caption.
1. **Before.** What happened here before the player arrives.
2. **Left behind.** What this era leaves in the room: wear, a repair, a thing put away, a thing kept out.
3. **Worn over.** What the next era puts over it: new paint over old, a notice pasted inside the pale square of
   the last one, a name corrected or left wrong.
4. **Who.** Who lived here, who left, who died, and who lied about it.

Three kinds of evidence carry the answers.
- **Materials.** Soot, a tide line, newer plaster or brick where a wall was made good, paint scrubbed off,
  a lighter patch where a picture hung, pencil marks up a door frame.
- **Media.** Notices, handbills, newspapers, slips, ledgers, rent and ration books, photographs, letters, a card
  from the Electricity Board. In era 5, phones and clips (section 9).
- **Assets.** The objects people owned and used, placed so that an attentive player can work out what happened.

**The rule for prop text.** Describe, never explain. The text says what is there, what is wrong with it, and
whose it is, in the plain words a person standing there would use. It never says what it means, never names the
link to another room, and never says the word "father" with Ned's name (the lore spine, question 9). A player who
puts two things together does it in their own head.

## 3. The places, era by era

Each entry gives what happened before, what the era leaves, what the next era wears over it, and the clues the
place holds. "Dressed" places carry new authored props in render/props (section 7); the others carry the
moving objects by narration until their plates are next rebuilt.

### Era 1, The Exchange (year 0)

**q1_census, the registrar's front counter. Dressed.** Before: the Crown Council's census, taken here every
harvest since the exchange opened, by Edwin Mott and before him his own teacher. Left: the residency rule on the
notice board in Edwin's round hand, the copy slips with their barless sevens, a child's thread lost in the queue
and swept to the skirting. Worn over: in era 2 the same rule hangs at the road post under four newer notices,
only its last line showing. Clues: the rule (media; it tells the player who is about to be sent up the hill);
the slips' sevens (the first sight of the Mott hand); the thread at the skirting (somebody Hesh stood in this
queue and lost it).

**q1_board, the registrar's upstairs room. Dressed.** Before: the board has met here for thirty years over the
same decanters. Left: Professor Calley's pamphlet on the table, with a woodcut goat on the cover that faces
forward; the board's own photograph being hung, Abel in the good hat; the minute book. Worn over: in era 2 the
Directorate reprints the pamphlet in the district office, the woodcut cropped. Clues: the forward goat against
the carved goat on the table leg; the minute book's sevens.

**q1_loft, Abel's hayloft. Dressed.** Before: the Furrowe hay, and before that the year Bet worked at the farm.
Left: Ilo's carving by the lantern, one spoon finished and five roughed out, each handle a goat looking back; on
the tie beam, cut with a knife point and dark with handling, a girl's initials and a spring three years back, and
beside them a second set scraped out with the flat of a blade. Worn over: the spoons travel (section 5); the beam
stays where it is and nobody sees it again. Clues: the beam is the hidden fragment of the faces chain (section
6), reachable only on the Inspector line.

### Era 2, The Quota (year 12, and Anni's kitchen at year 38)

**q_cells, under the district office. Dressed.** Before: the Crown's lock-up, limewashed every spring. Left: a
tin cup turned over on the shelf above the end bench; low on the end cell's wall, at the height of a boy sitting
down, a goat scratched into the limewash with its head turned back. On the runs where Tomas was held the
scratches are white; on the others they are an old soft line under many coats. Worn over: the cells are not seen
again. Clues: the goat's freshness is the only object in the game that says a Hesh child sat on that bench this
week. It never says which child.

**q_returns, the returns room. Dressed.** Before: the Crown Council's district office. Left: over the door, a
square of cleaner paint where the Council's arms hung, and the Directorate's notice pasted inside it, too small
to cover the corners; the harvest-year census slips in a bundle under the ledger. Worn over: in era 3 the same
room is the allocation counter, and the Directorate's notice has gone too, leaving a smaller clean square inside
the larger one. Clues: two governments in one wall.

**q_furrowe_kitchen. Dressed.** Before: Abel's house, Abel's hat. Left: the Gazette on the table, PLAIN RETURNS
ABOVE PLAN, with Jonah Krall shaking hands outside the exchange, and Mary has wrapped the dripping in the back page;
a sampler over the range in Mary's stitching. Worn over: the hat moves to the hall (era 3) and a bag on the
cupboard (era 4). Clues: the headline against the bowls on every table that night (told against true).

**q_brack_kitchen, Mill Street. Dressed.** Before: Josh Brack's house; Will grew up being hit in it on Fridays.
Left: the wedding photograph over the range, Edie laughing at something outside the frame; Will's boots on the
fender, clean on day 41, and on the runs where the boy was held, yellow clay dried to the ankle by the week after;
damp in the plaster low on the river wall. Worn over: in era 3 the photograph's glass is cracked in a star and the
damp is a brown line at table height with new plaster under it. Clues: the clay (the corner, again, without a
word); the damp (the first sight of the tide line).

**q_anni_room, Anni's kitchen at year 38. Dressed.** Before: the Haldane barns, the door table, every move the
family made. Left: the rent book, ORME ESTATES, HALDANE YARD, its cover spelt HALDENE and corrected in pencil;
five of Ilo's spoons in a jar by the cooker, worn thin at the bowl, and no sixth. Clues: the misspelling repeats
the ration card's, and somebody corrects it again; the sixth spoon (section 5).

### Era 3, Allocation (year 20)

**q3_mott_house, the old Mott house. Dressed.** Before: Edwin Mott's family house; Walter grew up in it. Left: the
door frame under the stairs, pencilled up with heights, I. and TAM at the bottom this year, and higher and older
under the paint, W.M. at eight, nine and ten; a goat chalked small on the third stair, scrubbed until only its
legs show; the handbill on the stair post, WHO DRINKS FROM THE HILL, unsigned, its seven without a bar; Ilo's
spoons in a jar on the door table, which the children are told not to use; Anni's wedding photograph on the
shelf. Worn over: in era 4 the house is flats, then the bank's. Clues: who lived here first (the Mott heights
under the Haldane ones); who wrote the handbill (the seven, against the slips and the blotter); the faces chain.

**q3_office, the allocation counter. Dressed.** Before: the returns room (era 2). Left: the nested pale squares
over the door; the spike of slips by the window, MILL ST, UNFIT, WATER, a household's name on each, and one slip
taken off the spike and put back on the shelf, BRACK, W.; Walter's blotter, with backward capitals in it that a
player can read in the window glass. Clues: the unfit listing was made by name (against the tide line in the
Brack kitchen); the blotter (against the handbill). The blotter does not prove Walter wrote the handbill. He may
have copied it out for the counter's board. The game keeps that open.

**q3_squad, the militia squad room. Dressed.** Before: the Directorate's post. Left: a mug turned over on its hook
over the tea table, with a name painted on it that nobody uses; a spade by the stove with yellow clay in the
grain of the handle; Will's locker, the door an inch open. On the runs where the boy was held, a little plaited
ring of blue thread hangs on the locker hook, cut through, the size of a boy's wrist. Clues: Will told Ned on the
Crown's steps that he told Gedge to leave the thread on the boy (q_lore.lwd, q.coda.corner). The ring in his
locker contradicts him, and only a player who has heard the Inspector's evening and seen the Militia's locker
holds both.

**q3_furrowe_hall. Dressed.** Before: Abel's house, now Ned and Mary's. Left: Abel's hat on the hall stand where he
left it the week he died, the brass gone dull; a photograph of Ned at twenty-seven in a borrowed collar, a long
careful face; a pale oblong on the paper where a larger picture hung until this year. Clues: Ned's face, set
against Anni's wedding photograph in the Mott house. Nothing says why the oblong is pale.

**q3_brack_kitchen. Dressed.** Before: era 2's kitchen. Left: the tide line at table height and the plaster below
it a shade too white; the range rusted to the same line; the wedding photograph with its glass cracked in a star
from one corner. Worn over: in era 4 the photograph goes to Davey's flat in a new frame. Clues: the Bracks had
the same water as the Hesh houses on the spike, and stayed.

**q3_school. Dressed.** Before: the exchange's offices. Left: Mim Sallis's desk with KESH cut small into the lid
and inked over so often the ink stands up; the ruler on the teacher's table. Clues: the word that cost her hand.

**q3w_far_bank, Aaron's kitchen at year 46. Dressed.** Left: Aaron's long-service paper in a frame, HALDEN, A.,
the last letter gone and nobody writing it back; the stamped relief bowl on the high shelf (built). Clues: the
third misspelling of the name, the first nobody corrects.

### Era 4, The Closure (year 70, and Mira's flat at year 103)

**q4w_saro_flat, Mira's flat at year 103. Dressed.** Left: a biscuit tin of papers, and in it a card from the
Electricity Board for the Row, SUPPLY DISCONNECTED, ARREARS, dated two weeks before the fire, with NO LIGHTS
written on the back in a child's capitals. Clues: the official report says electrical fault. The card says the
house had no electricity.

**q4_joss_flat, Haldane Yard. Dressed.** Left: the spoon rack on the wall with six notches and nothing in them,
a cleaner shape round each notch where a spoon hung for fifty years; Joss's clocking card tucked in the frame of
the mill photograph, the last week stamped in red. On a later run, after one that found where Tomas lies, one spoon
is back, lying under the rack with its bowl stained yellow in the grain. The plate shows the empty rack on every run;
the returned spoon is in the text only. Clues: the spoons were sold (era 5's shop has them); the yellow spoon
is the across-runs fragment of section 6.

**q4_furrowe_house, Pell's kitchen. Dressed.** Left: Abel's hat in a bag on the high shelf, which Nan takes
down for dressing up; Pell's surveyor's book from year 20, open at the Mott house, one room under the stairs where
he wrote two, crossed it out and wrote one. Clues: fate f.e3_sholl turned on that figure.

**q4_brack_flat, over the Crown. Dressed.** Left: Will and Edie's wedding in a new frame, the old cracked glass
kept behind the photograph; a photograph of the Crown in the water, a rowing boat at its door. Clues: the flood
of year 18 exists, in the one family's house that was not moved for it.

**q4_police, the station desk. Dressed.** Left: a cardboard stencil cut as a goat, facing forward, white paint on
its edges, in a police bag labelled FOUND, THE ROW, NO OWNER; over the desk a pale oblong where a portrait hung
under the Directorate. Clues: the stencil against the Hesh goat; the report in the typewriter (built).

## 4. Context clues: the rules

Every clue has one rule: what it implies, what it agrees with or contradicts, and which playthroughs find it.
"Any" means any class on any route that passes the place.

| Clue | Implies | Corroborates or contradicts | Who can find it |
|---|---|---|---|
| The residency rule on the census board (era 1) | The hands at the counter are being sorted, not counted | Corroborates the frost list (e1.lore.frost_list) | Any |
| The copy slips' sevens (era 1) | Edwin's office hand | Matches the ledger (era 2), the handbill and the slips (era 3) | Any |
| The forward goat on Calley's pamphlet (era 1) | The town draws the goat facing forward | Contradicts the carved goat's turned head | Any |
| The initials on the loft beam (era 1) | Bet was in this loft before her marriage, with somebody whose initials were scraped off | Agrees with Ned's portrait and Anni's face (era 3); never confirmed | Inspector line only (hidden) |

| The pale square over the returns door (era 2) | The office changed masters and kept the room | Nested in era 3's patch | Any |
| PLAIN RETURNS ABOVE PLAN (era 2) | The official account of the winter | Contradicted by every table that night | Any |

**Told against true.** Six clues contradict an official account inside the world: the Gazette's headline, the
unfit listing, the Well House notice set against the handbill, Will's word against his locker, the fire report
against the Electricity Board's card, and the long-service paper against the family's own spelling. None of them
is resolved in text. Both halves stand in the world where people left them, and a player who finds both puts
them together in their own head. Nothing in the game points out the match.

## 7. The props in render/props

Reusable first (the "reusable tools, not game code" rule): the makers take positions, sizes, era styles and text as
data, and no maker knows a Low Water name.
- `render/props/letters.mjs`: a full 5 by 7 capital and figure set (the era 4 kit had 29 glyphs) laid on any plane
  as a surface kind, with hand roughness, for notices, handbills, headlines, labels and slips.
- `render/props/layers.mjs`: household objects with an era style (crockery, a sampler, a spoon jar and rack, a
  biscuit tin, a hat in a bag, a clocking card), trade tools (a surveyor's book, a spade, a stencil, a typewritten
  card, a minute book, slips on a spike), signs and notices with text, memorial objects (a framed photograph with
  a cracked or replaced glass, a long-service paper, a flood photograph), personal belongings (a locker, a desk lid
  with a cut word, a plaited thread), and material evidence (a tide line, a pale patch, a scrubbed chalk mark,
  pencil heights up a door frame, a scratched mark in limewash).
- `render/props/dress.mjs`: lays a list of placed items over a built world without touching the world's builder,
  and adds the examine marks to the place's spots. A place file passes its list; nothing else changes.
- The lists themselves are game content and live in `render/places/layers/`.

The variety each era gains is measured by `render/tools/variety.mjs` (distinct prop kinds per place and per era),
reported in PROGRESS with the before and after.

## Does not prove

That a player notices any of it. propcheck proves a scripted attentive player looked at each prop; lorecheck
proves each route can be completed by the runs its rule allows. Neither shows that the clues are found, read, or
put together by a person, or that the texts pass the read-aloud test. Playtests are the check this page cannot be.
