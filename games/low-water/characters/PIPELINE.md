# Characters: pipeline decision

2026-10-16. Character art and animation lead. Companion to AUDIT.md (what exists) and
PROGRESS.md (what was done, in order).

## 1. Decision

**Real-time, low-poly 3D skinned characters, shaded in the plates' language, drawn through the
same per-pixel style passes as the places.** One skeleton for the whole cast, one clip library,
every timing read from data. A figure is rendered each frame to a G-buffer (material id, normal,
world position, a bind-space position for brushwork, depth), then shaded: brushwork anchored to
the body, light quantised into painted value steps, shadows that lean violet, a self-shadow from
the dominant lamp, a rim from the lamps behind, ink heavy on the silhouette and light on folds, and
on the quay the sodium value ramp. The same G-buffer can go through the engine's painter, its
dither lighting (blackouts) and its palette lighting (the Gut), so the figures can follow every
light regime of the look bible.

Two uses of the same source back it up:

- **Painted close-ups.** For dialogue portraits and slow shots, the engine's paint-over painter
  (the pass that painted the plates) runs over the figure's G-buffer. It boils in motion (section
  3), so it is for stills and slow moves, not play.
- **Baked sprites for crowds and as a no-GPU fallback.** The rig renders to sprite sheets at fixed
  facings for background people (the Guild in the weigh-house, the floor children), where a few
  hundred frames a person is affordable and nobody fights.

**Not chosen:** hand-painted 2D with a cut-out rig, and 3D rendered to painted sprites as the main
path. Reasons in section 2.

## 2. The options, judged

Scores are my judgment from the prototypes in section 3; the evidence column says what each rests on.

| Criterion | (a) Real-time 3D skinned, painterly shading | (b) Hand-painted 2D, cut-out rig | (c) 3D rendered to painted sprites | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Readability at quay distance | Good at a closer camera; value design and rim carry it | Good if painted for that size | Same as (a): same model, same shading | stills/01; at today's quay camera nobody reads (48 px true size), whatever the pipeline |
| Detail up close | Fair to good: sculpted heads, self-shadow, ink; reads as stylised CG, not paint | Best possible, with a painter on staff | Better than (a) for stills (the painter runs offline) | stills/03 live against painted |
| Animation consistency | Best: one skeleton, one clip set, retargeted by proportions | Weak: each facing of each character is separate art | Good, but facing is quantised (8 or 16 steps) and turns pop | sheets in out/, reel |
| Combat responsiveness | Best: continuous facing, any blend, timing changes take effect at once | Weak: 8 facings at most, every new move is new art | Fair: facings quantised; retiming by frame holds only; a new key means a re-bake | runtime/actor.mjs, proto/actor_test.mjs |
| Cost for a cast of dozens | Low: a proportions record, a costume script, a palette, a style layer | Very high: no painter on this project; art is generated in code | Medium: same authoring as (a) plus bake time and storage per character | section 4 |
| Fit with the look bible | Good: G-buffer feeds the engine's painter, dither and palette passes; value designs hold under sodium | Good in principle | Good, and the painter can run per frame offline | stills/05 |
| Runtime cost | Highest: about 6 ms a figure at gameplay scale, 12 ms at room scale on the CPU in Node; trivial on a GPU | Lowest | Lowest | proto/bench.mjs |
| Engine work needed | Skinning and a clip sampler (CPU reference now, GPU later) | Cut-out rig player | Sprite atlas player (exists: Motion sprites) | AUDIT.md section 2 |

Outside support, from the clean-room study (cleanroom/spec/CHARACTER-PRESENTATION.md, reported
facts only): one reference game is reported to use 3D-modelled, rigged characters over painted
environments, and another is reported as a painterly 3D action game. That is the shape of (a). It
does not prove (a) is right here; the prototypes below are what the decision rests on.

Why not (b): the project has no human painter, and every frame of art here is generated by code.
A cut-out rig still needs each facing painted separately, which is where consistency breaks for a
cast of 38 and a combat set of 21 clips.

Why not (c) as the main path: at gameplay scale it looks the same as (a) (stills/05: the painter
adds nothing visible at 110 px), it quantises facing, which combat in the reference style cannot
afford, it cannot follow a camera change or a close-up without a new bake, and it costs storage.
My estimate (moderate confidence, not measured): 774 frames a character in the current clip set,
times 8 facings, is 6,192 sprites per character per scale; at about 100 x 140 px with colour and
normal that is roughly 0.5 GB raw, perhaps 50 to 100 MB compressed, per character per scale.

## 3. Prototypes

Built cheaply on the same rig so only the shading path differs (proto/compare.mjs).

- **A, real-time.** Skinned mesh, rasterised to a G-buffer, shaded with object-anchored brushwork,
  banded light and ink, relit by the place's light rig (render/rigs.mjs: the three quay lamps with
  the sodium ramp; paraffin lamps in the Brine and the weigh-house).
- **C, painted bake.** The same G-buffer, lit by a neutral rig, painted by
  `web/shaders/paintover/painter.mjs` from the Low Water worktree, inked, then relit at run time as
  painted × L(N) / L_neutral(N).

Measured on this machine, CPU, Idle priority:

| Measure | A | C | How |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cost per figure at 110 px (2.5x quay camera) | about 6 ms (skin 1, raster 3, shade 2) | about 1.5 s offline per frame | proto/bench.mjs; proto/compare.mjs timings |
| Cost per figure at 180 px (rooms) | about 12 ms | about 2 to 10 s offline | same |
| Frame-to-frame change, neighbouring idle frames (lower is steadier) | mean 0.0018; 0.7 % of pixels change by more than 5 % | mean 0.0131; 2.6 % | proto/boil.mjs, one pose pair; does not prove the ratio holds for every clip |
| Look at gameplay scale | indistinguishable from C | indistinguishable from A | stills/05-pipeline-a-vs-c.png |
| Look up close | stylised CG with brushwork | softer, more painted | stills/03-closeups.png |

The runtime figure for A is from Node with no GPU. In the browser prototype (Canvas 2D) it is
workable for two to four figures. A game with a crowd needs the GPU path (section 5).

## 4. What a character costs to make

Per character: a proportions record (height, leg, head, neck, shoulders, hips, arm, stoop), a
costume script built from shared parts (torso, two-panel coat skirt, sleeves, legs and boots, a
sculpted head with feature controls, hats, props on the grip axis), a palette over the shared
materials, an optional style layer over the shared clips, and optional timing overrides. Fennick
and Marrow are 55 and 50 lines each. Each took about an hour of build-and-look iteration in this
session (moderate confidence; it will drop as the parts library grows).

## 5. Engine work

**raw-native has no skinning, no clip sampler, no glTF skins** (AUDIT.md section 2). The pipeline
needs, in order:

1. **CPU reference (now).** Skeleton forward kinematics, linear-blend skinning, phase-timed clip
   sampling with a monotone cubic, the phase warp and two-bone IK, as a pure module with tests:
   HarperZ9/raw-native#171 (`web/anim/`, 11 node tests). The game-side rig in `characters/rig/` and
   `characters/anim/` keeps the Low Water parts (semantic channels, costumes, the clip library);
   once #171 is on the branch the game serves, the game-side skinning and sampling can call it.
2. **GPU path (after the GPU is free).** WGSL skinning in the web host and a character G-buffer
   pass, so the per-pixel style passes run on it. The CPU reference is the test oracle.
3. **glTF skins and animations (optional).** Only if outside tools are ever used; the cast is
   authored in code today and does not need it.

None of this touches `src/renderer/gpu/shaders/style*`.

## 6. Camera and scale (for the director and the systems engineer)

At today's quay camera a person is about 48 px tall at true scale in a 720-line frame (under 7 % of
the frame height). No pipeline makes that legible for a fight.

The clean-room spec v2 (cleanroom/spec/CHARACTER-PRESENTATION.md section 7, 2026-10-17) gives a
target from the references: about 130 px at 1080p (12 % of the frame) at one reference's default
zoom, and proposes 130 to 160 px (12 to 15 %) for Low Water's quay. In the 720-line plates that is
87 to 107 px. My recommendation, matched to it:

- A gameplay camera 2 to 2.5 times closer on the quay (about 96 to 110 px, 13 to 15 % of the frame),
  following the player, over plates baked at a higher resolution or in tiles. The stills show 2.5x.
- An orthographic camera with its position snapped to whole plate pixels when it scrolls, so the
  painted plate does not shimmer (spec v2, section 7.1). The plates use narrow perspective cameras
  today (fovY 0.16 to 0.37), close to orthographic; the figures are already drawn orthographic.
- Rooms with a closer zoom limit than exteriors, 1.5 to 2 times today's.

Animation rate: the spec reports 60 Hz authoring and per-segment retiming in one reference. The clips
here are continuous curves laid out by phase, so they play at 60 Hz and retime per phase without
re-keying; `timing.json` counts at 30 because combat's export does, and `drive()` takes seconds.
The calm turns take the spec's numbers (section 7.2, halved to 30 fps): `turn` 39 frames, free from
12; `turn_run` 28 frames, free from 8, carrying the body 0.25 m.

## 7. Known limits of what is built

- Up close the figures still lean toward stylised CG. Banded light with brush-edged planes and the
  painter for portraits (fed the banded light) move them toward the plates; a hand sculpting pass on
  heads would help most.
- Coats swing on spring joints driven by the body's motion; there is no cloth simulation or collision.
- Feet hold because loop speed is measured from the planted foot; there is no IK foot lock, so
  steps and slopes can still show a slip.
- Two hand shapes (open and fist), no fingers that move.
- No layered blending (upper body attacking while legs walk); clips cross-fade whole-body.
- The sodium look is a value ramp, not the engine's spectral pass.
