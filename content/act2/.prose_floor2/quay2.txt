Ten past seven. In the weigh-house somebody's hand puts the quay switch up, and the click carries the length of the quay in the frost.

The three sodium lamps wake red, then rose, then orange, and Gullet is one colour again. Under a sky with every star in it, every face on the quay can be seen.

The wall above the Brine has nothing on it. The assessor came down the stairs with a pocketful of pins.

On the wall above the Brine, under one pin, the claim: half past six. The assessor knows the line by heart now, and the town is about to find out what it bought.

On the wall above the Brine, under one pin, Keel's signature, dry. From the quay the harbour office window is lit, and somebody inside is not sitting down.

On the wall above the Brine, under one pin: down at 21:38, up at 21:52. The click of the switch tonight is a minute early, and the assessor counts it anyway.

On the wall above the Brine the green prism hangs on its nail and catches the first orange of the quay through the window, and is the same colour as everything else.

On the wall above the Brine, in pencil: 22:20, a light, nothing of Merrow's burning. Out on the bay, past the Spit, nothing is burning now either.

On the wall above the Brine, a drawing of a man standing in a puddle. At the top of the quay steps, in the first orange, a man stands in a puddle.

Nothing on the quay says what the Shelf decided. Everyone on it knows.

At the mole steps the Low Company's boats lie chained together under a Board padlock the size of a ham. A notice on the harbour office door: SALVAGE PROHIBITED, BY ORDER, and under it, in another hand, FOR THE SHELF.

On the weigh-house door a printed notice: THE ANNUAL VOTE IS PROHIBITED. Nobody has torn it down. Nobody in an apron is on the quay to tear it.

The harbour office has a Municipal padlock on it, and a paper pinned beside it: UNDER REVIEW. The Lamplighters stand where they always stand, and write more slowly.

Out on the mole a Trust boy with a lantern is walking to Mother for the first time, and keeps stopping to look back at the Brine, where the curtain on the porthole that faces the steps does not move.

Everything is where it was. People are very polite to the bowler, and look past it at the sea, which is out again, and has not apologised.

Two notices on the harbour office door, one Board, one Municipal, pinned over each other so that neither can be read whole. A third, in chalk, below them, says the Shelf cannot make up its mind.

Lamp Quay under a clear sky. The steps go down to the floor, which the sea is giving back slowly. The Brine's portholes, the weigh-house's balance, the harbour office's window, the mole and Mother at the end of it.

Far out past the Spit, two lanterns move on the black, low, where the stranded barges lie. Somebody on the quay says the word salvage, and somebody else says it louder.

At the bottom step the Board's boat, and two tide-wardens in canvas coats sitting in it doing nothing. Beyond it a boat nobody knows, long and dark, with six men in it from the evening coach, and crowbars wrapped in sacking.

Requisitions. Right. The Board'll hear of it.

He unlashes the small boat astern and holds it to the step while the assessor gets in, with the expression of a man memorising a face for later.

One warden is sitting on the bottom step in a foot of harbour with his boathook floating beside him. The other holds his wrist and does not come on. Up on the quay four poles have stopped moving and four pencils have started.

The assessor is off the steps and back along the mole with the case held to the lee side and the boathook's scrape on the stone behind. The Board's boat pushes off without its passenger.

A shove, and the harbour, which at this hour of a frozen night is not water so much as an opinion about water.

Twenty-five minutes later the assessor is on the Brine's step under a borrowed blanket. The Board's boat is a lantern going out toward the Spit.

Halfway across, a boot finds the assessor under the tarpaulin. Nobody shouts. Somebody takes the bowler, looks at it, and puts it back on the assessor's head, carefully, as if it were evidence.

The assessor arrives at the Spit sitting on the sacks with a man on either side.

Nan Cockle is sitting on her upturned boat with a lantern, eighty-one and in no hurry. The assessor tells her where the copper is, or shows her the sack.

Copper. The Low Company's, from the Old Light. You know where it lies and you'd let it go back to us for a seat in a boat.

Nan Cockle looks at the oilskin and the mud to the knee and does not ask what the assessor is.

Floor folk. Take Lugg's punt, it's sound. Bring it back with the same number of holes.

Twenty minutes by her lantern, cork in the hole and tin over the cork and nails through the tin, with fingers that stop answering halfway and answer again. They roll her over. She sits on the water and does not take any of it in.

There. She's been dry since my sixtieth. Mind you bring her back.

The patch holds for the length of the mole and then begins, politely, to weep. The assessor rows with one hand and bails with the bowler, and reaches the Spit wet to the waist with the boat half full.

She'll be on the bottom by morning. You owe me a boat, Shelf.

A flat black punt tied short at the end of the steps, with no oars in it and a name painted on the stern: COFFIN. Nobody takes her out. She cannot be rowed against any tide, and comes back only on the flood.

Ebb at its hardest from eleven to twenty past, setting straight for the Spit's elbow. After that it slackens and swings east.

Cast her off at the exact minute, and let the ebb do the rowing.

Leave her tied.

Fourteen minutes past eleven by the assessor's watch. The painter comes off, and the Coffin goes, turning once, then straight, with no sound but the water under her. The lanterns on the Spit grow without anyone having to row toward them.

Four minutes late. The ebb has begun to swing, and takes the Coffin past the Spit's elbow and sets her on a bank of mud two hundred yards short. The assessor goes the rest of the way on foot, at the bottom of the bay, in the dark, to the knee.

Out from the mole steps, and the quay's orange falls behind until it is a stain on the black. Ahead, the Spit, and on it two barges lying on their sides under the stars, and lanterns moving on them.
