In plain words

four ideas

A measuring instrument starts as a law of nature that ties something you want to know to something you can see or hear. The room meter uses the fact that a room rings at certain notes, and a bigger room has more of them packed below any pitch. The thermometer uses the hiss every resistor makes, which grows with its temperature. The polariscope uses the way clear plastic bends polarised light when it is squeezed.

Between the law and a trustworthy number sit ten steps: a simulation of the whole instrument, the electronics, the board, the code, calibration, a test where a computer feeds known signals through the real parts, tests out in the world, a receipt for every reading, and finally a way to build many of them that all pass the same tests. Each step has a pass mark that can fail.

PLAIN_RESULT

Nothing here was bought or built. The thermometer and the polariscope are on paper. The room meter needs no new hardware, so its simulation, its round-trip test and its receipts are real code that ran. Every receipt says UNCAL until a real calibration exists.
