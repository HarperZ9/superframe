    Light, noise, time, space

    The four axes

    In plain words

    A lens, a mirror, a film, a retina, a neuron and a sequence aligner look unrelated. Each one takes something in and hands something on, and each keeps part of what came in. Here they share one interface, so the same ruler reads all of them.

    A mirror returns nearly all of it. A lens spreads it. A sensor turns it into counts. A spike train keeps a rough rate. Move a step and the sum changes, or the chain refuses, because each step can only read what the last one made.

    Light is how many photons arrive. Noise is what they arrive through. Time is how long you wait for them. Space is how finely you cut them up. Smaller cells share the same photons among more places, and they gain until read noise wins.

    The working

    Every kernel declares its input and output representation with units, a grade and its axes, and the field type measures the grade on the data. A kernel with unmeasured bits, a failed declared structure, a wrong unit or a false lossless claim is refused.

    Budget per step: I = (space modes)(time modes) log2(1 + SNR), with modes counted above the noise floor on the data. Kept is measured independently, from the coherence with the source: sum over modes of -0.5 log2(1 - gamma^2). This formula is not a bound. Across 218 swept steps it fails on 17: spikes, coarse ADCs and dim catches, where one SNR averaged over all modes reads near zero while low modes still carry bits. Counting every mode against the floor fixes those, and fails on others at bright shot noise, where the noise follows the signal: 24 with the measured lens table bound, 33 in a build without it.
